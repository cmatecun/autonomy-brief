**Week of October 20–October 26, 2026 | ~7 min read**

## Executive Summary
- Waymo plans to quintuple its fleet to 20,000 vehicles by end of 2027, targeting 8,000 by year-end 2026 (*Semafor*)
- Tesla disclosed 3.3M unsupervised miles driven across six cities, with daily mileage accelerating 117% in four weeks (*Tesla Robotaxi Safety Report*)
- Bolt selected NVIDIA's Alpamayo Stack to power its European robotaxi program with Lucid vehicles, starting in Madrid in 2027 (*NVIDIA, Bolt*)

- Rivian acquired world model startup Third Dimension to accelerate self-driving development for Uber robotaxis (*TechCrunch*)

## Key Developments

### Market Moves

Waymo announced plans to expand to Madrid, Spain in 2027, its third European city after London and Munich. The company will begin testing with Jaguar I-PACEs across multiple municipalities including Madrid City, Alcobendas, and Getafe (*Waymo*). Waymo also launched airport service at Harry Reid International Airport in Las Vegas just five weeks after opening the market to the public, marking its sixth major airport deployment (*Waymo*).

Bolt finalized its autonomous vehicle technology stack for Europe, selecting NVIDIA's Alpamayo Stack to power its Lucid-based robotaxis. The program begins in Madrid in 2027, with Germany and France named as priority markets for future deployments (*NVIDIA, Bolt*).

Tesla released its first Robotaxi Safety Report, disclosing 3.3M unsupervised miles driven with zero injuries, airbag deployments, or pedestrian/cyclist hits. The company is logging approximately 79,671 miles per day as of mid-October, up from 32,292 miles per day in mid-September (*Tesla*). Tesla also reported its first Cybercab crash when the vehicle drove into a water-filled pothole at 5 mph (*NHTSA filing*).

Platform Aeronaut projects the US autonomous vehicle fleet will reach 225,000 vehicles by 2030, with 1P networks accounting for 145,000 vehicles and Uber-partnered fleets reaching 70,000 vehicles (*Platform Aeronaut*).

Rivian acquired Third Dimension, a UK startup that uses AI to convert images and videos into 3D environments, to accelerate development of its self-driving system for the robotaxis it plans to supply to Uber (*TechCrunch*).

GM announced it is targeting "eyes-off" autonomous driving capabilities for its pickups and SUVs by 2028, expanding beyond its current Super Cruise system (*Automotive News*).

### Technology & Product

Aurora CSO Nat Beuse detailed the company's fully driverless trucking operations and the steps taken to close its safety case for autonomous freight. The interview covered Aurora's operational design domain, edge case planning, uptime tracking, and what changes as the fleet scales (*The Driverless Digest*).

Tesla executives pushed the timeline for unsupervised FSD to Q2 2027 during the company's earnings call, clarifying it will initially only operate within existing Robotaxi zones. Tesla also acknowledged it now needs to build its own maps for FSD after years of claiming a maps-free approach (*Tesla Q3 earnings call*).

XPeng demonstrated its Next Generation Pilot (NGP) powered by VLA 2.0 through a 30-minute urban driving loop in Paris during the Paris Motor Show, showcasing the system's European capability (*Road to Autonomy*).

Mercedes-Benz announced a launch date for its point-to-point driving system, maintaining its internal development track alongside its partnership with Wayve (*various sources*).

### Capital & Financials

SpaceX reported $4.4B in Q3 profits on $14.6B in revenue, up 161% year-over-year, with Elon Musk predicting revenue between $500B-$1T by end of 2027. Musk provided updates on the potential Tesla/SpaceX merger, noting special committees have been formed to review the deal (*SpaceX earnings call*).

Anthropic's draft S-1 filing revealed $16.3B in revenue for the first half of 2026, up from $1.1B in the same period last year, while disclosing nearly $9B spent on infrastructure and $600B+ in future computing commitments (*WSJ*).

Tesla reported 67% year-over-year growth in FSD Supervised subscriptions, with the feature now purchased with 75% of new orders (*Tesla earnings*).

Bedrock Robotics is reportedly raising $600M to automate construction with robotics (*Axios*).

### Regulatory & Policy

Washington D.C. moved forward with a robotaxi bill that would initially cap operators at 200 vehicles and delay commercial rollout until 2028, while charging companies a "vehicle miles traveled" fee to support displaced rideshare drivers and Metro funding. The bill faces rising opposition from labor groups and mysterious advocacy organizations (*Washington Post*).

A poll backed by anti-Waymo labor unions found 58% of D.C. voters opposed to autonomous vehicles and 22% in support. However, Waymo provided crash data to media after the Mayor's Office denied a FOIA request for the same information (*Washington Sun, various sources*).

WeRide, Uber, and Spanish fleet operator AVOMO received Spain's first national Level 4 permit from the DGT in September for up to 20 vehicles in Greater Madrid, ahead of Waymo's arrival (*as previously reported*).

XPeng won Guangzhou robotaxi trial approval, a step toward public service in the city (*South China Morning Post*).

### Safety & Operations

Auve Tech's Chairman Taavi Rõivas (former Prime Minister of Estonia) detailed the company's driverless shuttle playbook for airports and private campuses, explaining redundancy systems, remote operator ratios, and deployments at Tallinn Airport and in Japan (*The AV Market Strategist*).

Waymo released data showing 95% fewer serious crashes than human drivers in its operating areas (*various sources*).

A Tesla driver using Full Self-Driving struck and killed a pedestrian crossing I-80, highlighting ongoing concerns about the supervised system's capabilities and driver attentiveness (*news reports*).

WeRide announced autonomous vehicles have completed testing and deployment milestones in multiple Asian markets, including Cyberjaya, Malaysia, where a Level 4 RoboVan began a six-month public-road trial (*BERNAMA*).

## Strategic Takeaways

The week's announcements reveal a maturing industry entering a new phase of geographic and operational expansion. Waymo's aggressive scaling plan—quintupling its fleet to 20,000 vehicles by end of 2027—represents the most ambitious deployment timeline from any Western AV company. The speed of this expansion, particularly the five-week window from Las Vegas market opening to airport service, suggests Waymo has developed repeatable playbooks for new market entry (*Semafor, Waymo*).

Tesla's disclosure of 3.3M unsupervised miles offers the first concrete window into the company's operational reality beyond marketing claims. The 117% acceleration in daily mileage over four weeks indicates rapid expansion, though the total remains approximately 14% of Waymo's current monthly rate (*Tesla, calculations based on Waymo data*). More significantly, Tesla's acknowledgment that it needs proprietary maps and that unsupervised FSD will initially only work in Robotaxi zones represents a major strategic pivot from years of claiming vision-only, maps-free autonomy would work everywhere. This suggests Tesla is converging toward the operational model pioneered by Waymo rather than blazing a fundamentally different path.

The European market is becoming increasingly crowded with multiple technology approaches. Bolt's selection of NVIDIA's Alpamayo Stack for its Lucid vehicles, Waymo's Madrid announcement, and WeRide's existing Spanish permit with Uber create a competitive landscape where no single player arrives first (*NVIDIA, Waymo, WeRide*). This differs sharply from the US pattern where Waymo typically establishes market presence before others. The technology diversity—from Waymo's proprietary full stack to NVIDIA's supplier model to Wayve's learning-based approach—means the European rollout will effectively test multiple autonomy architectures simultaneously in shared markets.

The regulatory picture remains fragmented, with Spain moving quickly to issue national permits while Washington D.C. considers restrictive local rules including vehicle caps and fee structures designed to protect incumbent labor (*Washington Post, various sources*). The D.C. debate illustrates the ongoing political challenges facing the industry, particularly in cities with strong labor constituencies, even as technical capability improves. However, the mysterious funding and false attribution in some anti-AV advocacy efforts (*Washington Post*) suggest the opposition may be less organic than it appears.
