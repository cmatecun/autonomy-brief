Anthropic S-1
-------------

From Bloomberg ( https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-26/anthropic-ipo-s-1-filing-offers-first-look-at-claude-maker-s-finances ) :

Anthropic PBC filed for its long-awaited IPO, a landmark moment in the artificial-intelligence industry that is growing ever-hungrier for cash to power ahead. The company behind Claude expects to begin trading on the Nasdaq Global Select Market under the symbol ANT, a filing Monday with the US Securities and Exchange Commission showed. Anthropic filed confidentially in June for a listing.

The filing moves Anthropic a step closer to being the first company focused primarily on developing the most advanced AI models to go public. Anthropic and some of its shareholders are offering stock in the IPO, according to the filing. The amount of shares they’re offering will be disclosed in a later filing.

Anthropic’s IPO will serve as a litmus test for how much investor demand exists for new equity tied to the AI trade, after optimism surrounding the technology helped power a four-year bull market in US stocks. Just two hours before Anthropic’s filing, Meta Platforms Inc. said it is seeking to raise as much as $95 billion from selling common and preferred stock in one of the largest equity offerings in history. Earlier this year, Google-parent Alphabet Inc. targeted raising as much as $85 billion in equity. Anthropic is expected to be followed onto public markets next year by its main competitor, OpenAI.

Numbers, finally — albeit somewhat incomplete ones. The figures provided are through June 30, i.e. the end of Anthropic’s major growth spurt; those numbers go stale on November 12 and will have to be updated if Anthropic doesn’t list by then — current expectations are the week of November 16.

Anthropic has in many respects timed this listing perfectly, thanks to the explosion in its top-line revenue in the first half of this year which, combined with the company’s delay in securing compute, led to extremely impressive margins (more on these in a moment); it will be interesting if somewhat slower growth in the 3rd quarter puts a damper on the listing (or possibly a revelation that Opus 5.5 re-accelerated growth turbocharges it). Revenue growth is, needless to say, a very big deal.

The Cost of Compute
-------------------

The most interesting financial numbers were absolutely buried in the S-1 ( https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2130357/000162828026067168/anthropicpbc-sx1.htm ) , all the way down on F-39 in the “Consolidated Financial Statements”; that is where Anthropic actually broke out its costs by function:

The two most interesting are Cost of Revenue and Research and Development, because that is where the company’s compute costs lie (along with Sales and Marketing, which is where the cost of free access is housed). Those still aren’t entirely clear numbers, particularly because Research and Development contains personnel costs, but you can make some assumptions to get to the following estimate (I subtracted the Cost of Revenue and estimated free inference — I used half of Sales & Marketing — from total compute to come up with R&D compute):

There are some really interesting takeaways here.

* This is a real tech company ( https://stratechery.com/2018/lessons-from-spotify/?access_token=eyJhbGciOiJFUzI1NiIsImtpZCI6IkxzanFRcEhoMUJUdmU0djJQUUVuejIiLCJ0eXAiOiJKV1QifQ.eyJhdWQiOiJzdHJhdGVjaGVyeS5wYXNzcG9ydC5vbmxpbmUiLCJhenAiOiJIS0xjUzREd1Nod1AyWURLYmZQV00xIiwiZW50Ijp7InVyaSI6WyJodHRwczovL3N0cmF0ZWNoZXJ5LmNvbS8yMDE4L2xlc3NvbnMtZnJvbS1zcG90aWZ5LyJdfSwiZXhwIjoxNzk1Njg5NDM4LCJpYXQiOjE3OTMwOTc0MzgsImlzcyI6Imh0dHBzOi8vYXBwLnBhc3Nwb3J0Lm9ubGluZS9vYXV0aCIsInNjb3BlIjoiZmVlZDpyZWFkIGFydGljbGU6cmVhZCBhc3NldDpyZWFkIGNhdGVnb3J5OnJlYWQgZW50aXRsZW1lbnRzIiwic3ViIjoiMDE5NTE2ZjEtYTM0Ni03NjE0LTllOTYtYjYzYmQxMWZlODU2IiwidXNlIjoiYWNjZXNzIn0.dxUYZ6xa4XP3ajdNUOwRcAWqKvToCG_NwEqufoHWeHlodL9D45wbJLHGM0wp5Nn6j59jZp3jd9jWwVl-YW0QfQ ) , in that Anthropic has meaningful leverage on its cost to serve. In the first half of this year the company had gross margins of 85%.
* Gross margins are inflated because Anthropic reports gross revenue from access to its models sold through AWS, Azure, and Google; those costs are in Sales and Marketing, and may be somewhere around 37% ( https://x.com/_seandavid/status/2114751806362272174?s=46 ) on around half their revenue. Still, these are real tech company margins, at least once revenue is big enough.
* Anthropic’s compute costs are dominated by R&D. Moreover, the cost of R&D compute is increasing faster than the cost of inference, at least over the last year.

What this means is that Anthropic in isolation seems like an incredible business; the challenge, however — and one reason why the company might want the industry to “pace the frontier” ( https://stratechery.com/2026/pacing-the-frontier-ais-digital-limits-ai-commissars/?access_token=eyJhbGciOiJFUzI1NiIsImtpZCI6IkxzanFRcEhoMUJUdmU0djJQUUVuejIiLCJ0eXAiOiJKV1QifQ.eyJhdWQiOiJzdHJhdGVjaGVyeS5wYXNzcG9ydC5vbmxpbmUiLCJhenAiOiJIS0xjUzREd1Nod1AyWURLYmZQV00xIiwiZW50Ijp7InVyaSI6WyJodHRwczovL3N0cmF0ZWNoZXJ5LmNvbS8yMDI2L3BhY2luZy10aGUtZnJvbnRpZXItYWlzLWRpZ2l0YWwtbGltaXRzLWFpLWNvbW1pc3NhcnMvIl19LCJleHAiOjE3OTU2ODk0MzgsImlhdCI6MTc5MzA5NzQzOCwiaXNzIjoiaHR0cHM6Ly9hcHAucGFzc3BvcnQub25saW5lL29hdXRoIiwic2NvcGUiOiJmZWVkOnJlYWQgYXJ0aWNsZTpyZWFkIGFzc2V0OnJlYWQgY2F0ZWdvcnk6cmVhZCBlbnRpdGxlbWVudHMiLCJzdWIiOiIwMTk1MTZmMS1hMzQ2LTc2MTQtOWU5Ni1iNjNiZDExZmU4NTYiLCJ1c2UiOiJhY2Nlc3MifQ.WT1DDi7D6c08JrvOkYeK1VG9W53s9-1eariDCXYHxyrFxhfuSk9FtwJqiMSZnxeRaHHgVSOcfSC5BLq-o0wJZQ ) — is that the cost of R&D compute is the variable that is arguably the most out of their control. The scale of Anthropic’s investment in R&D compute is a function of competition; those revenue figures ultimately rest on Anthropic having frontier models, at least for now, which means ever more compute.

This also gives a different spin to the idea of recursive self-improvement, i.e. the process where AI improves itself. Anthropic is already an RSI organism, at least from a financial perspective: the company is the biggest consumer of its own compute and its own AI, all in the service of building an even better AI. Folks complain about circular financing in the AI trade; the reality is that the companies at the core of that trade are the ultimate ouroboros, where their long-term financial upside is dependent on not eating themselves completely!

To that end, it is interesting to think about how the approximately $855 billion in future compute costs Anthropic has committed to might be allocated; the assumption is that the vast majority of that money is for inference, but if current trends hold it is R&D compute that will consume a major part of it.

Who Is Paying?
--------------

That noted, the $855 billion number is an estimate — basically I added up all disclosed commitment categories, which could have some overlap — and not locked in stone:

* $269.4 billion is non-cancelable minimum cloud spend with AWS, Google, and Microsoft.
* $161.2 billion is non-cancelable equipment leases with Broadcom that must be paid when equipment is ready.
* $21.0 billion in short-term commitments to unspecified counterparties is non-cancelable.
* $85.9 billion is committed to Nscale and Lambda as capacity is accepted.
* Up to $120.5 billion in commitment to SpaceXAI is cancelable with 90 days notice starting in 2027.
* The remaining ~$200 billion is a catch-all for unknown commitments.

The hyperscaler angle is particularly interesting. First, those are the contracts that are the most concrete. Second, those contracts were, in the case of Amazon and Google (and Broadcom), signed in conjunction with Note Purchase Agreements (NPAs), in which the hyperscalers basically loan money to Anthropic with the right to convert that debt into equity. That money, of course, flows right back to the hyperscalers to rent compute.

So who is actually paying? Despite those incredible margins, Anthropic reported an $80 billion loss in the first half of this year, because its liability to Amazon under earlier NPAs was marked to the dramatically increasing market price of the stock Amazon was entitled to, resulting in a $72 billion fair value charge. That right there is the clue: Anthropic isn’t out extra cash because its valuation increased; rather investors were diluted.

Anthropic has $77 billion in NPAs which have not yet been drawn on, which is to say that $80 billion “loss” is only the beginning, at least if the valuation continues to increase. That money is, whenever it is borrowed for compute from the lender, ultimately paid for by investors being diluted (although there will likely never be as large of a “loss”, which is to say a corresponding gain, for those other NPAs — that was an all-time investment by Amazon); meanwhile, every time Anthropic’s valuation increases by 50%, 2% of shares may go to the Anthropic Public Benefit Fund; all of this is before we get to the actual capital that Anthropic hopes to raise in this initial public offering, and the future equity offerings that will likely follow.

In short, what is happening with this IPO, along with Meta’s equity offering, which followed on Google’s offering earlier this year ( https://stratechery.com/2026/the-google-capital-company/?access_token=eyJhbGciOiJFUzI1NiIsImtpZCI6IkxzanFRcEhoMUJUdmU0djJQUUVuejIiLCJ0eXAiOiJKV1QifQ.eyJhdWQiOiJzdHJhdGVjaGVyeS5wYXNzcG9ydC5vbmxpbmUiLCJhenAiOiJIS0xjUzREd1Nod1AyWURLYmZQV00xIiwiZW50Ijp7InVyaSI6WyJodHRwczovL3N0cmF0ZWNoZXJ5LmNvbS8yMDI2L3RoZS1nb29nbGUtY2FwaXRhbC1jb21wYW55LyJdfSwiZXhwIjoxNzk1Njg5NDM4LCJpYXQiOjE3OTMwOTc0MzgsImlzcyI6Imh0dHBzOi8vYXBwLnBhc3Nwb3J0Lm9ubGluZS9vYXV0aCIsInNjb3BlIjoiZmVlZDpyZWFkIGFydGljbGU6cmVhZCBhc3NldDpyZWFkIGNhdGVnb3J5OnJlYWQgZW50aXRsZW1lbnRzIiwic3ViIjoiMDE5NTE2ZjEtYTM0Ni03NjE0LTllOTYtYjYzYmQxMWZlODU2IiwidXNlIjoiYWNjZXNzIn0.YERmylUtoaPH6CjRE-TfKca3uZaph_uZMFGuuWT6--MViUb2mTv6zobluz88HKywsoMk9Ks8gX0m3qAfXmZrAg ) , is that public market investors are being asked to pay for the AI buildout that the credit market is increasingly nervous about, and those payments are not necessarily straightforward. The price isn’t just shares, but the fact those shares will inevitably represent a continually decreasing share of the company.

This is why the revenue numbers will matter. In the long run, revenue does, of course, need to cover costs; in the short run, however, revenue will matter for the narrative which public stockholders will need to buy, such that they buy assets that are set up from the beginning for dilution. That will be fine if the valuation increase outpaces the dilution, and, to be fair, it is entirely reasonable to decide stock in a frontier lab is, no matter the price, a reasonable call option on seemingly unlimited upside. That’s the thing about revenue growth: when it’s up-and-to-the-right you can get away with anything.
