** Anthropic
------------------------------------------------------------

My biggest relief in the Anthropic S1 is that at last we can get away from these absurd annualised revenue numbers leaked in round numbers without specific dates or proper definitions. Now we have actual GAAP quarterly revenue and an actual P&L. Happy days! And those revenue numbers more or less exactly line up with the explosive growth that had been leaked or released.

After that, I think this is kind of a Rorschach blot: people see what they’re interested in.  Operations people look at customer concentration and revenue retention. Accelerationists see the role of Claude in R&D. If you want to focus on the debt burden, you can see close to $1 trillion in future purchase commitments and a plan to build its own data centres. If you want to focus on circular revenue, there’s plenty of that here as well, and Anthropic itself points out that the hyperscalers are customers, suppliers, shareholders and competitors, which makes for an interesting governance conversation. Indeed, you can also look at the governance itself: as is the fashion, the founders lock in an absurdly complex shareholder structure that guarantees them control - here all seven of them, which starts to look like the procedure for electing the Doge of Venice.

What interests me, though is what this tells us about the strategic questions around the deployment of AI, and the role that AI labs will play within that. Here I care most about the core unit economics, but the puzzle is that they don’t really have very much predictive value.

** Results season and an AI correction
------------------------------------------------------------

As I pointed out previously, there were two fundamental changes in AI this year. First, we have massive product-market fit for AI in software development, and second, capex has escalated far beyond the cash flows of big tech companies, meaning that they have to raise money from equity and debt markets instead. These days those markets are feeling pretty bipolar: on one hand “oh my God, look at these numbers going up!” but on the other hand “oh my God, look at these numbers going up!” The higher the investment gets and the more the valuations and the leverage extend, the more people worry that the whole thing is overcooked, worry that the whole thing is going to collapse and look for signs that it might be about to.

We had a lot of that this week. Google said that AI Overviews are having an impact on search revenue, Intel’s numbers came in short, Amazon’s capex outlook was high, Meta is raising $95 billion in new equity (with Nvidia, AMD and Broadcom looking at taking stakes - more circularity), OpenAI cut its latest fundraising from $30bn at $1.4tr to $20bn at $1.25tr, S&P downgraded Meta’s credit rating outlook and Microsoft offered buyouts to 60,000 employees. People worry about the revenue and they worry about the investment.

In a different environment (say, a mature industry where we understood what was going on) it would be worth doing a deep dive on Google’s ad revenue blip, but that’s not really what’s happening here. People are looking for a sign that the curves might be changing direction, and more fundamentally, worrying that the multiple trillions of dollars of projected investment in AI infrastructure won’t produce a great return, or if it does, will produce a return that goes to other people, and today we just don’t know (and the latest quarterly numbers won’t tell you either). But meanwhile, no green on the screen. ** Binance hacked for $6bn
------------------------------------------------------------

Crypto people used to tell me how important it was that the government couldn’t take their money, except it turns out that with crypto, the North Korean government can take your money. The FBI blamed North Korea for a hack this week that got access to internal systems at Binance and transferred $6.5 billion (!). Binance claims that it managed to freeze or recover about 90% of that. But still, as the saying goes, crypto is an automated bug-bounty system. ** New science
------------------------------------------------------------

This week, OpenAI and Anthropic both announced disproofs of the Exponential Time Hypothesis (ETH), a 30-year-old foundational assumption in computer science that set limits on how efficient an algorithm to solve certain problems could be. To simplify hugely, this assumption meant that for such problems, computer scientists (including cryptographers) may not have looked for a more efficient solution because ETH said there wasn’t one, but in fact there may be. ** Doordash buys Instacart
------------------------------------------------------------

The real question is is what you can load onto a two-sided network, with what CAC, densities and commissions on each side.
