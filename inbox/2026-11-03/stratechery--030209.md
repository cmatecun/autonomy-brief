Apple Earnings
--------------

From the Wall Street Journal ( https://www.wsj.com/tech/apple-beats-wall-street-expectations-with-boost-from-higher-prices-36749418 ) :

The Apple growth and profit machine is wavering, and the boost from price increases might not be enough to keep it going at historic rates. That dials up the pressure on Apple’s new chief executive, John Ternus. The company saved the bad news for its earnings call, an hour after it posted its results for the September quarter, projecting that for the current quarter its high-margin services business will continue to slow and that its widely watched gross profit margin will continue to fall.

Apple shares had been flat in post-close trading but declined as much as 4% after the company made the projections, which fell short of Wall Street estimates. The impact of high memory costs are weighing even more heavily on the company, Ternus and Apple Chief Financial Officer Kevan Parekh said on the company’s earnings call. Apple has stockpiled cheaper components purchased earlier, but those inventories are dwindling…

Investors have bid up Apple shares because it has been a port in the AI storm—a reliable and capital-efficient business that stands apart from rivals which are spending hundreds of billions of dollars racing to the AI frontier. While others invest in data centers, Apple has been pouring its own cash into buybacks and dividends. But that safety may be challenged if Ternus can’t speed up Apple’s innovation engine.

Actually, I thought that on his maiden earnings call ( https://sixcolors.com/post/2026/11/hello-everyone-its-john-apples-q4-2026-earnings-call-transcript/ ) Ternus made a good case that Apple’s innovation engine is running pretty well:

This is an amazing time at Apple. We just completed one of the most prolific launch periods in our history. It’s been a remarkable time for innovation across all of our categories. One of the greatest testaments to that is the all-new iPhone Duo, the most transformational iPhone since the original. We’re also incredibly excited about iPhone 18 Pro and 18 Pro Max, which take massive leaps forward on camera, battery life, and performance—features that our users care deeply about. Meanwhile, touch has arrived on Mac for the first time with the all-new MacBook Pro. And we were excited to announce the new iMac and 14-inch MacBook Pro. The all-new iPad mini features a stunning new OLED display and brings a super thin design to the smallest member of the iPad family. Apple Watch now offers users the clearest window yet into their heart health. We introduced AirPods 5, which feature industry-leading active noise cancellation in an open-ear design. And we’ve laid the foundation for an even more intelligent smart home with the all-new HomeView, Apple TV 4K, and HomePod mini.

That’s a lot of new products, and the headliner — the iPhone Duo — appears to be a big hit, murmurs about hinge issues notwithstanding. Speaking anecdotally, the response to the device amongst normal users is extremely positive, even if it doesn’t appeal to more hardcore users ( https://daringfireball.net/2026/10/the_iphone_duo ) ; to the extent that is true, that’s a positive sign, in two regards: first, it’s a pleasant reminder that people who obsess over this stuff don’t always represent normal users, and second, there is still room for Apple to segment the market, not just by price but also through fundamental design (as for me, I’m waiting for the Air 2).

Meanwhile, the new MacBook Pro with touch ( https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2026/10/apple-unveils-groundbreaking-macbook-pro-with-proled-xdr-display/ ) is exactly the computer I’ve been hoping for ever since I first used computers with touch displays at Microsoft 15 years ago. What Microsoft got wrong with Windows 8 was that they overhauled the interface to be touch-first; yes, you can use a mouse-and-keyboard with a touch interface easily enough, but it’s too easy: you give up a lot of power and control in exchange for denominating the user interface by the least precise input method.

Apple didn’t do that: touch is just an extra interaction layer that is there when you need it, and not catered to when you don’t; that’s exactly where Microsoft ended up too, which is why nearly all high-end Windows devices still have touch, even if the operating system long ago reverted towards being built around precise mouse-and-keyboard interfaces. This isn’t a touch-screen computer; it’s a computer that you can touch when and if you find it convenient (and having used them, it’s very nice to have!).

Memory Problems
---------------

Apple first started pushing the narrative that they were a services company in 2016, but, as I argued at the time ( https://stratechery.com/2016/twitter-follow-up-apples-good-earnings-apple-is-not-a-services-company/?access_token=eyJhbGciOiJFUzI1NiIsImtpZCI6IkxzanFRcEhoMUJUdmU0djJQUUVuejIiLCJ0eXAiOiJKV1QifQ.eyJhdWQiOiJzdHJhdGVjaGVyeS5wYXNzcG9ydC5vbmxpbmUiLCJhenAiOiJIS0xjUzREd1Nod1AyWURLYmZQV00xIiwiZW50Ijp7InVyaSI6WyJodHRwczovL3N0cmF0ZWNoZXJ5LmNvbS8yMDE2L3R3aXR0ZXItZm9sbG93LXVwLWFwcGxlcy1nb29kLWVhcm5pbmdzLWFwcGxlLWlzLW5vdC1hLXNlcnZpY2VzLWNvbXBhbnkvIl19LCJleHAiOjE3OTYyOTU3MjksImlhdCI6MTc5MzcwMzcyOSwiaXNzIjoiaHR0cHM6Ly9hcHAucGFzc3BvcnQub25saW5lL29hdXRoIiwic2NvcGUiOiJmZWVkOnJlYWQgYXJ0aWNsZTpyZWFkIGFzc2V0OnJlYWQgY2F0ZWdvcnk6cmVhZCBlbnRpdGxlbWVudHMiLCJzdWIiOiIwMTk1MTZmMS1hMzQ2LTc2MTQtOWU5Ni1iNjNiZDExZmU4NTYiLCJ1c2UiOiJhY2Nlc3MifQ.B5qGRC7eOeQqWQFYFKu6ONlEo6u91osQA9w_ZPKtsnHxz4LRT5qA62b1M3OXVtpzKCQVms6ag1XvmKhy0gcnSQ ) , that was more aspirational and narrative selling than reality, given the fact the stock market was still focused on iPhone sales; three years later, however, when Apple stopped reporting unit sales and started talking about its installed base I called the switch: Apple was a services company ( https://stratechery.com/2019/apple-earnings-apple-the-services-company-for-real/?access_token=eyJhbGciOiJFUzI1NiIsImtpZCI6IkxzanFRcEhoMUJUdmU0djJQUUVuejIiLCJ0eXAiOiJKV1QifQ.eyJhdWQiOiJzdHJhdGVjaGVyeS5wYXNzcG9ydC5vbmxpbmUiLCJhenAiOiJIS0xjUzREd1Nod1AyWURLYmZQV00xIiwiZW50Ijp7InVyaSI6WyJodHRwczovL3N0cmF0ZWNoZXJ5LmNvbS8yMDE5L2FwcGxlLWVhcm5pbmdzLWFwcGxlLXRoZS1zZXJ2aWNlcy1jb21wYW55LWZvci1yZWFsLyJdfSwiZXhwIjoxNzk2Mjk1NzI5LCJpYXQiOjE3OTM3MDM3MjksImlzcyI6Imh0dHBzOi8vYXBwLnBhc3Nwb3J0Lm9ubGluZS9vYXV0aCIsInNjb3BlIjoiZmVlZDpyZWFkIGFydGljbGU6cmVhZCBhc3NldDpyZWFkIGNhdGVnb3J5OnJlYWQgZW50aXRsZW1lbnRzIiwic3ViIjoiMDE5NTE2ZjEtYTM0Ni03NjE0LTllOTYtYjYzYmQxMWZlODU2IiwidXNlIjoiYWNjZXNzIn0._yeyeZ3YxtU36rdYb7l7rFNk0VQpJRVaf-iiDHkG0dH1LQO0MFZG96jzWLPUj_S87YRSBpYVcFah4hOiaMolHg ). Three years after that, I was shocked when the company didn’t raise prices in the face of inflation ( https://stratechery.com/2022/the-services-iphone/?access_token=eyJhbGciOiJFUzI1NiIsImtpZCI6IkxzanFRcEhoMUJUdmU0djJQUUVuejIiLCJ0eXAiOiJKV1QifQ.eyJhdWQiOiJzdHJhdGVjaGVyeS5wYXNzcG9ydC5vbmxpbmUiLCJhenAiOiJIS0xjUzREd1Nod1AyWURLYmZQV00xIiwiZW50Ijp7InVyaSI6WyJodHRwczovL3N0cmF0ZWNoZXJ5LmNvbS8yMDIyL3RoZS1zZXJ2aWNlcy1pcGhvbmUvIl19LCJleHAiOjE3OTYyOTU3MjksImlhdCI6MTc5MzcwMzcyOSwiaXNzIjoiaHR0cHM6Ly9hcHAucGFzc3BvcnQub25saW5lL29hdXRoIiwic2NvcGUiOiJmZWVkOnJlYWQgYXJ0aWNsZTpyZWFkIGFzc2V0OnJlYWQgY2F0ZWdvcnk6cmVhZCBlbnRpdGxlbWVudHMiLCJzdWIiOiIwMTk1MTZmMS1hMzQ2LTc2MTQtOWU5Ni1iNjNiZDExZmU4NTYiLCJ1c2UiOiJhY2Nlc3MifQ.hTLJS9cWQdmRciVeyBK21aYPPaf5bvkbnSfoojcFTWJ6aGJjHUmy0cWBi6n5TBbEMqwlvmpUPG4vlnRuWUE1gA ) , but I shouldn’t have been: effectively lowering prices — which helps gain market share — is exactly the right approach to take if growing services is the top priority. Indeed, Apple continued to not (or barely) raise prices for years after that, and then came the memory crisis.

It was memory that dominated the Q&A on the earnings call, and it’s hitting Apple on multiple fronts. First, even with June’s price increases (which didn’t include the iPhone), margins were down by about one percentage point because of memory. Second, even with price increases both in September (on the iPhone) and October (on Macs and iPads, above and beyond the summer’s increase), margins will decrease by another 1.5–2 percentage points. Moreover, Ternus said it’s going to get worse:

Let me give you an overall view on memory. What we’re seeing with memory pricing right now is unprecedented, and as we said earlier in the September quarter, we did see higher memory costs as compared to the June quarter, which were partly offset by the benefit of carry-in inventory and lower costs on certain non-memory components. Now, for the December quarter, we expect even higher memory costs, partly offset by a few factors. So, as we said, we changed the price on many of our products, including Mac and iPad at the end of June, and iPhone in September, and these price increases provided some offset. We also expect to see some benefit from carry-in inventory in the December quarter as well, as well as lower costs on certain non-memory components. And looking beyond the December quarter, we see the pricing for memory purchases continuing to increase. As we said before, we do believe we will see a decreasing benefit from the carry-in inventory on memory over time. And there is one more thing I wanted to add. As we work to improve supply, our manufacturing purchase obligations have increased by $33 billion from June to the September quarter, which you’ll see in our annual filing when it comes out tomorrow. Now back to Kevan for the other parts of it.

That last bit is point number three: Apple is having to make significant purchase obligations to secure memory, likely locking in these high prices. Fourth, despite those purchase obligations — or perhaps because purchase obligations are necessary — Apple actually doesn’t have enough memory! This was COO Sabih Khan, the second new addition to the earnings call:

So during the September quarter, we experienced supply constraints on iPhone, Mac and iPad, and this was driven by the high levels of demand, and as we’ve said before, we’re seeing less flexibility in the supply chain than normal. The Q4 constraints were primarily driven by the availability of the advanced nodes our SOCs are produced on, but as we have transitioned more of our lineup to the latest 2nm technology nodes, our constraints on advanced nodes are easing. And for the December quarter, we expect constraints on Mac and iPad, primarily driven by memory, as demand has been higher than what we expected.

I made this point previously ( https://stratechery.com/2026/apple-earnings-supply-chain-speculation-china-and-industrial-design/?access_token=eyJhbGciOiJFUzI1NiIsImtpZCI6IkxzanFRcEhoMUJUdmU0djJQUUVuejIiLCJ0eXAiOiJKV1QifQ.eyJhdWQiOiJzdHJhdGVjaGVyeS5wYXNzcG9ydC5vbmxpbmUiLCJhenAiOiJIS0xjUzREd1Nod1AyWURLYmZQV00xIiwiZW50Ijp7InVyaSI6WyJodHRwczovL3N0cmF0ZWNoZXJ5LmNvbS8yMDI2L2FwcGxlLWVhcm5pbmdzLXN1cHBseS1jaGFpbi1zcGVjdWxhdGlvbi1jaGluYS1hbmQtaW5kdXN0cmlhbC1kZXNpZ24vIl19LCJleHAiOjE3OTYyOTU3MjksImlhdCI6MTc5MzcwMzcyOSwiaXNzIjoiaHR0cHM6Ly9hcHAucGFzc3BvcnQub25saW5lL29hdXRoIiwic2NvcGUiOiJmZWVkOnJlYWQgYXJ0aWNsZTpyZWFkIGFzc2V0OnJlYWQgY2F0ZWdvcnk6cmVhZCBlbnRpdGxlbWVudHMiLCJzdWIiOiIwMTk1MTZmMS1hMzQ2LTc2MTQtOWU5Ni1iNjNiZDExZmU4NTYiLCJ1c2UiOiJhY2Nlc3MifQ.BBfbpsNyNFAvUPylOnugMzRqckULAtRkkoUoELG7R_w2n1YVqt0PHkSHwdONEk5u0oYNcw75cE4768hvJueJ-g ) in the context of those SoC constraints, which is that Apple isn’t just used to dictating price to suppliers, but also having the freedom to ramp its manufacturing up and down in response to demand while forcing those suppliers to maintain the capacity and inventory to meet their needs. Now, however, Apple has to actually forecast demand, knowing that if they forecast too low they won’t sell as much as they could have, and if they forecast too high they are the ones who have to carry excess inventory.

The Hardware Company Regression
-------------------------------

The good news for Apple goes back to the innovation question: the company is consistently missing its forecasts ( https://stratechery.com/2026/apple-earnings-more-on-amazons-earnings/?access_token=eyJhbGciOiJFUzI1NiIsImtpZCI6IkxzanFRcEhoMUJUdmU0djJQUUVuejIiLCJ0eXAiOiJKV1QifQ.eyJhdWQiOiJzdHJhdGVjaGVyeS5wYXNzcG9ydC5vbmxpbmUiLCJhenAiOiJIS0xjUzREd1Nod1AyWURLYmZQV00xIiwiZW50Ijp7InVyaSI6WyJodHRwczovL3N0cmF0ZWNoZXJ5LmNvbS8yMDI2L2FwcGxlLWVhcm5pbmdzLW1vcmUtb24tYW1hem9ucy1lYXJuaW5ncy8iXX0sImV4cCI6MTc5NjI5NTcyOSwiaWF0IjoxNzkzNzAzNzI5LCJpc3MiOiJodHRwczovL2FwcC5wYXNzcG9ydC5vbmxpbmUvb2F1dGgiLCJzY29wZSI6ImZlZWQ6cmVhZCBhcnRpY2xlOnJlYWQgYXNzZXQ6cmVhZCBjYXRlZ29yeTpyZWFkIGVudGl0bGVtZW50cyIsInN1YiI6IjAxOTUxNmYxLWEzNDYtNzYxNC05ZTk2LWI2M2JkMTFmZTg1NiIsInVzZSI6ImFjY2VzcyJ9.rffcObHjkDSMGQ6Ku6ZhE4SaLAcKvgD37DclR3y-H3ymeo8PInUMt3jrI7zyrwoS1kKo4Nk5jZegTzEkwDVyKA ) by underestimating demand. Of course it would be ideal if the company had enough devices to sell to everyone who wanted one, as soon as they want it, but in the grand scheme of things it’s good news that Apple devices seem to be more popular than ever. Moreover, that popularity is increasing even as the price increases. CFO Kevan Parekh was, understandably, unwilling to say this was a trend:

*If I kind of unpack the comments around supply chain, about availability of components, and your outlook for the December quarter, can you help us understand sort of the difference or maybe the delta of quantity versus price across the product lineup? Because it certainly looks like you’re looking at double digit growth across the iPhone and the Mac business, and we all know, I think, in the industry that there’s been pretty strong price inflation across the PC/Mac ecosystem, and you guys mentioned that you’ve reluctantly taken price as well. So can you help us understand kind of what’s the driving dynamics in that market?*

*KP* : We don’t normally provide that level of detail or color as we look at the outlook, other than to say, again, we did guide to the 7% to 10% year on year, and again, just as a reminder, that is on top of a phenomenal December quarter last year, where revenue grew 16%, and so we don’t provide the level of detail, but I would say it’s early to really read price elasticity at this point in time. The iPad and Mac were constrained since we had made those adjustments, and again, we just recently just adjusted the price on iPhone, so it’s really early to really assess any elasticity impact at this stage.

This explains why I was so shocked when Apple didn’t raise prices four years ago; from the very beginning of Stratechery ( https://stratechery.com/2013/where-is-the-bad-news/?access_token=eyJhbGciOiJFUzI1NiIsImtpZCI6IkxzanFRcEhoMUJUdmU0djJQUUVuejIiLCJ0eXAiOiJKV1QifQ.eyJhdWQiOiJzdHJhdGVjaGVyeS5wYXNzcG9ydC5vbmxpbmUiLCJhenAiOiJIS0xjUzREd1Nod1AyWURLYmZQV00xIiwiZW50Ijp7InVyaSI6WyJodHRwczovL3N0cmF0ZWNoZXJ5LmNvbS8yMDEzL3doZXJlLWlzLXRoZS1iYWQtbmV3cy8iXX0sImV4cCI6MTc5NjI5NTcyOSwiaWF0IjoxNzkzNzAzNzI5LCJpc3MiOiJodHRwczovL2FwcC5wYXNzcG9ydC5vbmxpbmUvb2F1dGgiLCJzY29wZSI6ImZlZWQ6cmVhZCBhcnRpY2xlOnJlYWQgYXNzZXQ6cmVhZCBjYXRlZ29yeTpyZWFkIGVudGl0bGVtZW50cyIsInN1YiI6IjAxOTUxNmYxLWEzNDYtNzYxNC05ZTk2LWI2M2JkMTFmZTg1NiIsInVzZSI6ImFjY2VzcyJ9.DGnUIPIDiIRP0lsI7PWSebc5J-qr_DW4Zb9kV2q7VhQMbPJz-HQmSbgek7LrufWW_HbxaNb3hJCpGMEAf2KePw ) I’ve been wondering just how high Apple could raise prices without significantly hurting demand, and well, the seeming answer — just as it was with the iPhone 5S vs 5C, and just as it was with the iPhone X ( https://stratechery.com/2018/the-iphone-franchise/?access_token=eyJhbGciOiJFUzI1NiIsImtpZCI6IkxzanFRcEhoMUJUdmU0djJQUUVuejIiLCJ0eXAiOiJKV1QifQ.eyJhdWQiOiJzdHJhdGVjaGVyeS5wYXNzcG9ydC5vbmxpbmUiLCJhenAiOiJIS0xjUzREd1Nod1AyWURLYmZQV00xIiwiZW50Ijp7InVyaSI6WyJodHRwczovL3N0cmF0ZWNoZXJ5LmNvbS8yMDE4L3RoZS1pcGhvbmUtZnJhbmNoaXNlLyJdfSwiZXhwIjoxNzk2Mjk1NzI5LCJpYXQiOjE3OTM3MDM3MjksImlzcyI6Imh0dHBzOi8vYXBwLnBhc3Nwb3J0Lm9ubGluZS9vYXV0aCIsInNjb3BlIjoiZmVlZDpyZWFkIGFydGljbGU6cmVhZCBhc3NldDpyZWFkIGNhdGVnb3J5OnJlYWQgZW50aXRsZW1lbnRzIiwic3ViIjoiMDE5NTE2ZjEtYTM0Ni03NjE0LTllOTYtYjYzYmQxMWZlODU2IiwidXNlIjoiYWNjZXNzIn0.U5DfORjhtu4c_tB_xxen-8MPIzprs_X3RCFk4CHNjkPI6vxeMEJNvM6Fn4HDBxwaWQFtpQmKZdMcqssZL-BCGw ) — is significantly higher!

There is, however, one fly in the ointment; this was the paragraph that came after Ternus’ opening extolling Apple’s hardware launches:

On top of it all, we’re unlocking more powerful AI experiences across our lineup, including with the all-new Siri AI, a profoundly capable and deeply personal assistant seamlessly integrated across our platforms. And like everything we make, Siri AI is built with essential privacy protections so no one can access users’ data, not even Apple. We launched Siri AI in beta for US English users in September and expanded to French, Japanese, Korean, Portuguese, and Spanish in October. We’re really excited to have rolled all these innovations out, and there’s still so much more we plan to do.

Needless to say, I found this a lot less compelling than the hardware launches! Ternus got several questions on the call about Siri AI and its lack of agentic capabilities; Ternus did suggest that Siri had the capacity to be more capable:

First and foremost, we’re incredibly excited to launch Siri AI alongside the latest generation of Apple intelligence features. Siri has long been popular with our users with more than two and a half billion requests every day, and with Siri AI, we really have the kind of the Siri that we always wanted. It’s primarily request-based today, but as we talked about at WWDC, it’s built on a modern orchestrator-driven architecture that gives us real room to expand its capabilities over time. As far as agentic capabilities, we think these are promising tools for users. We just want to make sure that users are aware of what they’re giving access to. We want to make sure this is done in a way that protects security and privacy. So we’re pushing really hard on that with Siri AI, but we’re also really encouraged by the fact that Apple devices, we think, are the best places to use all forms of AI.
