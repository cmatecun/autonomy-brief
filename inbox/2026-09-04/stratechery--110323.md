On that note, here were a few of our favorites this week.

* *The Market Speaks.* Anthropic released Fable 5.1 this week, and the most interesting part of their announcement had nothing to do with the model or its capabilities, but rather its terms. Anthropic is walking back their controversial data retention policies ( https://stratechery.com/2026/fable-5-1-enterprise-frontier-safeguards/ ) that sparked a huge backlash earlier this year. The reason? The company needs to make money, and OpenAI is competing hard (and OpenAI had a model release of their own, which I talked to OpenAI President Greg Brockman about in the Stratechery Interview ( https://stratechery.com/2026/an-interview-with-openai-president-greg-brockman-about-astra-and-alignment/ ) ). — Ben Thompson

* *Apple Finds Religion.* Dedicated readers and listeners are no doubt familiar with Ben’s crusade to fix the Vision Pro, and specifically Apple’s approach to producing live events in immersive video ( https://stratechery.com/2026/apple-you-still-dont-understand-the-vision-pro/ ). Well, Apple nailed it with their immersive broadcast of Friday Night Baseball. Come to Monday’s Dithering episode ( https://dithering.passport.online/member/episode/vision-pro-baseball?guid=https%3A%2F%2Fdithering.passport.online%2Fmember%2Fepisode%2Fvision-pro-baseball ) to hear John’s experience with the Red Sox and Yankees, and stay for Ben’s smug satisfaction (and don’t worry, he registers a few additional complaints for good measure). — Andrew Sharp

* *Society Loses Friction.* Meta announced last week that as part of a settlement with 29 states it will make sweeping changes to restrict the use of Instagram and Meta among teens. Ben had conflicted reactions to the news on Monday ( http://stratechery.com/2026/meta-settles-a-framework-for-regulating-content-the-rest-of-big-tech/ ) , and on Thursday’s Sharp Tech ( https://sharptech.fm/member/episode/fable-5-1-and-anthropics-data-retention-pivot-ai-civilizations-and-related-matters-q-a-on-meta-shopify-3-d-printing ) we went deeper on the libertarian vs. conservative tensions that will inform a variety of these regulation questions going forward. That conversation ultimately echoed a very early Stratechery riff ( https://stratechery.com/2013/friction/ ) on the society-altering implications of tech that removes friction; 13 years later, our generation is still catching up. — AS
