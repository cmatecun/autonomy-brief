Tesla Begins Offering Rides in a Car Without a Steering Wheel (link [ https://substack.com/redirect/b20e3dc2-b6da-4999-a2c7-d32d0bda619b?j=eyJ1IjoiMXlnZjcifQ.KOUIeBfeCRzhIwFnKXyQigR4j0VpF-SZzascYhSZunw ], no paywall [ https://substack.com/redirect/99c63492-c215-467b-bb5e-8e8b6feee11a?j=eyJ1IjoiMXlnZjcifQ.KOUIeBfeCRzhIwFnKXyQigR4j0VpF-SZzascYhSZunw ]). First impression: impressive, and I’m excited to try it when we head to Austin for our Urban Autonomy Summit [ https://substack.com/redirect/e157b80e-4547-4a27-a10d-8670ec72d7d9?j=eyJ1IjoiMXlnZjcifQ.KOUIeBfeCRzhIwFnKXyQigR4j0VpF-SZzascYhSZunw ] on 10/14. I like the way the doors open vertically, although I’m not sure how practical they’ll be in the real world. There’s also a ton of trunk space, but only having two seats still feels a little strange for a robotaxi.
On Thursday, Tesla said on its website that people could hail the Cybercab and “use it for errands, commuting and more.” The company said that in addition to two passengers, the vehicle would have room for “luggage, golf clubs, scooters and more.”
‘My First Ride in the Cybercab’ (link [ https://substack.com/redirect/224f9a43-d260-4469-9f2e-9fed98ac2c00?j=eyJ1IjoiMXlnZjcifQ.KOUIeBfeCRzhIwFnKXyQigR4j0VpF-SZzascYhSZunw ]).

‘I Made a Bet with Tesla’ (link [ https://substack.com/redirect/2e883e3f-19f9-4099-b624-6574eb398dd8?j=eyJ1IjoiMXlnZjcifQ.KOUIeBfeCRzhIwFnKXyQigR4j0VpF-SZzascYhSZunw ]).
‘Feds launch investigation into Tesla’s Cybercab deployment’ (link [ https://substack.com/redirect/a6232381-22b1-4122-b4f1-ffaee976a86c?j=eyJ1IjoiMXlnZjcifQ.KOUIeBfeCRzhIwFnKXyQigR4j0VpF-SZzascYhSZunw ]).
NHTSA said Friday that Tesla told the agency it self-certified the Cybercab as being compliant with all of the Federal Motor Vehicle Safety Standards (FMVSS). Automakers traditionally self-certify whether their vehicles comply with FMVSS rules. In the filing, the agency said it was opening the investigation to “examine the process and technical data on which Tesla relied when certifying the Cybercab and related issues.”

Uber, Wayve Unleash Supervised Robotaxis on London’s Winding Streets (link [ https://substack.com/redirect/448ecdc4-26bf-40b5-8e25-98887bcad17d?j=eyJ1IjoiMXlnZjcifQ.KOUIeBfeCRzhIwFnKXyQigR4j0VpF-SZzascYhSZunw ], no paywall [ https://substack.com/redirect/40343a6d-7619-4cf0-987b-dd1b0e7d53a5?j=eyJ1IjoiMXlnZjcifQ.KOUIeBfeCRzhIwFnKXyQigR4j0VpF-SZzascYhSZunw ]). This is a cool milestone, and kudos to Bloomberg for getting the headline right. It still bothers me every time I see people celebrating the launch of “autonomous vehicles” when the vehicles are, in fact, not autonomous yet lol.
Passengers who order a ride through Uber may be offered a trip in one of 15 Wayve-powered Ford Mustang Mach-E vehicles.A trained safety driver holding a private hire license from Transport for London will sit behind the wheel and take control of the vehicle if needed.
Bloomberg took a test drive in one of the Wayve-powered robotaxis. Over roughly 40 minutes, the car showed human-like nudging behavior to cut into a line of bumper-to-bumper traffic, handled tight lane changes, a protest in Parliament Square and pedestrians stepping out erratically into the road.
The safety driver took over twice. Once when the vehicle was trying to merge into gridlock and was briefly blocking oncoming traffic, and again to switch lanes to get around a few stationary cars. The rest of the time, his hands hovered just below the steering wheel as it turned on its own.
