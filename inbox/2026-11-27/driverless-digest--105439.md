Top Story of the Week
Nuro, Lucid and Uber begin driverless robotaxi testing in the Bay Area (link [ https://substack.com/redirect/d8beb7c2-b101-40b1-9a23-ebcb8fb15dc7?j=eyJ1IjoiMXlnZjcifQ.KOUIeBfeCRzhIwFnKXyQigR4j0VpF-SZzascYhSZunw ]). Nuro had a nice head start with years of experience [ https://substack.com/redirect/1eb226bc-5a70-4d41-bb19-0b397046df05?j=eyJ1IjoiMXlnZjcifQ.KOUIeBfeCRzhIwFnKXyQigR4j0VpF-SZzascYhSZunw ] developing and testing its autonomous driving technology, but it’s still impressive how quickly this partnership has moved. Uber, Lucid and Nuro announced [ https://substack.com/redirect/72d63c76-0222-481b-8d66-3f58067545cd?j=eyJ1IjoiMXlnZjcifQ.KOUIeBfeCRzhIwFnKXyQigR4j0VpF-SZzascYhSZunw ] the deal in July 2025, started supervised testing in December 2025, and are now testing without a safety driver just 16 months after the announcement. Next up is the planned commercial launch on Uber in Q1 2027, slightly later than the original 2026 target.
The other interesting piece of this partnership is the vehicle choice: the Lucid Gravity is a high-end luxury SUV that starts around $80k, while the rest of the industry is trending towards lower cost vehicles (the Hyundai IONIQ 5 that Waymo is rolling out starts at just $35k). Obviously neither company is paying retail, but that’s still a huge difference in vehicle costs. Should make for a great rider experience, but I’m curious how the economics will work out at scale.
There is now no safety driver in the vehicle. Employee rides in the driverless vehicles will follow soon, ahead of a public launch expected on Uber in Q1 2027. Testing will take place in Palo Alto, Mountain View, Los Altos and Sunnyvale, before expanding to more Bay Area cities next year. Lucid has delivered a fleet of more than 100 vehicles with hardware, software and rider-experience updates for this phase…Uber has committed to buying at least 35,000 Lucid vehicles equipped with the Nuro Driver, with plans to expand across dozens of global markets over the coming years.
