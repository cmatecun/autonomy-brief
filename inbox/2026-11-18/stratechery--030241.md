Nvidia Earnings
---------------

From the Wall Street Journal ( https://www.wsj.com/tech/ai/nvidia-nvda-3q-2027-earnings-report-stock-6e642303 ) :

Nvidia reported another quarter of record-breaking sales, with revenue of $115.8 billion, 6.3% higher than the $108.9 billion predicted by analysts polled by FactSet and more than double the year-earlier quarter. Net income rose to $65.3 billion in the quarter ended Oct. 25, also beating analyst expectations. In its most important segment, data-center revenue, Nvidia reported $107.6 billion in sales, up 110% from a year ago, driven by the rollout of the company’s new Vera Rubin servers. Looking ahead, the company forecast $130 billion in revenue for the current quarter, about 4% higher than Wall Street estimates. On a call with investors, interim CEO Colette Kress said the company was raising its forecast revenue growth for fiscal 2028 from 70% to 75%.

This was, needless to say, a significantly less interesting earnings call ( https://seekingalpha.com/article/4969356-nvidia-corporation-nvda-q3-2027-earnings-call-transcript ) than usual, given the lack of one Jensen Huang. Indeed, the only really interesting part was, unsurprisingly, about him, and it came at the very top from Chairman of the Board Harvey Jones:

On November 12, our Board of Directors announced that after disclosure of personal behavior inconsistent with the company’s code of conduct, standards and cultural norms, Jensen Huang voluntarily resigned his positions as President, CEO and Board member. The Nvidia Board unanimously accepted his resignation and does not intend to reinstate Jensen as CEO. The Board appointed Colette Kress, Nvidia’s longtime and well-respected CFO, as Interim CEO. In addition, the Board announced it was initiating a search for a permanent Chief Executive, and we have since retained Heidrick & Struggles to lead the search.

While this was an unexpected transition, the Board acted deliberately and in the best interest of the company’s employees, customers, partners and shareholders. This morning, the company announced that our Board has established a National Security and Export Control Oversight Committee that will include a select group of our independent Board of Directors, senior external advisers and legal counsel. This committee will enhance our engagement with the U.S. government, including all relevant agencies. We will not be addressing or taking questions on today’s call regarding the circumstances of Jensen’s resignation and the ongoing investigation. Our Board will communicate further when appropriate.

The headline was that first paragraph, particularly the declaration that the board “does not intend to reinstate Jensen as CEO”; this came hours after Huang released a statement ( https://x.com/JensenHuang/status/2122726005219737649 ) expressing interest in returning:

Since my departure, I’ve seen an outpouring of calls from Nvidia’s employees, investors, business partners, and customers for me to return as CEO. Many continue to reach out to me directly as the company navigates complex technology, market, competitive, and geopolitical dynamics. Nvidia is a once-in-a-generation company. If the Board of Directors decides that my return is in the best interest of our shareholders, our employees, the company, and America, I would be honored to answer these calls and return as CEO.

Semafor reported ( https://www.semafor.com/article/11/17/2026/some-nvidia-directors-push-to-bring-huang-back-despite-board-opposition ) that some members of the board do favor Huang’s return, but Jones’ statement was pretty unequivocal; I suspect the reason had to do with that second paragraph. Specifically, Jones tied Huang not returning to Nvidia’s board establishing a “National Security and Export Control Oversight Committee”. What seems quite clear to me is that Nvidia’s board received some sort of ultimatum: this isn’t a “we can’t rehire Huang because he broke the company’s code of conduct” situation; it’s a “we will be in serious trouble with the powers that be if we rehire Huang” reality.

There was a striking article in the Wall Street Journal ( https://www.wsj.com/tech/jensen-huang-backdoor-ai-data-centers-a5e4499d ) on Sunday, entitled “How Jensen Huang Built the Backdoor Route That Keeps Beijing in the AI Race”; it’s about data centers in Malaysia, and this is how it opens:

An hour outside of the Malaysian capital, Bangladeshi workers this past week were driving dump trucks and excavators at a site stretching across 117 football fields. It was a world away from the Silicon Valley boardroom where, hours later, Chief Executive Jensen Huang would be ousted. Yet this site in Southeast Asia and others like it are at the heart of the Huang affair.

Here, a Chinese company plans to use Nvidia’s advanced artificial-intelligence chips. Thanks to Chinese customers, data-center industry leaders say Malaysia could become the second-largest data center hub in the world, after Virginia, packed with top-of-the-line Nvidia chips China’s leading AI companies can use to challenge American dominance.

Huang built a multibillion-dollar business to feed the ravenous appetite of Chinese customers for American chips, cultivating a network of Asian partners who could serve the Chinese far from Washington. He made a friend of President Trump and lobbied Washington hard to keep the backdoor to China open. China jumped to take advantage of the avenues Trump didn’t close. Its AI developers exploited the Nvidia chips they desperately needed to catch up with America’s leaders, either using smuggled chips or accessing them remotely in places like Malaysia.

The second part of this story was on Monday; from CNBC ( https://www.nbcnews.com/tech/tech-news/us-closes-loophole-let-chinese-companies-use-top-ai-chips-rcna609634 ) :

The Commerce Department moved to further restrict the sale of top-tier American AI chips Monday morning, addressing mounting concerns about how companies in China and other countries might evade American export restrictions by taking advantage of longstanding loopholes.

As part of a new rule, data center customers in Indonesia, Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand and Brazil must now receive a special license from the Commerce Department in order to buy advanced AI chips from American companies such as Nvidia or AMD. Over the past several years, businesses in those countries have bought billions of dollars worth of high-end chips, raising concerns that some chips might be illegally smuggled into China.

The rule, issued by the department’s Bureau of Industry and Security, will also require existing overseas data center customers to receive a similar license if they provide “significant volumes” of computing power to companies whose headquarters are in China, Russia, Cuba or other countries banned from receiving certain types of advanced American technology. Companies in these countries have allowed Chinese businesses to access their most advanced AI chips remotely, skirting rules that prevented Chinese companies from accessing these chips on Chinese territory.

Notice the days: on Sunday the Wall Street Journal wrote about a practice — selling chips to data centers in Southeast Asia that were used remotely by Chinese companies — as if it were a crime, and that practice only required a license as of Monday. Which, to bring you all back to kindergarten, is after Sunday. That’s the rub, though: Huang’s ultimate crime was in not abiding by the spirit of the chip ban; more than that, in fact, he fought vociferously against it, and did little if anything to help enforce it. That what Nvidia did may very well have been legal was not, for those powers that be, a defense; if anything, it made his demise all the more necessary. And, by extension, it’s why I’m very doubtful he’ll be coming back.

Office Convergence
------------------

From Ars Technica ( https://arstechnica.com/gadgets/2026/11/microsoft-calls-copilot-the-new-os-for-work-at-ignite-keynote/ ) :

Opening the keynote at its Ignite event, Microsoft CEO Satya Nadella outlined the company’s vision for its new “frontier ecosystem,” announcing Tuesday the general availability of Word, Excel, and PowerPoint inside Copilot, which he described as “the new OS for work.”

The announcement is a striking inversion of how we’ve traditionally used software. Rather than an app like Word being the attraction and Clippy the assistant to help you use it, Microsoft envisions the new Copilot integrations will see agents driving things, with the software packages riding shotgun. Applications will become plugin components of the agent’s work, backed by an organizational knowledge layer that draws context from emails, meetings, calendars, chats, and files. Users will now be able to create documents, spreadsheets, and presentations directly in Copilot, with the resulting files remaining editable in their respective Office applications.

Meanwhile, last week, from Bloomberg ( https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-10/anthropic-unveils-office-tools-ahead-of-ipo-taking-on-microsoft ) :

Anthropic PBC is releasing a suite of tools that weave the company’s artificial intelligence capabilities into more everyday office work, a move that puts it in sharper competition with two of its backers: Microsoft Corp. and Alphabet Inc.’s Google.

The features, set to be unveiled at a company event on Tuesday, include a video call assistant, a data dashboard tool and a spreadsheet service called Claude Sheets that closely resembles Microsoft Excel and Google Sheets, but with Anthropic’s AI built into it. Many of the new tools are available in a beta format for paid users.

Anthropic has been working to bolster adoption of its services among a broader mix of business professionals as it nears a landmark public listing later this month. The Claude maker has previously seen strong demand among software engineers for using its tools to streamline the process of writing and debugging code.

OS Wars, AI Edition
-------------------

And yet, as absurd as this dichotomy may seem, you can make the case that dueling spreadsheets is actually the bigger deal. Microsoft CEO Satya Nadella was explicit about what Microsoft was doing:

VideoPress Video Player ( https://videopress.com/embed/faUeFiFN )

You know, Copilot is the new operating system for work. It brings together all of the form factors of human-computer interaction, whether it’s chat or co-work or code or autopilot, all into one experience. It also brings together the disparate categories that we have all referenced, knowledge work, coding, business process applications. Apps are just plug-ins to co-pilot. That’s why we, in fact, have brought all of Office, all of its richness, right built into Copilot.

And underlying this is a model system that matches the right intelligence to the right context and the right form factor to accomplish any task that you may have. You know, Home gives you one place to start, whether you need a quick answer or want to really complete a complex multi-step task. And Code brings you an infinite SaaS factory to every individual inside the organization

If there is one thing that has characterized the incumbent response to AI, it is the extent to which they have completely thrown their erstwhile partners under the bus. The most obvious example is Google, which has basically stopped sending people to websites; Meta’s big bet is Muse, a personal agent who you can talk to instead of your friends and family. Microsoft, meanwhile, is happily reducing apps to mere plug-ins, and bragging about how they are enabling every employee to commoditize the SaaS ecosystem.

And, I must say, all of the strategies make perfect sense. It is very obvious, to me anyways, that the best way to work is with an agent. To the extent you need artifacts of your work, just have your agent make them; “Artifacts”, notably enough, is exactly what Anthropic calls these features ( https://claude.com/resources/articles/more-ways-to-work-with-claude-sheets-whiteboards-and-interactive-connectors ) , and as for apps:

Starting today, partners can embed their apps directly in Claude, so you can work in Adobe, Atlassian, and others without switching tabs. Plugins from Salesforce (beta), NetSuite, and Workday (beta) come with built-in skills, so sellers can prep for a call, accountants can analyze financial data, and HR teams can handle time off and expenses.

This is, clear as can be, an operating system battle. Operating systems are the layer between the hardware and the software, the chokepoint where the most value accrues. Anthropic’s bet is that its hardware — their model — gives them the edge; Microsoft’s bet is that no one wants to be locked into one model, so they will abstract it away. Both have already accepted the reality that in this fight, mere apps are plugins, lucky enough to be invited to the fight for the enterprise.
