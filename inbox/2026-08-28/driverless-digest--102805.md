An op-ed by Waymo arguing that cities should create a formal process for “staging” - letting AVs park at the curb between rides instead of continuously circling.
Because AVs spend much of their time providing trips, the cumulative value generated by staging far exceeds that of a curb space occupied by a privately owned vehicle. For a commercial district, this means that local businesses could receive eight to 10 times as many potential customer visits than if the space were designated for private vehicle storage only.
Op-Ed: San Francisco Should Not Give Free Curb Space to Waymo (link [ https://substack.com/redirect/3f970277-e927-45bf-921c-c090eaa15888?j=eyJ1IjoiMXlnZjcifQ.KOUIeBfeCRzhIwFnKXyQigR4j0VpF-SZzascYhSZunw ]). A rebuttal to Waymo’s op-ed was also published.
I don’t see how an empty Waymo idling outside a storefront generates more value than a parked private car, whose owner at least has some chance of being a customer. The argument rests entirely on total curb turnover, as more vehicles cycling through a spot, in theory, means more potential trip origins and destinations nearby. But that’s a claim about vehicle throughput, not foot traffic to any specific business the AV happens to be parked in front of.
This isn’t a new problem and based on my experience with ride-hail drivers, cities have never done a great job figuring out where Uber and Lyft drivers should wait between trips, especially because there is often nowhere to park in the busiest places and at the busiest times. That means both human drivers and AVs end up driving around empty while waiting for their next trip. Is it better for them to park instead? Probably, but Waymo or anyone else should have to pay for the right to use that space. That is why we have parking meters in busy commercial areas.
At the same time, if curb prices are too high, driving around could become the cheaper option for Waymo, even though it creates more congestion. The answer is complicated but probably a combination of paid staging, fees for empty miles or congestion, and greater use of private parking lots for off-street staging. Any dedicated curb spaces should also be shared among AVs, taxis, and human ride-hail drivers rather than reserved exclusively for Waymo.
