Cyber Thievery in Cranbury, NJ
A small New Jersey town got tricked into wiring $26M to cybercriminals. Here are the facts:
The town of Cranbury, NJ was in the process of buying land for an affordable housing project when it received fraudulent wiring instructions and sent the entire $26M to the wrong account on September 28th.
Fun fact: this is the town where George Washington and Alexander Hamilton met in 1778 before the Battle of Monmouth.
Police moved quickly enough to freeze $17.3M before the thieves could steal the rest of it, which means Cranbury should get most of the money back. But ~$9M is still missing, and town officials say they don’t yet know how much of that could be covered by their $5M insurance policy.
The weird part is that residents didn’t find out about any of this until November 6, three days after a local election. Police Chief Giuseppe DeChiara said it was intentional because going public earlier could have tipped off the thieves and made it harder to recover the money. Some residents, including Carol Haring, the Republican candidate in that race who lost by 27 points, aren’t buying that explanation and think the timing looks political.
“We’re expected to believe that it’s a coincidence that you suddenly felt comfortable releasing this information at 3 p.m. last Friday afternoon, right after the election?”
Catherine Colaizzo, a 66-year-old chiropractor, summed up the whole thing best: “I can’t even get on my iCloud account without a two-factor identification. I don’t know how you would transfer $26 million.”
