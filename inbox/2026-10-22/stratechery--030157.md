Moffett’s job has, over the last few months, become particularly fascinating thanks to one Elon Musk and SpaceX. I thought that Moffett and his team had some of the best coverage of SpaceX’s S-1: Moffett, befitting his telecoms experience and reputation for starting with the math, is skeptical of many of SpaceX’s more outlandish claims, particularly when it comes to Starlink. At the same time, that skepticism is leavened with a real appreciation of option value, and the reality that we very well may not yet know what the company will ultimately be capable of.

We get into all of that in this interview, including why launch capacity is what matters most, SpaceX’s search for nails, the difference between broadband and mobile service, and whether or not SpaceX should buy a traditional carrier. Then, at the end, we go back to the dot com era, where Moffett accurately predicted — based on math — exactly how the bubble would burst. How is he thinking about the current era, and why does he think that so many of the numbers people are focused on is just noise?

All those were real revenues — some of them weren’t, but most of those were real revenues. But that’s not important. I don’t really care what Nvidia is getting paid by some other company for its GPUs. I don’t care what a neocloud — what CoreWeave is being paid.

All I care about is, are the revenues coming into the system — which means coming into Anthropic, coming into ChatGPT — and it can be enterprise, it can be government, and it doesn’t have to be today, it can be the projected revenues coming into the system are greater than the costs of building the whole value chain? Ignore all the revenues going on between layers of the value chain, because that’s just noise, all that really matters is enough money coming into the system to support the system?

I think the question right now — it’s not that these aren’t amazing products, and it’s not that they aren’t going to be transformational to the world, it’s, “Is there going to be the price realization that allows them to be profitable enough that it supports the rest of the value chain?”. That’s why the market is panicking right now, because the market is saying, “We don’t know”, it’s not obvious.

*What do you think?*

*CM:* I think my suspicion is that these are businesses where, if you’re doing something at the bleeding edge of research — like I’m doing protein folding for some next drug compound — you may absolutely need the best model. But if you’re saying, for my enterprise I want to transform customer service, I don’t need the best model, I’m going to use a cheaper model that’s two or three generations old, and I think there’s going to be a lot of price deflation.

You’re already seeing it with DeepSeek at the cost-per-token — or the cost-per-answer, I guess, is really what matters in open-source models — that’s one-eighth or one-twelfth of frontier models. *So the risk is, “Is there enough revenue?” — as opposed to, how do you balance that versus, “Is there enough capital?”. Do we just hit a canyon ( https://dithering.passport.online/member/episode/nvidia-buys-hugging-face ) ?*

*CM:* Well, there’s that too. Even if there is enough revenue, then the question is, “Is there enough capital?”, but I guess I’m of the mind that if the revenue is there, there’s usually capital for it, and if there isn’t capital for it, then it will be capacity-constrained and the price will be very high.

*Wasn’t the problem that the revenue — even in the most optimistic scenario — is going to take time, and the capital need is now, and there’s just a timing mismatch?*

*CM:* By the way, we won’t know definitively the answer about whether the future revenue cash streams will be sufficient to support the CapEx, and so we’re going in with our blindfold on and just wishing for the best.

*Have we ever actually paid off all the fiber? Even though it’s all used.*

*CM:* Well, a lot of it went bankrupt, there were a lot of bankruptcies.

*That’s what I mean. To your point, let’s ignore all the middle noise. Let’s just take telecoms as a whole — has telecoms ever actually paid back all the investment that went in in the 90s?*

*CM:* That’s a good question. I would imagine probably yes by now, but it’s not obvious.

*But barely.*

*CM:* Yeah, you probably have to go back and check.
