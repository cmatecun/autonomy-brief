Nvidia Backs OpenAI Data Center
-------------------------------

From the Wall Street Journal ( https://www.wsj.com/articles/openai-locks-in-lease-for-huge-data-center-in-ohio-with-backing-from-nvidia-7474bb9c ) :

OpenAI has signed a 10-gigawatt data-center deal in Ohio with SoftBank’s SB Energy that is partly backed by Nvidia, a project expected to become one of the largest artificial-intelligence hubs to date. The deal is backed by a commitment from Nvidia aimed at helping the developer raise debt financing without exposing the chip maker to a substantial amount of risk, people familiar with the matter said.

Nvidia has agreed to backstop a portion of the value of the completed data center at the site, starting with the first phase of the project, which represents around 5 gigawatts of power. In return for its commitment, Nvidia will be the exclusive chip provider for the first half of the Ohio site and take an equity stake in SB Energy, the people said. Nvidia has the option to expand its commitment later on to the full campus.

OpenAI’s 20-year lease, parts of which were announced Monday, is the latest complex transaction aimed at helping an AI firm secure an enormous amount of computing capacity. Chip firms including Nvidia and Google have increasingly agreed to use their robust balance sheets to help startups such as OpenAI and Anthropic sign leases that developers can raise debt against while trying to manage their own risk.

This news is very much in line with what I wrote about last week in Nvidia’s Risky Business ( https://stratechery.com/2026/nvidias-risky-business/?access_token=eyJhbGciOiJSUzI1NiIsImtpZCI6InN0cmF0ZWNoZXJ5LnBhc3Nwb3J0Lm9ubGluZSIsInR5cCI6IkpXVCJ9.eyJhdWQiOiJzdHJhdGVjaGVyeS5wYXNzcG9ydC5vbmxpbmUiLCJhenAiOiJIS0xjUzREd1Nod1AyWURLYmZQV00xIiwiZW50Ijp7InVyaSI6WyJodHRwczovL3N0cmF0ZWNoZXJ5LmNvbS8yMDI2L252aWRpYXMtcmlza3ktYnVzaW5lc3MvIl19LCJleHAiOjE3ODk2Mzk0NDIsImlhdCI6MTc4NzA0NzQ0MiwiaXNzIjoiaHR0cHM6Ly9hcHAucGFzc3BvcnQub25saW5lL29hdXRoIiwic2NvcGUiOiJmZWVkOnJlYWQgYXJ0aWNsZTpyZWFkIGFzc2V0OnJlYWQgY2F0ZWdvcnk6cmVhZCBlbnRpdGxlbWVudHMiLCJzdWIiOiIwMTk1MTZmMS1hMzQ2LTc2MTQtOWU5Ni1iNjNiZDExZmU4NTYiLCJ1c2UiOiJhY2Nlc3MifQ.mrbywOUtmhG-CL5hfCtjc9dLMyGswIv41eMV1xV7gBGAeOLx0svwXBKPVBPRNVhlyc0ju_kDSssffMepIQ8ifC88YqajHUuWG-5C9CwJEnnVw_4oxJkFxgsjAfXyWsaefaK2qA1Tgz6QMQdCIs-04MuJAjcA6N_BSq_i9iMFvwHo4s-XMGW7A7PYUdHthgb1qdE0_9k_Sn1_ilHJKRHZOV1sGO9_k-XQaHnUliP1h5Cb6-uSiZ_yelUGFpSdyuwFAQZwmRFhwtiSKsbxIOVzUigMgzG2vxMfbt6hrfoi3uChgspalPR1fF01WLuKej5cRoFgy0amlss1jMarXLCbjQ ) : Nvidia is taking on risk to lower OpenAI’s cost of capital so that they can finance Nvidia chips (and not buy chips from Amazon or Google). That is, in terms of long-term expected value, a de facto price cut; it’s just manifesting in the form of taking on risk as opposed to actually charging lower prices.

This deal was rumored in late July, when it was reported ( https://www.wsj.com/tech/ai/nvidia-in-talks-with-openai-to-guarantee-250-billion-financing-for-data-center-3dd6eae3 ) that Nvidia would backstop the entire project; the market wasn’t too happy about that news, and in this reworking Nvidia is only backstopping the data center portion, not the chips. Nvidia is making a $1.5 billion equity investment into SB Energy; one suspects this is part of an agreement for Softbank to take on more of the chip risk.

One additional point that might not have been totally clear in last week’s Article: I think that Nvidia’s actions are perfectly rational, even if they do have the effect of expanding the potential blast radius for a bubble burst. There is a scenario where “it’s different this time”, specifically, the scenario where AI-derived revenues expand very quickly such that they not only obviate the risk of these build-outs, and not only pay down the debt being taken on to build and equip data centers, but also fund future capex investment. And, in that world, it is very much in Nvidia’s interest to not only have data centers ready to receive its chips, but to ensure that the companies driving that revenue remain committed Nvidia customers. And, to take this full circle, Nvidia’s investments make it more likely the upside scenario is the one that wins out. No risk, no reward.

Anthropic News
--------------

To that end, the latest news on Anthropic’s revenue numbers is notable; from Bloomberg ( https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-17/anthropic-revenue-run-rate-surpasses-65-billion-ahead-of-ipo ) :

Anthropic PBC is on track to generate annualized revenue of more than $65 billion based on its current performance, according to people familiar with the matter, up more than sevenfold from its pace at the end of last year. The company’s run rate, a metric that projects full-year revenue from a shorter period, hit $65 billion by the end of July, said the people, who spoke on condition of anonymity to discuss private information. Anthropic shared the figures as part of a regular update with investors, one person said. The dramatic acceleration in revenue bolsters Anthropic’s plans for a public listing. Anthropic and OpenAI have filed confidential paperwork to go public, with Anthropic expected to make its Wall Street debut as soon as this fall, ahead of OpenAI.

On one hand, this number is staggering, no matter how it is calculated (i.e. Anthropic reportedly uses gross revenue from hyperscalers, we don’t know what period the run rate was calculated from, etc.). Assuming the calculations are at least consistent, the trajectory is incredible…but also growth is slowing a tad. Between April and May reports suggested Anthropic’s run rate increased by about $17 billion; now the latest news is an increase of $18 billion between May and July. Again, we really have no idea what these numbers mean, or the specific dates they were calculated on, but if there is any sort of consistency, the trends are amazing, but not perfect.

One more piece of Anthropic news: Bloomberg reported last week ( https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-13/anthropic-said-in-talks-to-buy-ai-startup-decart-for-6-billion ) that the company was in talks to buy Decart AI, an Israeli startup focused on world models. That would be an interesting departure for Anthropic, which has been resolutely focused on text and code, but may be necessary for AGI.

The more pertinent fact about Decart, however, is its Decart Optimization Engine ( https://decart.ai/optimization-engine ) , which they claim derives massive efficiency gains in both training and inference. This actually makes perfect sense for Anthropic: unlike OpenAI, which had to deal with the ChatGPT onslaught years ago, Anthropic has only recently had to deal with crushing demand; they could develop the capabilities to manage that and use their infrastructure more efficiently, but given their timelines it makes a ton of sense to pay for that expertise right now.

Google Buys Spirit Airlines Data
--------------------------------

From Bloomberg Law ( https://news.bloomberglaw.com/business-and-practice/google-aims-to-boost-ai-with-purchase-of-spirit-airlines-data ) :

Google LLC won a bankruptcy auction for a trove of deindentified business data, software code, and operations records from collapsed low-cost carrier Spirit Aviation Holdings Inc., saying it plans to use the assets to improve its artificial intelligence. The tech giant’s $10 million bid includes a vast repository of the defunct airline’s data, including 100 million emails, 500 million Microsoft Teams chats and collaboration records, plus information related to revenue, aircraft operations, employee productivity, and audits and fraud, according to an Aug. 14 notice in the US Bankruptcy Court for the Southern District of New York.

Google’s purchase doesn’t include personal data and privileged materials like Spirit’s 97.5 million passenger profiles or an estimated 50.2 million records related to the Free Spirit loyalty program data, according to court records. The data will also go through a process to ensure that it can’t be associated with particular customers, according to the court filing.

This isn’t totally unprecedented: Google beat out Mercor for the data; The Information reported last week ( https://www.theinformation.com/articles/startups-find-old-slack-threads-tickets-suddenly-high-demand ) that Mercor (a data provider for AI companies) was making offers to failed startups for similar data. Spirit Airlines is unique, however, in that its data goes back 26 years.

That argument still holds; if anything the cost and complexity of training only makes the factory label more appropriate, given the transformation that needs to happen to data to make it useful (and, notably, AI factories is exactly what Huang says Nvidia is building).

At the same time, Levie isn’t wrong to invoke this analogy: no one was paying $10 million for a bankrupt companies Microsoft Teams messages back in 2018; data has value for data’s sake in a way it never has before, and it doesn’t really matter what data it is. That sounds like data is fungible and intrinsically valuable — kind of like oil. I’ll grant it.
