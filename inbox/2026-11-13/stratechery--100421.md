* *The Shocking Demise of Jensen Huang.* This week’s coverage was dominated, unsurprisingly, by the shocking and sudden end of Jensen Huang’s 32 year tenure as CEO of Nvidia. The end came on Thursday morning, and just a few hours later, we recorded a Sharp Tech episode recounting Huang’s legendary history, figuring out what happened, and looked forward to what this might mean for Nvidia, the tech industry, and America going forward. Also see Andrew’s view from DC, Jensen Huang Was Always Going to Lose ( https://sharptext.net/2026/jensen-huang-was-always-going-to-lose/ ) , and listen to Wednesday’s episode of Sharp China ( https://sharpchina.fm/member/episode/jensen-huang-likely-out-at-nvidia-the-megaspeed-maelstrom-in-d-c-apec-in-seven-days-legislation-that-may-come-next ) about the geopolitical angle, and Thursday’s Dithering ( https://dithering.passport.online/member/episode/jensen-fired ) about both the parallels and differences between Steve Jobs and Apple. — Ben Thompson

* *Enterprise, Consumer and Intel.* The more things change, the more they stay the same: Intel remains on the back foot in the AI era, and the foundry dreams continue to flounder. On the heels of disappointing earnings and 2027 guidance that promises yet more letdowns, Ben’s Update on Wednesday ( https://stratechery.com/2026/intel-earnings-amd-earnings-intel-and-amds-divergence/ ) examined the state of America’s would-be national champion by drawing comparisons to AMD, an Intel peer that’s red-hot right now. To understand why one company is capitalizing on skyrocketing enterprise demand while the other confronts flatlining consumer sales, the answer starts with chips chiplets and leads to more profound questions about culture, Intel’s nature, and its bifurcated strategy for the long-term (and, for subscribers, look forward to more on this topic in Sunday’s Sharp Tech mailbag episode). — Andrew Sharp
* *And Then There’s Professional Basketball.* As a palate cleanser after a week of Jensen drama, this week’s Greatest of All Talk ( https://goat.passport.online/member/episode/agony-and-ecstasy-after-three-weeks-the-earn-their-ice-all-stars-difficult-conversations-about-aj-and-season-ticket-holder-decorum ) was a look at the top and bottom of the NBA after the first three weeks. On the euphoric side of the ledger we have Warriors fans (pre-Steph injury), Lakers fans, Spurs fans, and on the other end spectrum there is nothing positive to say in Brooklyn, a familiar malaise in Cleveland, and precisely one silver lining in Salt Lake City. All of it was a great time; we also hit Adam’s experience with the NBA in Mexico City last weekend, the hardest working players in NBA history, and an anxiety-inducing conversation about the number one pick, AJ Dybantsa. — AS

Filling out a web form is not a value-add activity for anyone. No one chooses to fill out web forms in their spare time. That’s why these agents have been particularly useful.

I’ve written ( https://stratechery.com/2026/apps-agents-and-aggregation/ ) about how these platforms are giving ordinary people an AI agent with a computer, enabling agents to do genuinely useful things on our behalf.  When one of those things is spending money, it needs a wallet that knows what it is, and isn’t, allowed to do.

Stripe has learned that’s really about deciding how much freedom to give it. You might approve one purchase, or give it $500 for flights this week, and let it figure out the rest. The wallet’s job is to keep it within those lines while handling everything between “go buy this” and the purchase actually happening.

That’s what Stripe is building with Link: You approve what it spends, it can come back to you if the final price changes, and eligible purchases can even come with protection if the agent makes a mistake.
