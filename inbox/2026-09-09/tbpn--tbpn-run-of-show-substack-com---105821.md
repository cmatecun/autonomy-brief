Steve Jobs Would Have Loved AI, and Been Hated for It
Steve Jobs predicted ChatGPT in 1985, it’s an awesome video:
Here’s the transcript:
Do you know who Alexander the Great’s tutor was for about 14 years? You know, right? Aristotle. When I read this, I became immensely jealous. I think I would have enjoyed that a great deal. Through the miracle of the printed page, I can at least read what Aristotle wrote without an intermediary. And maybe if there’s a professor, they can add to that. But at least I can go directly to the source material. And that is, of course, the foundation upon which our Western civilization is built. But I can’t ask Aristotle a question. I mean, I can, but I won’t get an answer. And so my hope is that, in our lifetimes, we can make a tool of a new kind—of an interactive kind. My hope is someday, when the next Aristotle is alive, we can capture the underlying worldview of that Aristotle in a computer. And someday, some student will be able to not only read the words Aristotle wrote, but ask Aristotle a question and get an answer. That’s what I hope that we can do.
Steve Jobs is perhaps the most famous techno optimist, his “bicycle for the mind” quote is still repeated constantly, and you could see his excitement whenever he gave a keynote about a new creative tool launched by Apple. Particularly funny to watch him just chilling on a stool jamming on GarageBand with John Mayer [ https://substack.com/redirect/bc54810e-2eba-4c07-a394-899990419fbe?j=eyJ1IjoiMXlnZjcifQ.KOUIeBfeCRzhIwFnKXyQigR4j0VpF-SZzascYhSZunw ]: 
And thanks to modern AI, you can now ask (an LLM version of) Steve Jobs, or Aristotle, or Alexander the Great what they would think about AI.
Here’s the Steve Jobs answer from Astra.
And here’s Aristotle:
I like these answers, and I like to imagine a version of the world where Steve Jobs is inspiring Americans with a positive view of what artificial intelligence will do for the future. But I’m not sure that’s how it would play out. Because of political polarization, economic stagnation, wealth inequality, negativity bias on social media, and a host of other factors, I believe the tide would have shifted against Jobs in this imagined alternate reality. Not to say that the vision would be incorrect. Giving people new abilities is awesome, making things that are excellent is more important than ever, but the urge to attack is ineluctable and we’d probably get another round of biopics that take an even harsher view of his legacy.
Nevertheless, it’s unacceptable to back down, simply because you aren’t being properly lionized. Everyone must still channel Aristotle and take whatever steps they can to improve human flourishing. Resist the idea that a life requiring less human effort is automatically a better life. Keep working.
