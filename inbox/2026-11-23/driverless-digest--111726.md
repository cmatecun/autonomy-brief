In today’s episode, I’m speaking with Greg Rogers [ https://substack.com/redirect/2551fedb-ef7a-46b7-a1d7-2274d5843d9e?j=eyJ1IjoiMXlnZjcifQ.KOUIeBfeCRzhIwFnKXyQigR4j0VpF-SZzascYhSZunw ], former Grassroots & Policy Campaigns Lead at Waymo and founder of The Innovation Majority Institute [ https://substack.com/redirect/bc1aab8c-5166-4965-be9e-be505e1f9dd8?j=eyJ1IjoiMXlnZjcifQ.KOUIeBfeCRzhIwFnKXyQigR4j0VpF-SZzascYhSZunw ]. Greg shares how he got his start in AV policy while driving for Uber and Lyft, and how much the industry has changed since those early days.
We dive into why D.C.’s proposed AV bill stalled in committee, even after lawmakers added a cap on robotaxis and wheelchair accessibility requirements. Greg walks me through how a proposed per-mile fee would have helped fund public transit and support workers. We also discuss who pushed back on the bill and why I think its failure was embarrassing for D.C.
We talk about what robotaxis could mean for rideshare drivers, how the new fleet jobs compare to driving for Uber and Lyft, and a provision that would have let rideshare drivers organize. I also ask Greg what he’d say to a D.C. resident who doesn’t follow AVs.
Finally, we discuss why the nation’s capital matters so much to AV companies, from the size of its ride-hail market to the lawmakers and visiting leaders who could experience the technology firsthand.
