# Nvidia Reinstates Jensen Huang as CEO

**From:** StrictlyVC <connie@strictlyvc.com>  
**Date:** 2026-11-24T06:45:22.000Z

---

## Top News

**Nvidia**’s board unanimously **reinstated Jensen Huang **as CEO just 11 days after his resignation, reversing its declaration last week that he would not return after receiving input from senior U.S. government officials and intense pressure from Silicon Valley, investors, and the White House. Nvidia shares surged more than 30% on the news. The Wall Street Journal has [more here](https://www.wsj.com/articles/nvidia-board-reinstates-jensen-huang-as-ceo-a6b49914).

**President Trump** said he personally pressured **Nvidia’s board** to reinstate **Jensen Huang** as CEO, telling CNBC, “I made the deal,” and said he gave directors assurances that Huang posed no national security threat. TechCrunch has [more here](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/23/trump-reportedly-called-into-nvidia-board-meeting-to-advocate-for-huangs-return/). 

The New York Times reports that **President Trump **directed **Vice President JD Vance** to help engineer **Jensen Huang’s return to Nvidia**, with Vance securing assurances for the board that the administration did not view Huang as a national security threat and that he was **not facing criminal charges**. The weekend effort culminated in Huang’s reinstatement Monday, sending Nvidia shares up more than 30%. [More here](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/11/23/us/politics/trump-nvidia-huang.html).

The **Commerce Department** plans to place Singapore-based **Megaspeed** – the Nvidia customer at the center of the Jensen Huang scandal – on its Entity List, according to Semafor, **effectively barring U.S. companies** from selling it semiconductors or other restricted technology without a special license. TechCrunch has [more here](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/23/megaspeed-the-company-at-the-center-of-the-jensen-huang-scandal-appears-headed-for-a-u-s-blacklist/).

## Figma Employee Sues, Alleging Company Forced Her to Confront Harassers

View image: (https://media.beehiiv.com/cdn-cgi/image/fit=scale-down,format=auto,onerror=redirect,quality=80/uploads/asset/file/5c765176-312c-472e-bba3-f5f054846ddb/GettyImages-1222551151.jpeg?t=1795491181)
Caption: Image Credits: Japatino / Getty Images

**By Dominic-Madori Davis**

A Figma engineer is suing the design software company and its co-founder and CEO, Dylan Field, over allegations that she faced sexual harassment and retaliation while managers and HR repeatedly told her to confront the very people she accused of misconduct.  

The lawsuit, filed Monday in U.S. District Court for the Southern District of New York, alleges that Figma’s culture of “direct feedback” led managers and HR to make engineer Serry Park address harassment herself rather than investigate her complaints. Park, who joined Figma in 2022, alleges the company’s response damaged her career, compensation, and mental health. The lawsuit also alleges that Park, who is Asian, faced racial and gender discrimination, including performance criticism based on stereotypes that Asian women are meek, timid, and conflict-averse.

“Figma was my dream company for a long time,” Park told TechCrunch in an interview before the lawsuit was filed. “It was a place where I felt that I could level up in my career, working on a lot of really cool, interesting things at scale.” 

Park is seeking damages, a jury trial, and a court order prohibiting Figma from requiring employees who report harassment to confront their alleged harassers. She also wants the company to adopt and enforce a process for investigating harassment complaints promptly and confidentially.

When reached for comment, a spokesperson for Figma said, “Figma does not tolerate discrimination or harassment. Figma also does not tolerate extortion. These claims have no merit, and we will present the facts in court.”  

### **Faith in the Process**

Park told TechCrunch she initially had faith in Figma’s complaint process, expecting that if she documented the alleged harassment and reported it to the right people, the company would act. But as an engineer in a male-dominated field, Park told TechCrunch, “I don’t feel that they took me seriously throughout this process.”
