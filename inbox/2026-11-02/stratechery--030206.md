Google Earnings
---------------

From the Wall Street Journal ( https://www.wsj.com/tech/ai/alphabet-sheds-half-a-trillion-in-market-value-in-stocks-worst-day-ever-bde7bb6e ) :

Google’s stock suffered its worst day on record Thursday as investors confronted the reality that a company that has long been a dynamo of growth and profits faces painful trade-offs from the artificial-intelligence transition. The slide erased roughly $500 billion from Alphabet’s market value. Google’s 13% decline also weighed on major U.S. stock indexes, fueling a decline in the Nasdaq composite and amplifying fears that the costs of the global AI build-out may be outrunning the returns.

The tech giant reported on Wednesday that its Google Services unit, which includes search advertising, YouTube and subscriptions, generated $96.3 billion, missing Wall Street expectations of $98.5 billion, signaling that Google’s biggest revenue engine is slowing as AI reshapes how consumers search for information and products. Google Search revenue grew 12% year over year in the third quarter, down from 17% growth in the second quarter and 19% in the first quarter. Google also reported greater-than-expected operating expenses and negative free cash flow, highlighting the staggering costs the company is incurring as it races to secure data center capacity and develop new AI models.

The slowdown underscores a challenge for Google: how to remake its cash-cow search business around AI without cannibalizing it. It also spooked Wall Street, which has signed off on Big Tech’s AI spending spree in part because the core businesses at companies like Google, Microsoft and Amazon.com are reliable profit-printing machines.

You can, if you want to be pedantic, blame Google for cannibalizing their business; after all, they are the company that invented the transformer, which undergirds the large language model, which represents the threat to Google’s business that investors have been worried about for four years now. On the other hand, what happened four years ago was not an LLM-based product from Google, but rather OpenAI: indeed, Google was, up to that point, doing an excellent job of not cannibalizing anything!

I was immediately worried about Google ( https://stratechery.com/2023/ai-and-the-big-five/?access_token=eyJhbGciOiJFUzI1NiIsImtpZCI6IkxzanFRcEhoMUJUdmU0djJQUUVuejIiLCJ0eXAiOiJKV1QifQ.eyJhdWQiOiJzdHJhdGVjaGVyeS5wYXNzcG9ydC5vbmxpbmUiLCJhenAiOiJIS0xjUzREd1Nod1AyWURLYmZQV00xIiwiZW50Ijp7InVyaSI6WyJodHRwczovL3N0cmF0ZWNoZXJ5LmNvbS8yMDIzL2FpLWFuZC10aGUtYmlnLWZpdmUvIl19LCJleHAiOjE3OTYyMDkzMjYsImlhdCI6MTc5MzYxNzMyNiwiaXNzIjoiaHR0cHM6Ly9hcHAucGFzc3BvcnQub25saW5lL29hdXRoIiwic2NvcGUiOiJmZWVkOnJlYWQgYXJ0aWNsZTpyZWFkIGFzc2V0OnJlYWQgY2F0ZWdvcnk6cmVhZCBlbnRpdGxlbWVudHMiLCJzdWIiOiIwMTk1MTZmMS1hMzQ2LTc2MTQtOWU5Ni1iNjNiZDExZmU4NTYiLCJ1c2UiOiJhY2Nlc3MifQ.RyA8xPLlT5vXehs8Ztn0bKD5D1Hwyp7CqLUmTr5AS1tSOEAfSVUmxEs2jSxalLY_8myvDI5MrPKE22aAi1pkVw ) , drawing the analogy to Kodak, another company that invented the technology that doomed their cash cow; in fact, however, Google has, until this earnings, defied expectations, and done so not by building a chatbot (yes the Gemini app exists), but rather by transforming search into a chatbot. And you can see why that might work: as long as humans are part of the experience there is an opportunity for advertising.

Facebook Déjà Vu
----------------

There was a real déjà vu moment on the call ( https://abc.xyz/investor/events/event-details/2026/2026-Q3-Earnings-Call-2026-Ujou16CTw9/default.aspx ) , right at the top in CEO Sundar Pichai’s opening remarks:

Let’s turn to Search, which is bringing the benefits of AI to more people than any other product in the world. Just like our shift to mobile over a decade ago, we are focused on building the best Search user experience, followed by monetization.

I immediately wrote in my notes: “That sounds like Facebook.” This is one of CEO Mark Zuckerberg’s favorite tropes when it comes to Meta building new product experiences (I first picked up on it in 2015 ( https://stratechery.com/2015/daily-update-facebooks-earnings-naverline-disappoint/?access_token=eyJhbGciOiJFUzI1NiIsImtpZCI6IkxzanFRcEhoMUJUdmU0djJQUUVuejIiLCJ0eXAiOiJKV1QifQ.eyJhdWQiOiJzdHJhdGVjaGVyeS5wYXNzcG9ydC5vbmxpbmUiLCJhenAiOiJIS0xjUzREd1Nod1AyWURLYmZQV00xIiwiZW50Ijp7InVyaSI6WyJodHRwczovL3N0cmF0ZWNoZXJ5LmNvbS8yMDE1L2RhaWx5LXVwZGF0ZS1mYWNlYm9va3MtZWFybmluZ3MtbmF2ZXJsaW5lLWRpc2FwcG9pbnQvIl19LCJleHAiOjE3OTYyMDkzMjYsImlhdCI6MTc5MzYxNzMyNiwiaXNzIjoiaHR0cHM6Ly9hcHAucGFzc3BvcnQub25saW5lL29hdXRoIiwic2NvcGUiOiJmZWVkOnJlYWQgYXJ0aWNsZTpyZWFkIGFzc2V0OnJlYWQgY2F0ZWdvcnk6cmVhZCBlbnRpdGxlbWVudHMiLCJzdWIiOiIwMTk1MTZmMS1hMzQ2LTc2MTQtOWU5Ni1iNjNiZDExZmU4NTYiLCJ1c2UiOiJhY2Nlc3MifQ.PvRcDCGjsU_8Vf-VV4u0tB9VOivRlgmvUtwdgymy8i-X4q6eE6UH3Bh-RKYdUl2YIPm9mN5yU8EiT8nr5iQhcA ) ): first get the experience right, then grow engagement with the experience, and only then monetize it. One particularly succinct Zuckerberg articulation of this concept was on the Q3 2018 earnings call ( https://www.roic.ai/quote/META/transcripts/2018-year/3-quarter ) :

We’re following our normal playbook here of building out the best consumer products first and focusing on succeeding there before ramping up ads.

What makes this notable is the broader context; here’s the full quote:

On Stories, we are even better-positioned. People now share more than 1 billion stories every day. We lead in almost every country. There are a couple of reasons we’ve focused on building Stories in all of our apps. First, I just think that this is the future. People want to share in ways that don’t stick around permanently, and I want to make sure that we fully embrace this. Second, Stories is a medium like feeds that can feel very different in very different contexts. So just like most major social apps have feeds, including Pinterest, Twitter, or LinkedIn, but you wouldn’t say that those services do the same things, I think many services will have Stories in the future too, but will serve different functions.

Now, while this effort is going well, we’re also working through a couple of challenges here. One is that while WhatsApp Status and Instagram Stories immediately took off and have been huge successes, Facebook Stories started off slower. It’s now growing quickly and I think we’ll be in a better position soon, but our effort to shift Facebook from News Feed first to Stories first hasn’t been as smooth as I had hoped. But this is important for the Facebook community long term. Another challenge is that we’re earlier in developing our ads products for Stories, so we don’t make as much money from them yet as we do from feed ads. We’re following our normal playbook here of building out the best consumer products first and focusing on succeeding there before ramping up ads.

I’m optimistic that we’ll get ads in Stories to perform as well as feed over time, and that the opportunity will be even bigger because it looks like Stories will be a bigger medium than feed has been. But I want to be up front that even assuming that we get to where we want to go from a feed-only world to a feed plus Stories world, it will take some time and our revenue growth may be slower during that period like it was while transitioning our products to mobile.

That earnings call was the first one after Meta experienced the then-largest one-day loss by any company in U.S. stock market history ( https://stratechery.com/2018/facebook-lenses/?access_token=eyJhbGciOiJFUzI1NiIsImtpZCI6IkxzanFRcEhoMUJUdmU0djJQUUVuejIiLCJ0eXAiOiJKV1QifQ.eyJhdWQiOiJzdHJhdGVjaGVyeS5wYXNzcG9ydC5vbmxpbmUiLCJhenAiOiJIS0xjUzREd1Nod1AyWURLYmZQV00xIiwiZW50Ijp7InVyaSI6WyJodHRwczovL3N0cmF0ZWNoZXJ5LmNvbS8yMDE4L2ZhY2Vib29rLWxlbnNlcy8iXX0sImV4cCI6MTc5NjIwOTMyNiwiaWF0IjoxNzkzNjE3MzI2LCJpc3MiOiJodHRwczovL2FwcC5wYXNzcG9ydC5vbmxpbmUvb2F1dGgiLCJzY29wZSI6ImZlZWQ6cmVhZCBhcnRpY2xlOnJlYWQgYXNzZXQ6cmVhZCBjYXRlZ29yeTpyZWFkIGVudGl0bGVtZW50cyIsInN1YiI6IjAxOTUxNmYxLWEzNDYtNzYxNC05ZTk2LWI2M2JkMTFmZTg1NiIsInVzZSI6ImFjY2VzcyJ9.YWR1FRRWH0vYTDyRSIw58k13_ln-LNSkHTT3f9F_TS0KrfE4z738nNykwsp-1cr6cddzLL8vCHMwwHgxBzJTuA ) due to disappointing revenue growth in their core ads product; that decline, by the way, would be superseded four years later by Meta itself, thanks once again to lower-than-expected growth. Zuckerberg said on the Q3 2022 earnings call ( https://s21.q4cdn.com/399680738/files/doc_financials/2022/q3/Meta-Q3-2022-Earnings-Call-Transcript.pdf ) :

Moving to monetization, I’ve discussed in the past how the growth of short-form video creates nearterm challenges since Reels doesn’t monetize at the rate of Feed or Stories yet. That means as Reels grows, we’re displacing revenue from higher-monetizing surfaces. I think this is clearly the right thing to do so that Reels can grow with the demand we’re seeing, but closing this gap is also a high priority. Even with the progress we’ve made, we’re still choosing to take a more than $500 million quarterly revenue headwind with this shift. But we expect to get to a more neutral place over the next 12-18 months. I mentioned last quarter that Instagram Reels had crossed $1 billion annual revenue run rate. We continue scaling monetization across both Instagram and Facebook, and the combined run rate across these apps is now $3 billion.

Notice what happened: in 2018 Stories wasn’t monetizing as well as Feed; in 2022 Reels wasn’t monetizing as well as Stories. The shift was that Stories had in the ensuing four years started monetizing extremely well and — in case you didn’t realize — it turns out that Reels monetized extremely well too!

Pichai and Chief Business Officer Philipp Schindler made the case that this was the way to think about Google Search and its transition to AI Mode. Schindler said in his prepared remarks:

On AI Mode, many of these queries are upper‑funnel queries, allowing us to capture new high‑value commercial intent. In addition, the majority of AI Mode queries are incremental, creating more monetization opportunities over time. Additionally, our user research shows that people are comfortable with ads on these surfaces and find them highly relevant. Click‑through rates are already similar between sponsored and organic product list items within AI Mode.

Based on these collective results, we are now scaling high‑quality ads within AI Overviews and AI Mode to more users and queries. This includes taking our monetization footprint global for the first time. Today we are announcing the expansion of ads in AI Mode into international markets, alongside multi‑turn ads in AI Mode in the U.S. As always, our approach is deliberate — prioritizing the user experience and engagement first, and then scaling the formats that drive proven ROI for advertisers. That discipline is what gives us a strong, durable foundation for the future.

Schindler added in the Q&A that AI Mode was — along with currency exchange and vertical-specific dynamics like the end of the de minimis exception in the EU — responsible for the search miss:

To the next part of your question, it’s worth noting that the strong growth in AI Mode queries had an impact because of the lower monetization we were seeing in AI Mode. To be clear, we feel very good about our trajectory here. Our ad experiments have shown promising results and we’re now scaling ads within both AI Overviews and AI Mode to more users and more queries; including, as I mentioned, international markets and multiturn ads in the U.S.

I think that Google deserves the benefit of the doubt, here. Remember, AI Mode is leading to more queries, which, in theory, means more inventory; if inventory is growing then the cost-per-click should be falling, which historically — and counter-intuitively — is a bullish indicator for digital advertising businesses.

Now there are a few problems with this analysis. First, Google, annoyingly (and unlike Meta), doesn’t report the change in impressions growth for Google Sites, so we don’t know the inventory numbers. Second, contra my assumption, Google Sites cost-per-click growth actually accelerated last quarter:

What you can see clearly, however (even if you can’t see 4Q numbers given how Google reports annual numbers only), is the even more stark deceleration in clicks (which explains the cost-per-click increase, as advertisers were bidding for fewer clicks). The most obvious explanation is exactly what Schindler said: they simply haven’t turned on ads for enough people in enough places in AI Mode, even as AI Mode increasingly becomes the way people interact with Google. It’s the Stories story.

The Agent Play
--------------

The larger concern I have for Google is agents, and yes, it’s the same old concern I had when chatbots first became a thing: anything that pulls people’s attention away from search and its perfect business model is ipso facto bad for Google’s revenue outlook. As I noted above, however, chatbots still have the human in the loop, and honestly I’ve been impressed at how aggressively Google has moved to transform search to keep people using Google, even if it has led to this quarter.

The real concern is about what happens when humans are no longer in the loop. You can spin a story about AI Mode driving engagement first with monetization to follow; it’s a trickier story to tell when the premise is that human engagement actually declines. That, more than anything, seems to explain why Google has launched what seems like 100 agents, which is to say they’ve effectively shipped none of them, and why would they when the very premise is so threatening to their business?

And yet, this earnings call actually provided a lot of clarity about Google’s agent strategy, and I have to say, I find it compelling. From Pichai’s prepared remarks:

One area I’m excited about is helping people get things done, be it booking reservations, filling in forms, scheduling appointments, or making purchases. As AI becomes more personal and starts taking action for people and businesses, trust and security matter more than ever. People already put their trust in Google to get things done in their daily lives; and our leadership here remains an important differentiator. We focused first on getting these agentic experiences right with the Gemini agent for enterprise, because the bar for security, safety, and governance is extremely high.

As a next step, we’re bringing these features to Search. And also, of course, to our billion users on the Gemini app. People are already using the Gemini app to do more than ask questions; they’re using it to do things, including complex, multi‑step agentic queries. Our Gemini 4 series has been a really big step forward here and has already been handling these complex tasks really well. Agents will operate as a unifying layer across our products, so you’ll see us extend these agentic experiences beyond Search and the Gemini app to Chrome, Android and our Workspace products as well.

The Google agent is going to be Search! This seems so obvious: just as Google has basically transformed search into a chatbot, they’re going to make it an agent. Pichai explained more in the Q&A section:

First of all, on agents, what an exciting and dynamic area. I think when I look at our new models and we see the capabilities of the kind of complex tasks they can take in an automated way and execute, it was very, very high quality. It’s clear that the agentic capabilities we have all envisioned are going to be real. And so, I couldn’t be more excited to bring the next generation of agentic experiences to our users.

To your question, I would say a few things. I think the Gemini 4 model series has been important. We are working on distilled versions of our Carbon models, so that we can deploy it across a billion‑user scale efficiently, both from a compute, from a latency standpoint, and from a quality standpoint. So that’s been an important area of investment. Definitely making sure you need more than a capable model at scale, you need a robust harness, you need a highly secure runtime, and this is where GCP comes in, being able to give each and every one of our users a secure runtime with 24/7 availability in the cloud, the right ecosystem of skills and connectors. So, all of that is the foundation.

We have been hard at work, and we decided to roll it out to Enterprise first, which we announced with our Gemini agent for Enterprise. Essentially, because they have the highest bar for security governance and controls. It’s been well received, and we are in the process of expanding that. Later this year, we will bring that same experience to consumers, obviously starting with the one billion users in our Gemini app. We already see people in the app moving beyond Chat to what are task and agent‑like experiences. And so, the trust users have in our products, the fact that all their context is right there, I think sets us up well.

But we are really focused as a company on delivering that unified experience across our products, and you should expect to see a lot of progress later this year.

I’m going to give Pichai and Google the benefit of the doubt that Gemini 4 is actually the first Google model that is capable of delivering an agentic experience, and that distilling it such that it can be served efficiently takes time; I’m willing to grant that benefit because this strategy really is an aggressive one. Transforming search into its own disruption is no small matter, but given what the company did with AI Mode I’m inclined to believe them, and give them time to figure it out.
