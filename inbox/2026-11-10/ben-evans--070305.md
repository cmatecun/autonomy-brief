** Abstraction
------------------------------------------------------------

There’s an old saying in computer science that a computer should never ask you a question if it should be able to work out the answer. That used to mean “what printer do you have?” or “what floppy do you want to use?” but each platform shift brought a new level of things the computer could do, and things the computer didn’t need to ask you, unlocking new things that you could ask. AI gives us another of these changes. It brings new things the computer will do for you without having to ask, and unlocks new questions.

What kinds of questions will AI remove? Well, you open Salesforce and Salesforce asks you what database queries you want to run, and so that’s the question you have to answer. You open any app and it asks you what software things you want the software to do. But really, you shouldn’t be saying “please run this complex 15-step handcrafted query for me across our customer data” or “here is the 500 line SQL query that I would like you to run so that I can generate our FinOps update”.

** Elections and the US AI backlash
------------------------------------------------------------

America’s widespread hostility to AI in general, and data centres in particular, is pretty evenly distributed across Democrat and Republican voters, but Democratic candidates used it as a prominent campaigning point in their sweep of last weeks legislative elections, and on one analysis, 41% of planned construction is in states where a governor is now talking about opposing construction. There’s quite a lot of nuance to that - the single biggest state for data centre construction is Texas, where the governor is talking about slowing down plants that haven’t been approved yet, but not touching the ones that already have permits.So, its not clear quite how much this will actually slow things down (especially given there are more elections in two years). Meanwhile, some of the concerns are about general and unanswerable questions like the impact of AI on jobs, but the issues at the top of the list are often local water use and electric bills, which are really local planning issues that can be
solved without much of a slowdown.

Two broader points here, though. First, this fed into the general rerating of the AI sector by public markets, which is slightly contradictory - the stocks were down because these companies are spending too much too fast on building data centres, and now they won’t be able to spend as much as fast… so the stocks are down more. Of course, that’s how bull and bear markets work.

Second, Elon Musk spent almost half a billion dollars on the election, including $200m in Texas and others, and most of his candidates lost. Many of the loudest voices in Silicon Valley tied themselves to Donald Trump, and there’s another side of that trade coming.

(Finally, this is also bad for crypto, but very few people are still paying attention to that as more than arcane financial plumbing.) ** Anthropic’s IPO rolls on
------------------------------------------------------------

Anthropic published an updated S1, expanding on its ambitions for its own physical infra and giving revenue for Q3 that would seem to indicate that monthly revenue growth slowed down significantly in the course of the quarter. The company told investors it had a $47bn run rate (reportedly defined as previous 4 weeks x 13) in May and $65 billion 2 months later in July, but then the Q3 revenue was $17bn: if it had $5bn in July (65/13), that implies September could only have been say $5.4bn, meaning that annualised revenue only rose $5bn to $70bn in those two months, where it rose $18bn in the previous 2 months.

You could explain this by pointing to the end of token-maxing, companies managing token cost aggressively, and openAI gaining share, but you could also say that these are all rounded numbers with imprecise dates where the degree of imprecision is more than enough to swing the numbers in the other direction... and all of this nonsense is why we really want to move to proper quarterly GAAP numbers as quickly as possible. LINK (https://us.list-manage.com/xP62V30gJtG?e=bc4ee99da9&c2id=e2956131aa985567d31359cb60722597)

Unsurprisingly, investors have been telling Anthropic and its bankers that the $2 trillion valuation it was hoping for a couple of months ago is not sustainable, given that its revenue growth has probably slowed down and the market has derated the entire sector. People are now kicking around one-and-a-quarter or even one. I’m old enough to remember when people spent a long time arguing about the correct valuation for the Google or Meta IPOs, which two or three years later seemed like missing the point, but on the other hand, you could’ve lost a lot of money for al long time being too early on Amazon, and a lot of money you never made back buying companies that were building Internet infrastructure in the 90s. LINK (https://us.list-manage.com/l4a6KcVRYZf?e=bc4ee99da9&c2id=e2956131aa985567d31359cb60722597)

On the other side of the fence, OpenAI’s Greg Brockman admitted what we’d all seen from the revenue run rates - that OpenAI had a bad first half with no revenue growth, as all of the excitement swung to Anthropic. Now it’s back with revenue apparently doubling from June to September and getting into striking distance of Anthropic’s numbers, which of course is also why Anthropic’s own revenue growth seems to have slowed down. LINK (https://us.list-manage.com/14bWn7xPSE4?e=bc4ee99da9&c2id=e2956131aa985567d31359cb60722597)

** Cyber

The big question in cyber is whether using AI to find and patch vulnerabilities at scale will happen quicker than people using AI to find and exploit those vulnerabilities. Pointing to that, this week, OpenAI announced a project for scalable scanning and patching that had found and fixed more than 1000 paths to route on Linux. LINK (https://us.list-manage.com/VZz8cY7m9Mk?e=bc4ee99da9&c2id=e2956131aa985567d31359cb60722597)

However, the next question is how many people will get around to implementing the patches. This week the people hacking supermarket refrigerators expanded their attack across Europe. This is entirely based on old, unsupported and unpatched software, not cracking frontier security systems. LINK (https://us.list-manage.com/dJvHfRJ3CBc?e=bc4ee99da9&c2id=e2956131aa985567d31359cb60722597)

The engineer shouldn’t have fallen for that, but it shouldn’t have worked anyway. LINK (https://us.list-manage.com/fki0tKyNqaN?e=bc4ee99da9&c2id=e2956131aa985567d31359cb60722597)

** Cybertruck
------------------------------------------------------------

Tesla will shut down production of the Cybertruck. This one was a flop - while the company hoped it was a brave and radical attempt to make a reimagined and very cool pick-up, albeit with polarising looks, it turned out to be badly designed and built for a lot of the things that people who actually buy these things care about (even if they really only drive them to the mall). Musk says the resources will be put into the Cybercab robotaxis instead.

Tesla bulls think the company might actually be getting autonomy to work, and the S Curve will start to bend upwards, while everyone else thinks they’ve heard that once a year for over a decade. We will see. (I think it’s also entirely possible that Tesla will get out of the car business entirely, merge with SpaceX and become mostly an AI lab and robotics company, until the next bandwagon comes along.) ** Drones
------------------------------------------------------------

I think you could say there have been three phases of drones. First, the ‘consumer’ devices that quickly turned out to have almost no use case outside of professional video production. Second, the industrial companies that struggled to find product market fit for a decade, and third the sudden explosion in usage in Ukraine. However, those industrial use cases are still there and starting to work. Zipline just raised a $20 billion valuation, having expanded for medical delivery in Africa to ecommerce in the USA, and Axon, the Taser and police bodycam company that’s cornering the law enforcement tech market, just took a stake in Skydio.
