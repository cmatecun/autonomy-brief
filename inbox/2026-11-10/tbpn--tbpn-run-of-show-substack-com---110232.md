Huang, Alice Huang: Shaken, Not Stirred.
The Chinese Spy story continues to get worse. Here’s where the key accusations stand:
Nvidia may have interfered with a federal investigation. Commerce Department investigators were investigating Megaspeed for potentially smuggling Nvidia chips into China. After Nvidia’s general counsel contacted Commerce, investigators faced unusual restrictions, including routing Nvidia inquiries through a senior political appointee and avoiding references to Megaspeed.
Nvidia intervened regarding Alice Huang’s detention. General counsel Tim Teter called Commerce Undersecretary Jeffrey Kessler after Singapore detained Alice Huang. The question is whether Jensen was leveraging Nvidia’s influence to help her.
Nvidia undercounted 50,000 GPUs sold to Megaspeed. Nvidia told regulators its inspections had accounted for the chips, but Bloomberg identified approximately 50,000 additional GPUs, worth billions of dollars. Nvidia said it had inspected additional warehouses.
The FBI, Congress, and Nvidia’s board are investigating. Trump said the FBI was looking into Huang, but was unclear about what exactly. Nvidia’s board opened its own investigation and lawmakers raised questions about what other Nvidia executives knew.
Nvidia’s general counsel was denying the affair as recently as last week. This raises the possibility that senior executives either knowingly misrepresented the relationship or were themselves misled by Jensen.
Jensen had quietly hired crisis PR advisers a year earlier. Wired reported that Heller had been managing his behind-the-scenes communications since at least October 2025. This suggests the company and Huang may have anticipated the scandal long before publicly acknowledging the relationship.
The potential cover-up creates personal liability for Nvidia’s directors. There’s already a lawsuit filed, but if Jensen concealed the relationship from the board while Nvidia publicly characterized it as ordinary business, directors could face more shareholder litigation.

Happy Birthday Marines
Today is the 251st Marine Corps birthday. Both my dad and grandfather served in the Marines. My dad, Michael Fordyce, fought in Operation Desert Storm during the Gulf War as a machine gunner. My grandfather, Tom Fordyce, fought in the Battle of Khe Sanh during the Vietnam War as a sniper.
My grandfather doesn’t like talking about his time in the Marines because he doesn’t want to draw attention to himself. But, I think he’s one of the most heroic men I’ve ever met in my life.
He grew up as a rice and cattle farmer and the son of a WWII veteran. He told me that ever since he was a kid, he wanted to be a Marine. He enlisted after he graduated from Katy High School in Katy, Texas, a town of around 3,000 people at the time. After bootcamp in San Diego, he was shipped off to Da Nang, Vietnam at 18 years old.
He was stationed in Da Nang for about 6 months. He was then sent to Tokyo for a week of rest and relaxation (R&R). On his way back, he asked if he was going back to Da Nang. The pilots told him no and that he was going to Khe Sanh, one of the longest, bloodiest, and most famous battles during the Vietnam War. During that battle, he lost his arm.
My grandfather spoke with the local Huntsville newspaper [ https://substack.com/redirect/c98f9750-2766-48e0-9e7b-b971aec2dc27?j=eyJ1IjoiMXlnZjcifQ.KOUIeBfeCRzhIwFnKXyQigR4j0VpF-SZzascYhSZunw ] in 2013 to talk more about what he experienced at Khe Sanh.
“When I got hit, it was so quick that you didn’t have any pain, but when I looked down I saw my arm was pretty much mangled. The corpsman came rushing over as quickly as he could and took my left hand. He folded it back up underneath my forearm and wrapped it up.
When you get wounded and you have to get a helicopter to come and get you out of a combat situation, you want to make sure other soldiers get you to the spot the helicopter is going to land. My friends were very instrumental in making sure I was on the gurney and that I was protected from enemy fire while the helicopter came in. You could hear the enemy rounds hitting the chopper. All that’s going through your mind is, ‘Oh no. Are we going to get shot down?’
I remember being on the gurney. There was a preacher and there was a corpsman cutting my pants and boots off. Taking my shirt off. Next thing I knew I was completely nude on this gurney. I remember them taking me to this operating room. My arm looked like somebody had taken a butcher’s knife and whacked it off. The doctor said to me, ‘Marine, we’re going to have to take your arm off.’ They took a saw and started sawing and I couldn’t take it. It was hurting so bad. So, they put me to sleep.”
Today my grandfather has a myoelectric hand, a robotic prosthetic hand that uses electrical signals from a person’s muscles to control its movements. “I don’t know how it works, but it just does.”
When I called my dad and grandfather to wish them a happy birthday, they said, ‘The only two holidays more important than the Marine Corps Birthday are Christmas and Easter. That’s it.” – Jackson
