Top Story of the Week
Lucid Motors has a potential robotaxi partner for Europe (link [ https://substack.com/redirect/10716f2e-e866-460f-95a4-25118533368f?j=eyJ1IjoiMXlnZjcifQ.KOUIeBfeCRzhIwFnKXyQigR4j0VpF-SZzascYhSZunw ]). Bolt is partnering with Lucid to develop and deploy at least 25,000 fully autonomous vehicles across multiple European cities and countries, as part of its broader goal of having 100,000 autonomous vehicles [ https://substack.com/redirect/f1fa46f4-2b17-4eb2-bd3d-2754e43a95ff?j=eyJ1IjoiMXlnZjcifQ.KOUIeBfeCRzhIwFnKXyQigR4j0VpF-SZzascYhSZunw ] on its platform by 2035. The vehicles will be based on Lucid’s upcoming Midsize platform and use NVIDIA’s Hyperion autonomous driving hardware, while Bolt plans to own and operate the fleet.
There are still some important details missing though. Bolt hasn’t actually placed an order for 25,000 vehicles, no money has changed hands, and the companies haven’t announced a deployment timeline or most importantly, named the autonomous driving software provider.
Even with those questions unresolved, Bolt is clearly spending more time and money in the AV space, with Lucid now joining Stellantis and Pony.ai as major partners. It’s another sign that Bolt sees autonomous vehicles as an important part of its long-term ride-hailing strategy.
I’m excited to dig into this partnership and hear more about Bolt’s AV ambitions with their CEO on the podcast later this month. Stay tuned! [ https://substack.com/redirect/4b41c91b-0ad0-4265-9c3c-a1bcf3cda0be?j=eyJ1IjoiMXlnZjcifQ.KOUIeBfeCRzhIwFnKXyQigR4j0VpF-SZzascYhSZunw ]
For Lucid, the robotaxi opportunity is also starting to look meaningful. Between this partnership and its separate agreement [ https://substack.com/redirect/1bb93e3b-8cc1-4b68-b389-c9b3640b5efe?j=eyJ1IjoiMXlnZjcifQ.KOUIeBfeCRzhIwFnKXyQigR4j0VpF-SZzascYhSZunw ] with Uber and Nuro for at least 35,000 vehicles in the U.S., Lucid now has potential robotaxi programs covering at least 60,000 vehicles.
