## Top News

According to reporting by The New York Times, **U.S. officials** told congressional staffers in a classified briefing Tuesday that they had found no evidence **Chinese intelligence** orchestrated Nvidia CEO **Jensen Huang’s affair **with Alice Huang or that she passed significant intelligence to Beijing. The Wall Street Journal has [more here](https://www.wsj.com/politics/national-security/no-evidence-china-orchestrated-jensen-huangs-affair-officials-brief-congress-00228bb7).

The **Justice Department **did not formally investigate Jensen Huang before President Trump pushed for his reinstatement as Nvidia CEO, with Attorney General Todd Blanche instead basing his assessment that Huang posed no national security threat on ongoing investigations into Nvidia customers **Super Micro **and** Megaspeed**, as The Wall Street Journal first reported. Reuters has [more here](https://www.reuters.com/business/us-justice-department-did-not-open-probe-into-jensen-huang-before-reinstatement-2026-11-25/).

Defense Secretary **Pete Hegseth** and other senior Pentagon officials reportedly discussed invoking the **Defense Production Act**, a 1950 law granting the president extraordinary economic powers, as **leverage** to pressure **Nvidia’s board **into reinstating Jensen Huang as CEO, though the Pentagon denied that such discussions occurred. The Washington Post has [more here](https://www.washingtonpost.com/technology/2026/11/25/hegseth-pentagon-officials-floated-using-wartime-law-reappoint-nvidia-ceo/).

**Australia** passed a sweeping **online safety law** Wednesday requiring tech companies – including social media platforms and AI chatbot providers – to proactively reduce the risk of foreseeable **online harms** or face fines of up to 5% of their global revenue. The Associated Press has [more here](https://apnews.com/article/b3f044575e9d8106f30a14eee2b89a81).

In a case that raises broader questions about what happens to private AI conversations when chatbot companies go bankrupt, a coalition of **42 attorneys general** is seeking to block **Character.AI** from auctioning off **chat histories** from more than 21 million U.S. accounts, including 2.1 million belonging to minors. TechCrunch has [more here](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/25/state-ags-fight-character-ais-plan-to-auction-off-users-private-chats/).

## FCC Approves SpaceX Plan to Put AI Data Centers in Space

View image: (https://media.beehiiv.com/cdn-cgi/image/fit=scale-down,format=auto,onerror=redirect,quality=80/uploads/asset/file/e2d6c067-4d55-49bd-a1b5-5ed286b2d6ea/space-tesla-cybertruck1.jpeg?t=1795679489)
Caption: Image Credits: Ethan Swope / Bloomberg / Getty Images

**By Kirsten Korosec**

SpaceX was given conditional approval by the Federal Communications Commission on Wednesday to deploy and operate a constellation of up to 1 million satellites for its orbital data center business, a regulatory victory for Elon Musk and his bid to move AI data centers into space.

The FCC authorization was granted despite objections from Amazon, Viasat, and the American Astronomical Society, which [_flagged concerns_](https://aas.org/sites/default/files/2026-03/PUBLIC%20-%20Petition%20to%20Deny%20SpaceX%20Data%20Center%20Application%20%283-6-2026%29.pdf) over orbital debris and congestion, radiofrequency interference, and environmental consequences. Amazon, which is building a competitor to SpaceX’s Starlink service, argued in its [_petition_](https://aas.org/sites/default/files/2026-03/Amazon%20Petition%20to%20Deny%20SpaceX%20Data%20Center%20Application.pdf) that the application lacked basic details and described it as a “lofty ambition rather than a real plan.” Despite that lack of detail, Amazon also said the proposal “risks real harm.”

The FCC dismissed some of the key concerns raised by opponents, including potential threats to scientific research, because the agency determined they fell outside its regulatory authority.

FCC Chair Brendan Carr said in a statement that the action “opens a bold new frontier for the computing infrastructure needed to power America’s leadership and growth.”

Musk thanked Carr in a [_post on X_](https://x.com/elonmusk/status/2125630856140308743?a=sc_15etovBwewXmpe3I&s=20), writing “Much appreciate. This will be a great asset to America and the world.”

This initial regulatory approval doesn’t mean orbital data centers are imminent.

Read more (https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/25/fcc-approves-spacex-plan-to-put-ai-data-centers-in-space/)
