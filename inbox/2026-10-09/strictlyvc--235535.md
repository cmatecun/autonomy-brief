## Anthropic Can’t Reliably Control Its AI Agents. It’s Cutting Off Its Internal Evals from the Live Internet Instead

View image: (https://media.beehiiv.com/cdn-cgi/image/fit=scale-down,format=auto,onerror=redirect,quality=80/uploads/asset/file/0703ad5d-bf6b-4aaa-9c36-0721e9b9a7af/Claude-AI-app__1_.jpeg?t=1791601994)
Caption: Image Credits: Anthropic

**By Tim Fernholz**

Anthropic said its models exploited websites on the internet, including some run by U.S. government agencies, and it will turn off live internet access for all of its internal evaluations until the frontier lab is sure it can monitor and control its AI agents.

The incidents, disclosed in a [blog post](https://www.anthropic.com/research/investigating-unintended-model-actions), involved AI agents [tasked to solve problems](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/25/for-months-openais-agent-swarms-have-been-attacking-online-databases-to-find-obscure-facts/) seeking resources on the internet. In the process, they exploited software flaws, accessed databases without paying fees, used URL shortening services to smuggle information past restrictions, and even [submitted](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/09/an-anthropic-ai-model-sent-a-false-homicide-tip-to-philadelphia-police/) a false murder tip to the Philadelphia police.

Anthropic said it discovered these new issues in a review of its model’s activities that began in July, demonstrating the lab’s lack of awareness of its software’s behavior in real time.

Notably, the company said that alignment training was not yet sufficient for skills like search and computer use that are central to its pitch that AI agents will be used by any professional who relies on digital tools.

The behaviors Anthropic disclosed are similar to incidents involving OpenAI agents that [collaborated](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/04/another-swarm-of-openai-agents-reached-the-open-internet-without-the-frontier-labs-knowledge/) to break into various websites in search of information, including some run by the Australian government.

## Going Public

**Anthropic** reportedly lost $42 billion in 2025 on just $4.6 billion in revenue according to Reuters’ analysis of its IPO prospectus. Looking ahead, the company expects to owe about $518 billion over the next several years for cloud, compute, and infrastructure, and roughly 80% of that can’t be canceled. Mashable has [more here](https://mashable.com/tech/anthropic-lost-42-billion-in-2025).

The collapse of **Firmus Grid**’s planned $5 billion IPO is the latest sign that public investors are growing more selective about AI infrastructure companies, demanding valuations that better reflect operating scale, debt costs, and revenue visibility rather than simply rewarding exposure to booming compute demand.
