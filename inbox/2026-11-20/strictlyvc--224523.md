## Anthropic Rival Says Companies Can (Mostly) Skip the Pricey Models

View image: (https://media.beehiiv.com/cdn-cgi/image/fit=scale-down,format=auto,onerror=redirect,quality=80/uploads/asset/file/281cd122-2758-4252-9d09-710d971995c6/Chenyu-Zhao.jpeg?t=1795207939)
Caption: Image Credits: Jaakko Meyn for Slush

**By Connie Loizos**

On the same day that Anthropic went public in the largest IPO on record, with investor enthusiasm for the AI lab through the roof, far away at the Slush startup conference in Helsinki, the topic du jour was open source everything.

That’s the enormous opportunity being chased by companies like [_Fireworks AI_](https://fireworks.ai/), as enterprises seek ways to avoid paying frontier prices for their AI needs.

Four-year-old Fireworks runs AI models for other businesses. The Bay Area-based outfit specializes in open-weight models, which anyone can download, and helps customers tune them on their own data so they run faster and cheaper than the models of Anthropic, OpenAI, and other heavyweights, and are harder for rivals to copy. Customers have included Uber, Shopify, and Notion.

Fireworks co-founder Chenyu Zhao and many others believe companies are gravitating toward platforms like his because frontier models are pricey and, in most cases, he says, unnecessary unless you need the very latest and greatest generalized intelligence. There are also growing concerns that frontier labs absorb too much of their customers’ know-how, even if only through the prompts used to get answers, a worry Microsoft CEO Satya Nadella [_raised in July_](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/13/satya-nadella-has-issued-a-shocking-warning-to-companies-using-ai/).

Fireworks has also gotten its first black eye, having disclosed two security incidents this fall. In one, hackers reached API keys and tokens stored as secrets for a limited number of accounts; in the other, attackers breached its cloud provider, made their way into a development system, and nabbed customer data, including billing records and some datasets.

Notion says AI chat transcripts from fewer than 1% of its workspaces were exposed, but it has disconnected from Fireworks. Uber says hackers stole the names, addresses, birth dates and driver’s license numbers of about 150,000 drivers through a vendor that [_TechCrunch identified as Fireworks_](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/16/uber-confirms-hackers-stole-thousands-of-drivers-license-information-in-data-breach/). Fireworks will have to win back the confidence of customers like them.

Fireworks, which is backed by Sequoia and Benchmark, among other firms, was assigned a [_$17.5 billion valuation_](https://fireworks.ai/blog/fireworks-series-d-funding) in summer. It’s reportedly in [_early talks_](https://www.businessinsider.com/fireworks-ai-is-seeking-new-funding-at-around-valuation-2026-10) for yet another round, though Zhao declined to comment.

Read more (https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/19/anthropic-rival-says-companies-can-mostly-skip-the-pricey-models/)
