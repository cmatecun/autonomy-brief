## Top News

**Anthropic** released its IPO prospectus today, laying out a** stark vision** of AI’s future: the technology could help cure cancer and Alzheimer’s, accelerate economic growth, and improve government services while also enabling cyberattacks, biological weapons, authoritarian surveillance, and even “catastrophic or existential risks to humanity.” The New York Times has [more here](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/10/26/technology/anthropic-ipo-s1-dario-amodei.html), and [here](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2130357/000162828026067168/anthropicpbc-sx1.htm) is a link to the prospectus.

**Anthropic**’s revenue jumped 1,368% year over year in the second quarter, while its first-half operating loss narrowed to $693 million. More than half of **Fortune 500** companies and over 30% of the **Global 2000** generated at least $100,000 each in revenue for Anthropic over the latest 12 months, but the company has also committed more than $600 billion to computing deals as it races to expand capacity. Reuters has [more here](https://www.reuters.com/legal/transactional/anthropic-opens-books-ais-explosive-growth-staggering-costs-2026-10-26/).

**Anthropic**’s IPO prospectus reveals an **unusually elaborate governance structure** designed to keep control away from ordinary shareholders: an independent trust can appoint four of seven directors, the founders control a fixed 50.1% of voting power for the remaining seats, employees can break certain board disputes through a special class of stock, and major backers Amazon and Google get no votes at all. Bloomberg has [more here](https://www.bloomberg.com/opinion/newsletters/2026-10-26/anthropic-raises-concerns).

## Anthropic’s IPO Filing Reveals Its Strategy for Keeping Enterprise Customers Hooked

View image: (https://media.beehiiv.com/cdn-cgi/image/fit=scale-down,format=auto,onerror=redirect,quality=80/uploads/asset/file/a496c106-3fda-46b9-b8fd-520023635604/GettyImages-2278152325.jpeg?t=1793077142)
Caption: Image Credits: Pavlo Gonchar / SOPA Images / LightRocket / Getty Images

**By Rebecca Bellan**

Anthropic is building the same kind of cross-product ecosystem that helped make tech companies like Amazon and Uber enormously sticky, and its [S-1](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2130357/000162828026067168/anthropicpbc-sx1.htm#ica694c859b8e458dbf6a73f56dde9d42_2979) suggests the strategy is working. More than half of customers spending at least $100,000 annually now use both the Claude platform and a first-party app like Claude Code or Cowork, up from about 30% at the end of last year. 

“The speed and depth of our enterprise customer adoption are powerful evidence of Claude’s position at the frontier of AI,” the S-1, [filed with the Securities and Exchange Commission on Monday](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/26/anthropic-officially-files-for-its-public-debut/), reads. 

As of September 30, Anthropic recorded more than 11,900 customers spending at least $100,000; more than 1,900 customers spending upward of $1 million; and about 250 customers spending $10 million or more annually. 

Anthropic attributes customer momentum to a “significant usage expansion as customers realize the value of deploying Claude across their organizations,” which drove net revenue retention of about 900% as of the end of the third quarter. 

Throwing shade at the entire legacy SaaS industry, Anthropic pitches Claude as not a “static product that serves a single use case,” but rather “frontier AI with rapidly advancing capabilities” spanning the models, the developer platform, and the applications users interact with directly. 

“An advance at any layer compounds across all three layers, accelerating the pace at which we can deliver more capable intelligence,” the filing reads, calling this “integrated approach” the reason more customers use both the Claude Platform and first-party apps.

The playbook is familiar. Uber began by selling rides but has expanded into food delivery and freight. With AWS, Amazon drew in customers with cloud computing services before expanding those relationships into storage, databases, and other offerings, making the ecosystem increasingly hard to leave. Like those companies, Anthropic doesn’t have to sell each product to a brand-new buyer: A business that starts with the API can deploy Claude Code to their engineers and then Cowork to their broader workforce. 

Read more (https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/26/anthropics-ipo-filing-reveals-its-strategy-for-keeping-enterprise-customers-hooked/)

## Essential Reads

**Cathedral**, a months-old cybersecurity startup founded by former DOGE staffers and valued at $1.4 billion, used AI agents for less than 12 hours to **penetrate third-party software** connected to **Anthropic** and access meeting transcripts, salary and job-offer details, and Slack discussions about advanced models. Anthropic says no customer chats, code, or model weights were exposed. The Washington Post has [more here](https://www.washingtonpost.com/technology/2026/10/26/researchers-cybersecurity-start-up-cathedral-got-inside-anthropic/).

**Google DeepMind**’s AI weather model correctly predicted that Hurricane Isaias would hit Florida’s western Panhandle even as conventional models initially pointed farther west, underscoring how quickly **AI forecasting** is improving; meteorologists say advances that once took a decade can now happen within a year. NPR has [more here](https://www.wusf.org/2026-10-26/how-ai-is-transforming-hurricane-forecasts).

**DraftKings** is facing mounting scrutiny from Congress and regulators in several states after The New York Times reported that it **used AI to identify customers** likely to keep gambling and losing when sent promotions; DraftKings denies targeting customers based on their losses. The New York Times has [more here](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/10/26/business/draftkings-new-york-louisiana-blumenthal.html).

The **California shopping mall** that doubled as Twin Pines Mall in **_Back to the Future_** could become a **10-megawatt data center**, part of a proposed redevelopment of the largely vacant property that has already drawn opposition from local residents. Fast Company has [more here](https://www.fastcompany.com/91629888/great-scott-the-back-to-the-future-mall-could-become-a-data-center).

Startups are **turning friendship into a paid service**, matching strangers for dinners, hikes, and other offline activities as loneliness rises. 222, which charges $22 a month plus event costs, tripled revenue last year and expects to quadruple it this year to the high seven figures. The New York Times has [more here](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/10/26/business/friendship-apps.html).
