So Huang, Farewell
Jensen Huang resigned as CEO of Nvidia this morning, less than 24 hours after it was reported [ https://substack.com/redirect/baca50e9-c8e5-496a-b7e4-0cc7d372cb9e?j=eyJ1IjoiMXlnZjcifQ.KOUIeBfeCRzhIwFnKXyQigR4j0VpF-SZzascYhSZunw ] that he had a second affair, this time with a colleague who reported directly to him. The New York Times says the relationship was with Kimberly Powell, Nvidia’s VP of healthcare, and that the two were seen being affectionate at a San Jose hotel during GTC in March.
Nvidia’s CFO Colette Kress [ https://substack.com/redirect/d7863769-afd9-4d64-8bf0-2ee158df2573?j=eyJ1IjoiMXlnZjcifQ.KOUIeBfeCRzhIwFnKXyQigR4j0VpF-SZzascYhSZunw ] will be taking over as interim CEO while the board searches for a permanent replacement. She’s been at Nvidia since 2013. She was at Microsoft for 13 years as CFO of the Server and Tools division, and three years running finance for Cisco’s Business Technology and Operations group. Kress said this morning in a press release that she’s committed to “building on the extraordinary foundation Jensen created over the last 33 years.”
About two years ago, Kress gave a post-earnings interview [ https://substack.com/redirect/89083877-99c6-4621-ad6b-7073fa5f666b?j=eyJ1IjoiMXlnZjcifQ.KOUIeBfeCRzhIwFnKXyQigR4j0VpF-SZzascYhSZunw ] with The Next Platform. Here’s her response when asked how she planned for Nvidia’s “explosive growth”:
We do not waste a day, we don’t waste a dime. And those two things are really important about us, our agility to move fast, but our ability to not waste things along the way.
We are in the early stages of this journey of accelerating computing. We are not going to get this all done in two years. These transitions are two decades, three decades of work to change to accelerating computing and to infuse AI in everything that we do.
Do we know what every day is going to be? Is it all going to grow at this same lovely speed? No. But we know it is going to be with us for decades. And if you keep with that understanding and vision and just continue to plan to make sure that you can do it at speed and meet the expectations on how fast things need to move, that’s all we’re going to do on the planning. And we do plan a lot.
John Gruber wrote [ https://substack.com/redirect/9ce3b23d-d630-4678-8845-4fa79d5ae311?j=eyJ1IjoiMXlnZjcifQ.KOUIeBfeCRzhIwFnKXyQigR4j0VpF-SZzascYhSZunw ] about Jensen’s succession plan today in his blog Daring Fireball, pointing back to a conversation Jensen had with Garry Tan just a few months ago about what Nvidia would look like under its next CEO:
You’re going to build an F1 racer, but you’re going to build it in a way that you can drive. You should adapt the car to you. Somebody asked me, Jensen, if you don’t use conventional management techniques and organizational techniques, what’s going to happen when you leave the company?
Well, when I die on the job — someday — I told them they’ll just have to reshape the company for the next CEO. And the reason that’s wisdom is because we’re the F1 drivers. We’re the racers. And the world is really competitive, and we’ve got to win. We’ve got to achieve our mission. And so whatever it takes to fit the car to you, whatever it takes to fit the organization to you, that’s what you got to do. And the next CEO, whatever their personality is, they can figure it out.
We’ll see if Kress or the future permanent CEO can figure it out.
Clip Spotlight: With Jensen out, what's in store for NVIDIA and the broader AI ecosystem?
So much of NVIDIA's moat was Jensen's incredible tenacity and work ethic. And his dealmaking. The Jensen discount that the market would put on the company would probably be extreme.  
In the private markets, a lot of VCs just assumed Jensen would backstop any AI startup above a certain size. There are a ton of neolabs that have raised ~$1B. And many were probably hoping that if they just showed they were on the path to making something commercially viable, they could count on NVIDIA as a backstop.  
We've gotten into this period where capital is the primary constraint to the buildout. If one of the biggest financiers of that buildout is potentially gone, the AI sector would be due for a major reckoning.
