Top Story of the Week
Waymo Co-CEO Says Uber App Option Was ‘Crapshoot’ for Riders (link [ https://substack.com/redirect/edffe95b-2e46-443a-b486-5bb3f488a956?j=eyJ1IjoiMXlnZjcifQ.KOUIeBfeCRzhIwFnKXyQigR4j0VpF-SZzascYhSZunw ], no paywall [ https://substack.com/redirect/2837d530-49e3-439c-9158-3a91a786e0ce?j=eyJ1IjoiMXlnZjcifQ.KOUIeBfeCRzhIwFnKXyQigR4j0VpF-SZzascYhSZunw ]). Harsh words from Waymo Co-CEO Tekedra Mawakana, but I think she has a point. We’ve seen that early AV adopters are willing to pay more and wait longer [ https://substack.com/redirect/2072acf9-c385-41a3-aa1e-9ef2ded6d2bc?j=eyJ1IjoiMXlnZjcifQ.KOUIeBfeCRzhIwFnKXyQigR4j0VpF-SZzascYhSZunw ] specifically to ride in an autonomous vehicle, so the Waymo roulette [ https://substack.com/redirect/6d980401-003e-4202-97f1-44aa86b72455?j=eyJ1IjoiMXlnZjcifQ.KOUIeBfeCRzhIwFnKXyQigR4j0VpF-SZzascYhSZunw ] in Austin and Atlanta never made much sense from a customer experience standpoint. Waymo has also shown [ https://substack.com/redirect/a8166a79-6467-415e-9e9a-eaa219091b14?j=eyJ1IjoiMXlnZjcifQ.KOUIeBfeCRzhIwFnKXyQigR4j0VpF-SZzascYhSZunw ] how quickly it can get people to download its own app, so the convenience of booking through Uber may not be as big an advantage as many assumed either.
Interestingly, Uber says it offered to add a dedicated robotaxi option, but Waymo declined. And we know Uber is open to this approach since it already offers [ https://substack.com/redirect/f1ec1e11-acf5-43b2-853c-3ec0e187f1eb?j=eyJ1IjoiMXlnZjcifQ.KOUIeBfeCRzhIwFnKXyQigR4j0VpF-SZzascYhSZunw ] a dedicated autonomous tier with WeRide in Abu Dhabi. To me, the better model going forward is what Waymo and Lyft are doing [ https://substack.com/redirect/3b19b6b3-0db2-4ce2-a760-fd65fb543bf3?j=eyJ1IjoiMXlnZjcifQ.KOUIeBfeCRzhIwFnKXyQigR4j0VpF-SZzascYhSZunw ] in Nashville: customers can book directly through Waymo when they specifically want a robotaxi, while Lyft provides additional demand via their app, and handles fleet operations behind the scenes. This allows Waymo to maintain its direct customer relationship while tapping into Lyft’s existing rider base to improve fleet utilization during periods of lower demand.
Tekedra Mawakana said her company’s plan to end an exclusive robotaxi partnership with Uber Technologies Inc. was in response to customer feedback asking for more consistency when ordering self-driving services… An Uber spokesperson said the ride-hail company had asked Waymo about adding a robotaxi ride option for Austin and Atlanta customers to more easily select that kind of trip, but Waymo said no.
Zipline raises $1.5 billion to build nationwide drone delivery network (link [ https://substack.com/redirect/cd4c62f1-67bf-4474-a262-a456a36053f1?j=eyJ1IjoiMXlnZjcifQ.KOUIeBfeCRzhIwFnKXyQigR4j0VpF-SZzascYhSZunw ]). I’m increasingly bullish on drone delivery, especially given how quickly drones can get orders to customers compared to traditional ground delivery. And as Keller Cliffton pointed out on our podcast this week, one of the big advantages is that airspace is regulated at the federal level. While getting that initial regulatory approval can be more difficult, once it’s secured, companies like Zipline can expand nationwide without having to navigate a different set of aviation regulations in every city. That’s a big advantage compared to robotaxis, which have to deal with a patchwork of state and local regulations. There are still plenty of challenges to scaling, but I think this gives companies like Zipline a real advantage.
Zipline has raised $1.5 billion at a $21.5 billion post-money valuation to build a nationwide drone delivery network in the US, expanding its manufacturing facilities and ramping up hiring. The round was led by Paradigm, WCM Investment Management and Layer Global. Valor Equity Partners, Fidelity Management & Research Company, Uber, Tiger Global, J.P. Morgan, ARK Invest and Salesforce Ventures also participated.

David Zipper: In my keynote at the California Transit Association’s annual meeting, I argued that cities have a narrow window to ensure autonomous vehicles enhance local transportation networks and do not degrade them (link [ https://substack.com/redirect/3c539248-3aa8-497b-83d2-19968656ce72?j=eyJ1IjoiMXlnZjcifQ.KOUIeBfeCRzhIwFnKXyQigR4j0VpF-SZzascYhSZunw ]). Some good suggestions here around congestion pricing, automated traffic enforcement, and market-based curb parking.
That said, I think cities have more time to prepare than David suggests. AVs are scaling more slowly than Uber and Lyft did because they can’t simply tap into an existing fleet of vehicles and drivers. Every robotaxi has to be manufactured, equipped, and deployed. For perspective, NYC alone has over 84,000 [ https://substack.com/redirect/e1e1b789-f7bd-43c6-993f-6d4cf5de4441?j=eyJ1IjoiMXlnZjcifQ.KOUIeBfeCRzhIwFnKXyQigR4j0VpF-SZzascYhSZunw ] licensed for-hire vehicles, while Waymo’s entire nationwide fleet is only around 4,000 [ https://substack.com/redirect/da6926e2-b7ea-47f2-8529-3744edf1e63f?j=eyJ1IjoiMXlnZjcifQ.KOUIeBfeCRzhIwFnKXyQigR4j0VpF-SZzascYhSZunw ].
And many of these issues, like congestion, traffic violations, and illegal parking, already exist with human-driven vehicles. Hopefully, cities can use the next 5–10 years to develop smarter transportation policies. Ultimately, I think cities should regulate the behavior and impact of vehicles, not just whether there’s a human behind the wheel.
