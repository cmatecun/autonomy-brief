## Jordan Belfort’s New AI Voice Startup, Tone, Wants to Sell Like the ‘Wolf of Wall Street’

View image: (https://media.beehiiv.com/cdn-cgi/image/fit=scale-down,format=auto,onerror=redirect,quality=80/uploads/asset/file/7e3d24fd-a7a2-4e89-a8e1-0ab1c76c152c/Jordan-Belfort.jpeg?t=1794869841)
Caption: Image Credits: Osiris Media, Inc.

**By Connie Loizos**

Years ago, as questions mounted about WeWork’s soaring valuation, I sat down with co-founder Adam Neumann. We were negotiating a stage interview, and he asked to meet me twice in the weeks leading up to it, once at a WeWork in San Francisco and again at his office in New York.

I knew full well that the numbers that SoftBank in particular had assigned to WeWork [_made little sense_](https://techcrunch.com/2019/01/07/for-softbank-no-majority-stake-in-wework-as-it-scales-down-talks-from-a-new-16-billion-investment-to-2-billion/), but I came away understanding why investors wanted to throw in their lot with him.

It’s similarly easy to understand why people are enchanted by Jordan Belfort, the self-proclaimed “Wolf of Wall Street.” Belfort famously pleaded guilty in 1999 to securities fraud and money laundering tied to Stratton Oakmont, the Long Island brokerage where salespeople pushed penny stocks in pump-and-dump schemes while Belfort acquired a Southampton mansion, a 167-foot yacht, and at least one helicopter. (He destroyed the latter two while high on quaaludes and cocaine, as depicted in Martin Scorsese’s famous adaptation of Belfort’s autobiography, “The Wolf of Wall Street.”)

You might think someone who spent 22 months in prison and was ordered to pay $110.4 million in restitution to 1,513 victims wouldn’t get another shake. The victims ultimately received “far less” than they were owed, TMZ [_reported in July_](https://www.tmz.com/2026/07/13/jordan-belfort-done-with-court-ordered-restitution/), noting that Belfort completed his court-ordered restitution obligations in late April of this year.

But Belfort, whose energy has a galvanizing quality, keeps resurfacing, including in recent years as a Miami-based sales consultant and one-time host of crypto workshops at his Miami Beach home. (A New York Times reporter attended a [_weekend workshop_](https://www.nytimes.com/2022/04/15/technology/jordan-belfort-wolf-cryptocurrency.html) several years ago where the price of admission was one bitcoin or its equivalent value at the time of $40,000.)

Now he is moving into the intersection of what he knows best — sales — and the hottest trend on the planet, AI, building out a voice-based AI platform. The secret sauce is that it imbues its voices with the kind of tonality that wins deals, he says, for example, by phrasing declarative statements as questions (which “builds engagement”) and even whispering at times (which “implies scarcity of information”).

Read more (https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/16/jordan-belforts-new-ai-voice-startup-tone-wants-to-sell-like-the-wolf-of-wall-street/)

## Essential Reads

**Nvidia** is expected to sharply scale back the financial guarantees, startup investments, and other support that made it an **“open checkbook” for the AI economy** under Jensen Huang, with interim CEO Colette Kress already having questioned the returns and size of some deals. UBS estimates a **pullback** could remove at least $50 billion from the market. The Wall Street Journal has [more here](https://www.wsj.com/tech/ai/nvidias-days-as-an-open-checkbook-for-the-ai-economy-are-numbered-90c7b388).

A new **Harvard study** estimates that 403 U.S. hyperscale **data centers** used as much electricity in a year as the entire state of **Michigan**, with most of that power generated from fossil fuels and data-center emissions now triple their pre-AI-boom level. WIRED has [more here](https://www.wired.com/story/data-centers-use-more-electricity-than-michigan/).

**Google** says it has observed **attackers** using a **fully autonomous AI framework** to turn newly disclosed software vulnerabilities into working exploits and launch them without human intervention, marking what its Threat Intelligence Group calls a significant escalation in agentic cyberattacks. SiliconANGLE has [more here](https://siliconangle.com/2026/11/16/google-says-an-ai-framework-turned-security-patches-into-exploits-with-no-human-in-the-loop/).

**Startups and VC firms** are increasingly hiring **“ecosystem” leads** to cultivate relationships with founders, customers, partners, and other stakeholders, reflecting a growing premium on in-person connection as AI-generated content makes digital outreach cheaper, noisier, and harder to distinguish. The New York Times has [more here](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/11/15/business/tech-jobs-ai-ecosystem.html).

Facing growing **opposition to land-based data centers**, startups are exploring floating and **underwater facilities** that could generate power from waves or tides and use seawater for cooling, though corrosion, storms, cost, and environmental concerns remain major obstacles. The Wall Street Journal has [more here](https://www.wsj.com/tech/ai/data-centers-ocean-f676429a).
