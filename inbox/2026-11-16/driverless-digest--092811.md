In today’s episode, I’m speaking with Markus Villig [ https://substack.com/redirect/cb2054b5-b0e0-4e56-ba75-c484a913dd27?j=eyJ1IjoiMXlnZjcifQ.KOUIeBfeCRzhIwFnKXyQigR4j0VpF-SZzascYhSZunw ], founder and CEO of Bolt [ https://substack.com/redirect/10b1285a-f0fb-4873-8625-047be5ca651a?j=eyJ1IjoiMXlnZjcifQ.KOUIeBfeCRzhIwFnKXyQigR4j0VpF-SZzascYhSZunw ]. Markus shares how he built the company into a global mobility platform, why cost efficiency is central to its business, and why he wants to navigate the autonomous vehicle transition before taking Bolt public.
We dive into Bolt’s AV strategy, including its partnerships with autonomous driving developers and automakers, and why the company plans to own and operate robotaxi fleets initially. Markus explains how Bolt’s existing customer base, operational experience, and relationships with European regulators could help it bring autonomous vehicles to market.
The conversation explores Europe’s unique regulatory and technical hurdles, the value of small-scale deployments, and why Bolt favors a handful of deep AV partnerships. We also discuss how human drivers and robotaxis could coexist, why Markus believes AVs can serve unmet demand, and how policymakers and labor advocates might respond.
Finally, we look at Bolt’s target of 100,000 robotaxis by 2035, why Markus sees that figure as a starting point, and how vehicle supply and manufacturing standardization could determine the pace of AV adoption.
