Another interesting story today is that both character.ai and Getty Images have filed for bankruptcy.
Character.ai filed for Chapter 11 after years of pressure over the safety of its chatbots, including lawsuits alleging that the platform contributed to teen suicides and self-harm. The company, which was valued at $1B just a few years ago, is now looking for a buyer. Character has agreed to a $20M bid from Disney [ https://substack.com/redirect/72617a6c-dee2-42cd-a9dc-1486838474ae?j=eyJ1IjoiMXlnZjcifQ.KOUIeBfeCRzhIwFnKXyQigR4j0VpF-SZzascYhSZunw ] to buy some of the company’s assets. According to a court filing, its estimated assets and liabilities are worth around $50-100M.
Getty, on the other hand, is filing for bankruptcy as part of a take-private deal with Koch Equity Development. The two main reasons for the company’s bankruptcy and deal with Koch: the failed $3.7B Shutterstock merger earlier this year, which cost the company more than $100M. And iStock, its royalty-free content platform, is getting crushed by the rise of generative AI use. In a Getty investor presentation [ https://substack.com/redirect/ac1b9ba8-9867-491d-9257-e3a6b82a17a8?j=eyJ1IjoiMXlnZjcifQ.KOUIeBfeCRzhIwFnKXyQigR4j0VpF-SZzascYhSZunw ], the company says they’ve seen “declining agency demand and disruption,” as AI tools make it easier for customers to generate the kind of stock pictures or videos they once would have paid Getty for.
Clip Spotlight: The Atlantic’s Josh Tyrangiel explains why SpaceX overtook Blue Origin despite Jeff Bezos having a two-year head start and far more capital to work with:

"Whereas at SpaceX, everybody was dirty and young. They were sleeping in the office. That's a product of the fact that Elon just didn't have as much money as Jeff."
