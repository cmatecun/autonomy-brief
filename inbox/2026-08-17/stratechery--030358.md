Stripe Acquiring OpenRouter
---------------------------

From Bloomberg ( https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-16/stripe-nears-deal-to-buy-ai-firm-openrouter-for-over-7-billion ) :

Stripe Inc. has finalized an agreement to acquire OpenRouter Inc., a startup that helps companies switch between artificial intelligence models, for more than $7 billion, according to people familiar with the matter. The deal, just months after OpenRouter raised money at a reported $1.3 billion valuation, underscores the demand from businesses to find the most cost-friendly AI solutions. It could also give Stripe, a payments processing firm, a stronger footing in the fast-growing artificial intelligence sector.

The final price for the acquisition could change. The discussions were described by people who spoke on condition of anonymity as the information is not public. A spokesperson for Stripe said the firm doesn’t comment on rumors or speculation. OpenRouter declined to comment.

Nothing is certain until confirmation, although this deal has been rumored for a few weeks; I’m going to assume it’s going to happen, in part because OpenRouter is a very interesting company to write about, and it’s the middle of August.

In addition, a disclosure: I used to write about Stripe more frequently, but over the last couple of years, have mostly not, and not just because the company resolutely refuses to go public and give us some actual data (although that is an eternal source of frustration). Rather, the two companies that I am most intertwined with on the business and product side of both Stratechery and Passport are Stripe and Automattic, so I am inevitably conflicted on both (I don’t write much about the latter either, as some curious emailers have noted in the past). What I have to say here isn’t affected by that, at least in my mind, but reader beware.

Let’s start with OpenRouter, however, and its position in the AI marketplace as a whole, and the best place to start is with an application developer who wants to include AI in their app.

One option is to just choose your favorite frontier lab model and use their API directly. You get a key from the frontier lab developer portal, configure your app with that key, and implement the lab’s documented API calls in your app. This works fine, and guarantees access to the leading-edge models, but if you ever want to change your AI provider you need to go into your app and change the API and add a new key from your new provider.

Another option is to choose your preferred hyperscaler and use their managed AI service. So, for example, if you are an AWS customer you might use AWS Bedrock, which has its own API called “Converse.” You implement the Converse API in your app, add its secret, and specify a model. The advantage of this approach is that to change models you simply specify a new model without having to change APIs and secrets in your app. You are, however, limited to the selection and pricing on Bedrock.

OpenRouter is like Bedrock but for the entire universe of models and inference providers. Every possible model that you might want is offered by someone , whether that someone be the lab who made the model, hyperscalers, neoclouds, or just some random bitcoin miner who decided their power was better served offering AI inference. It’s a true marketplace that developers can access by implementing the OpenRouter API; like Bedrock, you specify the model, but then OpenRouter finds the best inference provider on your behalf.

What is even more interesting is that OpenRouter not only operates across models and inference providers, but also at their intersection: most developers care more about which model they are using than about the inference provider, so OpenRouter will choose the best inference provider based on a developer’s preferences in terms of cost, latency, etc. It truly is a well-named company: it’s an open market and it routes inference demand to the best supplier for a given model. OpenRouter also has a new offering that chooses the model for you.

OpenRouter charges developers for this convenience and access. Inference prices are passed on with no markup, but developers pay for inference using credits bought from OpenRouter; those credits include a 5.5% markup (i.e. $1 of inference from a given provider costs you $1.055). OpenRouter achieved a reported ( https://www.theinformation.com/articles/startup-openrouter-fields-multi-billion-dollar-takeover-interest ) $50 million in annualized revenue in April (I’m assuming that’s net, not gross) with a reported 8 million developers in May; that implies $4.2 million in monthly revenue on $76 million or so in inference (around $900 million annualized). I’m sure that number is significantly higher now, but still, this is an expensive transaction.

Aggregating AI?
---------------

OpenRouter founder and CEO Alex Atallah — who previously founded OpenSea, the NFT marketplace — certainly sees synergies with Stripe. In May, when the company raised $113 million at a valuation of $1.3 billion, the New York Times ( https://www.nytimes.com/2026/05/26/business/dealbook/openrouter-ai-models-fundraising.html ) wrote:

Atallah, an OpenRouter founder and its chief executive, described his company as the A.I. equivalent of Stripe, the financial giant that handles all payments for customers through a single access point. It also helps companies avoid being locked into one particular model provider. “A lot of what we do is try to help people find the right vendor for the job,” he said.

I can see the analogy to an extent. First, there is the fact that both Stripe and OpenRouter are focused on developers with an easy-to-use API. Second, Stripe does do a lot of work — not just for developers, but also other platforms — in simplifying significant complexity in payments. I opened 2020’s Stripe: Platform of Platforms ( https://stratechery.com/2020/stripe-platform-of-platforms/?access_token=eyJhbGciOiJSUzI1NiIsImtpZCI6InN0cmF0ZWNoZXJ5LnBhc3Nwb3J0Lm9ubGluZSIsInR5cCI6IkpXVCJ9.eyJhdWQiOiJzdHJhdGVjaGVyeS5wYXNzcG9ydC5vbmxpbmUiLCJhenAiOiJIS0xjUzREd1Nod1AyWURLYmZQV00xIiwiZW50Ijp7InVyaSI6WyJodHRwczovL3N0cmF0ZWNoZXJ5LmNvbS8yMDIwL3N0cmlwZS1wbGF0Zm9ybS1vZi1wbGF0Zm9ybXMvIl19LCJleHAiOjE3ODk1NTMwMzgsImlhdCI6MTc4Njk2MTAzOCwiaXNzIjoiaHR0cHM6Ly9hcHAucGFzc3BvcnQub25saW5lL29hdXRoIiwic2NvcGUiOiJmZWVkOnJlYWQgYXJ0aWNsZTpyZWFkIGFzc2V0OnJlYWQgY2F0ZWdvcnk6cmVhZCBlbnRpdGxlbWVudHMiLCJzdWIiOiIwMTk1MTZmMS1hMzQ2LTc2MTQtOWU5Ni1iNjNiZDExZmU4NTYiLCJ1c2UiOiJhY2Nlc3MifQ.v5AZMC9M1O8TXh6Pkyey9Xgcdl1OC0I2u4XyVcNHVGTETtFersY395KiaPodDMcnPFxDF0SRpTbIxT2kJMfT9tGUR_ZgjY2bBG6sKJKeNAYnXi5f1sMoxF9A5YedcYOSFiJFenXzZjls06yBcVpuUnmD8ZA4WhJBvkf-yr2WqdJmgkooKWfRgn1COhIawVDRZpdLZXOCdKOTq1JYtzpCY7RzuJEO-Z78Ctwx3GUIKc-yGPbrf8fcffpQaSAjTfGbwF90WdP7xUmM-76hrHLan9bnhjQlqvk3HDUpt1lESHrMrkUg-rybQ5b34yKcVb4FZzYQYgpoGj8OM-osOKqVog ) by discussing a new startup that needed a bank account:

This explains that API call above: a Rocket Rides pilot doesn’t have the wherewithal to open a business bank account at Goldman Sachs, and Goldman Sachs doesn’t have the flexibility to offer a banking account to individual entrepreneurs. This, though, is the exact sort of problem platforms solve: they provide an abstraction layer that connects different sides of a market, even if those different sides have dramatically different needs and capabilities.

In other words, the analogy that Atallah is reaching for is that OpenRouter is, like Stripe, a platform, and like a platform, it has a two-sided network: on one side there are developers, who incorporate the OpenRouter API, and on the other side there are the inference providers; OpenRouter connects the two.

The more interesting question about this platform is whether or not it will have Aggregator characteristics. There is one future where the only models that end up mattering are the models from the frontier labs; recursive AI takes off and their lead becomes insurmountable, leading to not just the most advanced models but also structurally lower prices as their inference costs less and compute becomes abundant enough to close the current price umbrella. That is a world where controlling supply is the key to profitability. Sure, developers can use the OpenRouter API, but at some point they will question why they are paying a 5.5% fee and simply get their compute from the frontier labs directly.

In another future — the more open one that everyone other than the frontier labs wants — frontier labs may be the best but there are very competent alternatives, whether they be Chinese open models, an open Meta model, or other closed models that are also seeking customers. In this world — particularly if there is sufficient compute — controlling demand will be very powerful: inference suppliers will be carrying huge amounts of depreciation and will be heavily motivated to seek demand, giving the Aggregator of demand a powerful position in the market.

OpenRouter is extremely well-placed if the second scenario is the one that plays out: the company already has everyone from the frontier labs to the hyperscalers to neoclouds on its platform seeking to serve its developer base, which is large and, I assume, growing rapidly. That developer base is the real asset (although there are fair questions about its quality): the actual routing capability and marketplace could be built by someone else, but it would take time — time during which OpenRouter would accumulate even more developers who don’t want to change all of their API calls! And, once sufficient compute comes online and needs to be paid for, OpenRouter will have market power over inference suppliers.

Flipping the Business Model
---------------------------

It should be noted that Stripe has been a big winner from the AI boom, at least financially. Both of the big labs and the vast majority of AI startups — including OpenRouter — utilize Stripe for payments, which means that some portion of those amazing revenue numbers is going to Stripe (obviously not necessarily big enterprise deals, but definitely all of those subscription plans).

Stripe has also been forced to adjust to AI and the fact that tokens flow a lot more rapidly than dollars; the company acquired Metronome ( https://stripe.com/blog/metronome-stripe-building-the-future-of-billing ) earlier this year to cover usage-based billing, which Stripe Billing was never designed for. Stripe also is making a major play in stablecoins ( https://stratechery.com/2024/stripe-acquires-bridge-stablecoins-platform-of-platforms/?access_token=eyJhbGciOiJSUzI1NiIsImtpZCI6InN0cmF0ZWNoZXJ5LnBhc3Nwb3J0Lm9ubGluZSIsInR5cCI6IkpXVCJ9.eyJhdWQiOiJzdHJhdGVjaGVyeS5wYXNzcG9ydC5vbmxpbmUiLCJhenAiOiJIS0xjUzREd1Nod1AyWURLYmZQV00xIiwiZW50Ijp7InVyaSI6WyJodHRwczovL3N0cmF0ZWNoZXJ5LmNvbS8yMDI0L3N0cmlwZS1hY3F1aXJlcy1icmlkZ2Utc3RhYmxlY29pbnMtcGxhdGZvcm0tb2YtcGxhdGZvcm1zLyJdfSwiZXhwIjoxNzg5NTUzMDM4LCJpYXQiOjE3ODY5NjEwMzgsImlzcyI6Imh0dHBzOi8vYXBwLnBhc3Nwb3J0Lm9ubGluZS9vYXV0aCIsInNjb3BlIjoiZmVlZDpyZWFkIGFydGljbGU6cmVhZCBhc3NldDpyZWFkIGNhdGVnb3J5OnJlYWQgZW50aXRsZW1lbnRzIiwic3ViIjoiMDE5NTE2ZjEtYTM0Ni03NjE0LTllOTYtYjYzYmQxMWZlODU2IiwidXNlIjoiYWNjZXNzIn0.PnxaSJvOaaVpD6dwC8_GSmxJ0q8fA0somqwn2EMF55uo7tETrSyOAB0bXCf8rZKfJ5MON_hwCy5puZfdZ61j3I8RFdpKEbX_GfFFW5VEwg6VoND-7Sg3Jjwy_C-kfEMzn7lats1m2QuNxBWctUP0WlN1mi7vnXsD3a0EMnv4sp260zbySz_Sblw0Jk915zTnjIWZqXKgMOMia72Gpdbx0f-4jdhP1yAA3-Ge8bzjztfX6XvQoyQ3mrR8GTXv3oSYUUWo_O1ztI87a_vgxoYPKAPln5lx7KBFcGS_Twg8kqAdgs-D9kFD4sbZDLYVfIrUy2Xnx3d-ct9Vml2mHOnbdQ ) , which are likely to play a role in agent-to-agent commerce, where microtransactions make a lot of sense, and credit card fees do not.

What Stripe has not done, however, is aggregate demand. There are rumors — including a story in the Wall Street Journal over the weekend ( https://www.wsj.com/business/deals/stripe-advent-in-talks-to-buy-paypal-ea6aa2ba ) — about Stripe looking to acquire PayPal, which will give it a massive position in the consumer space, albeit one with equally massive integration challenges. OpenRouter figures to be a much better fit, at least culturally, and certainly Stripe can make OpenRouter’s margins better from day one.

The most compelling angle to this acquisition, however, is not margins, but rather business model: I actually think that OpenRouter has its business model completely backwards. I just made the case above that OpenRouter’s best possible outcome is to Aggregate inference demand. To that end, the most important thing for OpenRouter’s long-term strategic positioning is to own more and more demand; the way to own more-and-more demand is not to charge that demand a fee, but rather to make it free to use their service, and ideally over time, make it cheaper. The entities that should be paying OpenRouter are the suppliers!

It’s understandable how OpenRouter arrived at the business model that they have. First, compute is scarce, so what compute provider wants to earn less if they have plenty of demand that will pay full price? Second, OpenRouter started — as any Aggregator must — with a legitimately useful product that they were able to charge for, so they did. In the long run, however, the biggest benefit that Stripe can provide OpenRouter is the time and money to flip their business model around, making their product attractive to not just small developers but also massive corporations who want model flexibility but would never tolerate a 5.5% fee.

Stripe is uniquely positioned to do this. First, as I noted above, Stripe is making a lot more money these days thanks to AI, so they have the revenue to make OpenRouter’s offering free (on top of the inference costs, to be clear). Second, Stripe would still make money on a free OpenRouter offering, because they would be providing the payment services (developers still have to pay the inference providers). Third, Stripe has a whole host of offerings for developers using the OpenRouter API; indeed, more developers using OpenRouter will be good for Stripe’s own growth.

The way this ought to go seems straightforward to me. First, Stripe makes OpenRouter free. Second, Stripe starts charging fees to inference providers, starting with the more disparate providers who will have the hardest time finding developers. Third, expand those fees to hyperscalers who are bringing billions of dollars of inference online and need to earn a return. Finally, do the same to the frontier labs (if possible!). And, all along the way, ensure that everyone on OpenRouter is getting all of their financial needs handled by Stripe, ensure first class offerings for Metronome and Tempo, etc.

It’s possible, I should note, that this actually is the play: Stripe just wants more ins with AI developers, and just as importantly, wants to keep those ins away from competitors; that’s not very inspiring, but perhaps more believable than my Aggregator-in-hiding thesis. As I noted, routing isn’t necessarily a hard problem, and changing APIs isn’t that difficult (but as I have argued for 13 years now, discount inertia and undervalue convenience at your peril).

And again, all of this does depend on the frontier labs not running away with the market. Even then, however, as long as there are two of them, OpenRouter will have a place. And, for what it’s worth, keep in mind that Stripe CEO Patrick Collison is on Meta’s board ( https://investor.atmeta.com/investor-news/press-release-details/2025/Patrick-Collison-and-Dina-Powell-McCormick-to-Join-Meta-Board-of-Directors/default.aspx ) ; he is in a position to have a view as to whether or not the frontier labs will have competition going forward.

This, I must admit, is where I come back to the disclosure: I do believe in Stripe, and that alone gives me confidence in this deal. Beyond that, however, there is a platform here, and it’s one that has the potential to be much larger than even payments. I just wondered last week if we’ll have enough capital for the AI buildout ( https://stratechery.com/2026/nvidias-risky-business/?access_token=eyJhbGciOiJSUzI1NiIsImtpZCI6InN0cmF0ZWNoZXJ5LnBhc3Nwb3J0Lm9ubGluZSIsInR5cCI6IkpXVCJ9.eyJhdWQiOiJzdHJhdGVjaGVyeS5wYXNzcG9ydC5vbmxpbmUiLCJhenAiOiJIS0xjUzREd1Nod1AyWURLYmZQV00xIiwiZW50Ijp7InVyaSI6WyJodHRwczovL3N0cmF0ZWNoZXJ5LmNvbS8yMDI2L252aWRpYXMtcmlza3ktYnVzaW5lc3MvIl19LCJleHAiOjE3ODk1NTMwMzgsImlhdCI6MTc4Njk2MTAzOCwiaXNzIjoiaHR0cHM6Ly9hcHAucGFzc3BvcnQub25saW5lL29hdXRoIiwic2NvcGUiOiJmZWVkOnJlYWQgYXJ0aWNsZTpyZWFkIGFzc2V0OnJlYWQgY2F0ZWdvcnk6cmVhZCBlbnRpdGxlbWVudHMiLCJzdWIiOiIwMTk1MTZmMS1hMzQ2LTc2MTQtOWU5Ni1iNjNiZDExZmU4NTYiLCJ1c2UiOiJhY2Nlc3MifQ.mM-MrwFkXzd5MRuT_m3U8rtUn1EW-gzifQRrK9nEXVGBuZjCyeE20IF-TpmxLTAkmnzusESyRa3g30ic3iKjSl5w5pshlpQ0rtyz4n18iZ-jcO_HOCq4S6RBmnUaaOWqDDxP2cekRueLIA4QJqR8beY_r8980eiuTtMwovNDImtucvoiDqkE2V_zeS5-CWoazTdGknb2pdK_TAUzxyEIRetYGPUSZAwY-YH0GUY9lwzS4v1OVuHkxs6cfTpqp_2yi52rELD96JbuZDvGsSW1f4ue2sdncWNt1vSbVzgeQr8qc5xcyZsrC08wVDakjvs6m6b5NODsa7Unlq6NxAW_Ug ) ; a world where AI starts paying for itself is one where the rails for not just money but tokens are very valuable indeed.
