Top Story of the Week
Mercedes-Benz aims to close AI gap with China rivals through Wayve deal (link [ https://substack.com/redirect/86c07bbf-6991-405d-b238-3719e3d630d6?j=eyJ1IjoiMXlnZjcifQ.KOUIeBfeCRzhIwFnKXyQigR4j0VpF-SZzascYhSZunw ], no paywall [ https://substack.com/redirect/f49b9fdf-37a9-4a7d-9775-496be98cb22b?j=eyJ1IjoiMXlnZjcifQ.KOUIeBfeCRzhIwFnKXyQigR4j0VpF-SZzascYhSZunw ]). Wayve is building an impressive roster of major OEM partners, with Mercedes-Benz joining Nissan and Stellantis, and all three have now invested in the company too. That’s a pretty strong vote of confidence in Wayve’s approach, especially considering its technology hasn’t been deployed and validated at scale yet. Its end-to-end, mapless system is designed to generalize across different vehicles and geographies, so in theory, once the tech is ready, Wayve should be able to scale quickly across its OEM partners without having to map and engineer every new market from scratch.
Of course, there’s still a ways to go. Mercedes’ first product will be Level 2, Stellantis is targeting Level 2++ in 2028, and even Kendall acknowledges that getting from an impressive demo to a production-ready automotive product is a huge step. Still, Wayve is putting a lot of the right pieces in place and it’s impressive to watch.
Mercedes-Benz said it planned to integrate Wayve’s autonomous driving system into at least one model that it will deploy in the next two years, offering “advanced urban point-to-point driving assistance”.
