Clip Spotlight: How Jensen engineered his own downfall
Jensen was always putting out two contradictory messages. The first was that AI was such a transformative technology that we needed to spend trillions of dollars on building it out. That it had the potential to cure cancer, completely change the world, etc.
The second was his approach to selling chips, which was to always treat them like a conventional technology. He was just selling computer chips, in his view. And so he was going to sell them everywhere, to anyone. And he didn't think he should have to give up the Chinese market.
This is where Washington comes in. Their view was that Jensen should have ensured NVIDIA's chips didn't get to China. In other words, following not just the letter of the law, but the spirit of the law. From Washington's perspective, if you're increasingly seen as more of a defense contractor than a company like Apple, you can't just throw up your hands and say you didn't know China was getting your chips. You just have to ensure that doesn't happen. 
Jensen's "not a car" and "You're not talking to someone who woke up a loser" quotes on the Dwarkesh podcast are worth revisiting in this context. After last week's events, it's clear that what he's actually saying in the interview is that he's going to China, and he's going to win. He's been winning there for the last 20 years. From Jensen's perspective, given that Apple sells into China, Tesla sells into China, etc. — why can't he keep dominating there?
It's kind of an old-school perspective. And the approach obviously wasn't sustainable in the AI race. But that's the attitude that got Jensen and his company a bunch of enemies in Washington.
