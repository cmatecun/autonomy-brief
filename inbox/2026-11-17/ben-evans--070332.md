** Nvidia
------------------------------------------------------------

Unless you’ve been living in a hole, you’ll know that Nvidia’s board fired the founder and CEO, Jensen Huang, after discovering that not only had he been having an affair with the founder of a customer who may or may not be a Chinese intelligence asset and may or may not have been a conduit for smuggling Nvidia chips into China, but also had affairs with at least two Nvidia employees and lied about that to the board. And unlike some founders (from Mark Zuckerberg to the collective of Anthropic founders), he doesn't have a controlling stake, so he can be fired.

It looks like at least some of the story is leaking from parts of the US government, that want tighter controls on AI exports to China, in opposition to other parts of the US government that may not, for whatever reason. To simplify a bit, the current restrictions apply to chips going to China, but not cloud GPUs serving Chinese customers, so Nvidia built a huge business selling ships to data centres in Southeast Asia that are mostly doing that (20% of revenue was going through Singapore before it stopped disclosing the number - why?). Megaspeed was one of these - but may also simply have been shipping the chips directly to China anyway.

It’s entirely possible that the board knows more, and it’s also possible that there is more, and worse, to come out. Meanwhile, there’s a temporary CEO (the CFO, who Huang apparently belittled recently), while the board looks for a new leader. Tim Cook? ARM’s CEO? Emmett Shear?

This is all really sad, despite all the memes, but what does it mean for the rest of us? Nvidia doesn’t just dominate the market for AI chips - it’s also the main lobbyist against sanctions on China, a driving force setting the industry agenda, the bank for a large part of the AI industry (the famous ‘circular revenue’), and a bunch more besides, and Huang, personally was responsible for a lot of that. See this week’s column for discussion.

** Wearables and ambient AI
------------------------------------------------------------

Google announced its own smart glasses: following Meta, these have a camera, microphone, speaker, and an AI assistant, but no display, so they act partly as an audio-only endpoint to an AI assistant and partly as a way to place that assistant into your context, seeing and perhaps hearing what you do. It’s fascinating to see the market pivot away from getting a display of some kind onto your face, which remains a largely unsolved optics problem, into unbundling a piece of the smartphone use case, rather than trying to produce the next screen and replace phones entirely. Of course, this is also what the Apple watch does, and note Apple’s always listening Siri Recap feature.

Meanwhile, Apple’s HomeView device also does a bunch of interesting ambient AI listening face recognition and voice recognition, all of course with lots of clever privacy engineering. Recall that Apple has a tendency to ship building blocks in public: Bloomberg’s Mark Gurman reports similar glasses in planning, that might arrive in 2027. ** Agent shopping
------------------------------------------------------------

There’s a lot of buzz in both tech and big companies around the consumer agents that have launched in the last few months - obviously Meta’s Muse, but also Instinct, which is super-hot amongst SV insiders. Every consumer brand is asking its ad agencies and strategy consultants what this means, even though the actual user base is tiny so far.

This week United added itself to the list of companies blocking this, explicitly for two reasons: first operational questions of security and liability, which are in principle solvable, but second because, in the CEO’s words, “we don’t want to create another middleman” (recall that Mark Zuckerberg plans to monetise this by taking a percentage on purchases). LINK (https://us.list-manage.com/8zuD9NNqBwz?e=bc4ee99da9&c2id=e2956131aa985567d31359cb60722597)

On the other side of blocking, Reddit, which last week said it would transition to a logged-in only experience to stop LLMs scraping its content, will launch its own reviews product. (I wonder how many people who were served AI answers derived from Reddit content have ever heard of Reddit, though.) LINK (https://us.list-manage.com/L33nKiI9cG9?e=bc4ee99da9&c2id=e2956131aa985567d31359cb60722597)
