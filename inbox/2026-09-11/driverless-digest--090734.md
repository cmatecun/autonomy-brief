Top Story of the Week
Lyft riders can now hail Waymo robotaxis in Nashville (link [ https://substack.com/redirect/b147cc29-cbe7-4858-a099-778ea6b47747?j=eyJ1IjoiMXlnZjcifQ.KOUIeBfeCRzhIwFnKXyQigR4j0VpF-SZzascYhSZunw ], no paywall [ https://substack.com/redirect/e49de472-c4ee-4603-b667-2a1a03fd5b7e?j=eyJ1IjoiMXlnZjcifQ.KOUIeBfeCRzhIwFnKXyQigR4j0VpF-SZzascYhSZunw ]).
Through the partnership, Lyft customers may be matched at no additional cost with a Waymo autonomous vehicle when they order a regular ride. In order for a match to happen, the desired route must start and end within Waymo’s existing service area. Users can decline a driverless ride or opt out entirely.
I’m bullish on this model. Waymo can keep serving customers who specifically want a Waymo and may be willing to wait longer or pay more [ https://substack.com/redirect/701f1b90-ddbb-4995-9911-32cf2feb82b4?j=eyJ1IjoiMXlnZjcifQ.KOUIeBfeCRzhIwFnKXyQigR4j0VpF-SZzascYhSZunw ] for the experience, while still tapping into Lyft’s larger customer base during lower-demand periods. That feels like a win-win, and it solves a lot of the issues we’ve highlighted [ https://substack.com/redirect/25150e32-2614-45af-9b4b-08de2af4dc70?j=eyJ1IjoiMXlnZjcifQ.KOUIeBfeCRzhIwFnKXyQigR4j0VpF-SZzascYhSZunw ] with the “Waymo on Uber” model.
The main downside for Lyft though is what I like to call the Trojan horse problem: a Lyft customer gets matched with a Waymo, has a great experience, and then decides to start booking directly through Waymo instead.
But I think that’s a risk Uber and Lyft are going to have to live with. It’s tough for them to compete with Waymo on the actual ride experience, so their advantage needs to come from lower prices, shorter ETAs and better reliability.

But it’s certainly an indicator of where the company’s longer-term plans lie. Tesla wants to scale and it doesn’t seem to want to do it alone.

Pony.ai and Verne Kick Off Fully Driverless Robotaxi Test Rides in Zagreb (link [ https://substack.com/redirect/842d3b7b-7f0c-4716-8a4a-dd24ee293572?j=eyJ1IjoiMXlnZjcifQ.KOUIeBfeCRzhIwFnKXyQigR4j0VpF-SZzascYhSZunw ], no paywall [ https://substack.com/redirect/8a96fb60-f747-40a5-acde-ececdf211f75?j=eyJ1IjoiMXlnZjcifQ.KOUIeBfeCRzhIwFnKXyQigR4j0VpF-SZzascYhSZunw ]). This is a nice step up from their initial [ https://substack.com/redirect/fad75583-d6a9-4a8e-b8f8-f46a284a992e?j=eyJ1IjoiMXlnZjcifQ.KOUIeBfeCRzhIwFnKXyQigR4j0VpF-SZzascYhSZunw ] launch, where they still had a safety driver onboard. And it’s a meaningful milestone now that the vehicles are operating fully driverless, a first for Europe. But the deployment still looks more like a limited-corridor robotaxi service than a true citywide deployment.
One framework I’ve been thinking about for AV launches is whether the main barrier is technological or regulatory. In Europe’s case, this increasingly looks like a regulatory challenge. We already know Pony.ai’s technology can operate driverlessly from its operations in China, so the question is less whether the system can drive and more how quickly regulators will allow that capability to be deployed more broadly.
The test rides take place along a 22-kilometer route linking Verne's headquarters and one of Zagreb's main business districts with Franjo Tuđman Airport Zagreb. The fully driverless routes will be further expanded in the next few months, with the aim to cover the whole operating area in Zagreb.

Poseidon Aerospace lands $60M ahead of first pilotless test flight (link [ https://substack.com/redirect/40d4bf25-a7b8-4c5f-bfbb-442d23019f86?j=eyJ1IjoiMXlnZjcifQ.KOUIeBfeCRzhIwFnKXyQigR4j0VpF-SZzascYhSZunw ]). I’ve always been skeptical of the economics of autonomous aircraft since pilots are only a small portion of airline costs. But Poseidon’s thesis is broader: going pilotless can improve utilization, eliminate crew constraints, reduce aircraft weight by removing the cockpit and life support systems, and make it easier to operate in higher-risk environments where you may not want to put a pilot in harm’s way.
The startup is embracing autonomy and remote piloting, but it’s not bothering with the vertical takeoff and landing tech that is de rigueur among so many aviation startups. There’s also no hydrogen or electric powertrain to be found. Egret and its seaplane variant, Heron, are fixed-wing aircraft powered by regular old combustion engines.

‘Waymo is changing multi-stop behavior. Now when you make stops, you can only chill for 5 minutes, and then must continue’ (link [ https://substack.com/redirect/39672107-6384-437c-87c7-5606d7ddca68?j=eyJ1IjoiMXlnZjcifQ.KOUIeBfeCRzhIwFnKXyQigR4j0VpF-SZzascYhSZunw ]). Waymo’s stop product works fine if you’re picking someone up or dropping someone off. But if you’re riding solo and want to make a quick stop, you can’t really use it that way. If you get out of the vehicle, the car leaves, and you have to request another Waymo when you’re ready to continue.
That always felt like a strange product experience to me. If I add a stop, I’d expect the same car to wait for a few minutes while I run inside somewhere. A five-minute limit seems totally reasonable, but the product should actually let solo riders use those five minutes. And I’d be happy to pay for the waiting time too. I wouldn’t expect the car to sit there for free.
