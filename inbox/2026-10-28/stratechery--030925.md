Meta Earnings
-------------

From Bloomberg ( https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-21/meta-boosts-bet-on-ai-with-new-spending-outlook-stock-offering ) :

Meta Platforms Inc. shares fell after Chief Executive Officer Mark Zuckerberg told investors the company would bet even more aggressively on artificial intelligence. During its quarterly earnings report Wednesday, the social-media giant boosted its capital spending plans for the year and announced a new class of nonvoting stock that could ultimately be used to help pay for acquisitions and other expenses. The moves show that Meta has confidence that its already-stratospheric spending on AI is bearing fruit. After struggling to persuade investors to accept ballooning costs, Meta has made headway with its technology in recent months, especially with a new AI agent called Muse. Zuckerberg pointed to that progress as reason to keep plowing ahead.

Meta is indeed plowing ahead; from the Wall Street Journal ( https://www.wsj.com/tech/ai/meta-plans-35-billion-stock-offering-to-power-ai-buildout-eyes-95-billion-total-plan-10b8947e ) earlier this week:

Meta Platforms unveiled a plan to raise up to $95 billion in waves of stock offerings, as the company embarks on a cash-raising spree to fund its massive investments in artificial-intelligence infrastructure. The Facebook parent said Monday it will launch roughly $35 billion in public offerings, comprising $22.5 billion of Class A common stock and $12.5 billion of depositary shares representing mandatory convertible preferred stock.

Meta also plans to establish an at-the-market stock offering program of up to $60 billion, potentially beginning in the first quarter of 2027. That is the program by which the company will create a new class of nonvoting stock, a move it previously announced. The company expects to sell shares of its new “Class C” stock over time, though Meta said Monday it hasn’t finalized the arrangement.

One year ago, after Meta’s 3Q 2025 earnings, I wrote that the company was realizing the true cost of Reality Labs ( https://stratechery.com/2025/google-earnings-meta-earnings-the-cost-of-reality-labs/?access_token=eyJhbGciOiJFUzI1NiIsImtpZCI6IkxzanFRcEhoMUJUdmU0djJQUUVuejIiLCJ0eXAiOiJKV1QifQ.eyJhdWQiOiJzdHJhdGVjaGVyeS5wYXNzcG9ydC5vbmxpbmUiLCJhenAiOiJIS0xjUzREd1Nod1AyWURLYmZQV00xIiwiZW50Ijp7InVyaSI6WyJodHRwczovL3N0cmF0ZWNoZXJ5LmNvbS8yMDI1L2dvb2dsZS1lYXJuaW5ncy1tZXRhLWVhcm5pbmdzLXRoZS1jb3N0LW9mLXJlYWxpdHktbGFicy8iXX0sImV4cCI6MTc5NTc3NDE2NSwiaWF0IjoxNzkzMTgyMTY1LCJpc3MiOiJodHRwczovL2FwcC5wYXNzcG9ydC5vbmxpbmUvb2F1dGgiLCJzY29wZSI6ImZlZWQ6cmVhZCBhcnRpY2xlOnJlYWQgYXNzZXQ6cmVhZCBjYXRlZ29yeTpyZWFkIGVudGl0bGVtZW50cyIsInN1YiI6IjAxOTUxNmYxLWEzNDYtNzYxNC05ZTk2LWI2M2JkMTFmZTg1NiIsInVzZSI6ImFjY2VzcyJ9.hOvw0fyiNipQ8IPxyMgvh6-tG5FayGOB_UXYFI7K040_MxiCQ1viWaXn_1P1C7bo5vY4sTYoi60o0agJBkNtZg ) :

What I also think is happening is that Meta is finally paying the price for the billions of dollars it has spent on Reality Labs with precious little financial return to show for it. Yes, investors have long grumbled at how much money Meta has spent on AR and VR, but ultimately I think Zuckerberg has gotten a pass; even the stock market freakout in 2022 was more about TikTok and ATT than about the Metaverse (the Metaverse spending was taken as an indication that Meta’s core business was faltering).

This time, however, Zuckerberg once again seems to be asking for a blank check, and given that the last blank check is growing, not cashing, I think that investors are hesitant about writing another one. Again, I’m not sure this is necessarily fair — Meta is clearly leveraging more compute effectively in its ad business, and AI is not the Metaverse — but unless Zuckerberg wants to become the world’s biggest ad business salesman he’s going to need to show some tangible evidence sooner or later that Meta can in fact win on product.

What a difference a year makes. Meta has indeed shown up asking for that blank check, and just for context, this now-$97.5 billion worth of equity offerings very nearly matches all of Reality Labs’ recorded losses. Now, to be fair, those recorded losses only go back to Q4 2019; Meta bought Oculus for $2 billion in 2014, which is to say the cumulative losses since then are higher than the $100.8 billion on the books. Still, the fact we are in the same ballpark points to how much larger the AI expenditures are in aggregate.

What is different is that I strongly believe that Meta has earned the benefit of the doubt, and not just because I have been on the record since 2024 that Meta had arguably the largest AI opportunity ( https://stratechery.com/2024/metas-ai-abundance/?access_token=eyJhbGciOiJFUzI1NiIsImtpZCI6IkxzanFRcEhoMUJUdmU0djJQUUVuejIiLCJ0eXAiOiJKV1QifQ.eyJhdWQiOiJzdHJhdGVjaGVyeS5wYXNzcG9ydC5vbmxpbmUiLCJhenAiOiJIS0xjUzREd1Nod1AyWURLYmZQV00xIiwiZW50Ijp7InVyaSI6WyJodHRwczovL3N0cmF0ZWNoZXJ5LmNvbS8yMDI0L21ldGFzLWFpLWFidW5kYW5jZS8iXX0sImV4cCI6MTc5NTc3NDE2NSwiaWF0IjoxNzkzMTgyMTY1LCJpc3MiOiJodHRwczovL2FwcC5wYXNzcG9ydC5vbmxpbmUvb2F1dGgiLCJzY29wZSI6ImZlZWQ6cmVhZCBhcnRpY2xlOnJlYWQgYXNzZXQ6cmVhZCBjYXRlZ29yeTpyZWFkIGVudGl0bGVtZW50cyIsInN1YiI6IjAxOTUxNmYxLWEzNDYtNzYxNC05ZTk2LWI2M2JkMTFmZTg1NiIsInVzZSI6ImFjY2VzcyJ9.nJDZIzexXRpAWl4RiBuZCN0jygicWjd1sn9V07jtp1g7q6j6oRIFkU75k5exaGPxagLXUzaeQq95QFgaa_zlXw ) ; rather, go back to what Zuckerberg wrote in July 2025 ( https://www.meta.com/superintelligence/ ) when Meta’s AI efforts seemed dead in the water, even as the company was spending millions of dollars on talent and making billions of dollars worth of compute commitments:

As profound as the abundance produced by AI may one day be, an even more meaningful impact on our lives will likely come from everyone having a personal superintelligence that helps you achieve your goals, create what you want to see in the world, experience any adventure, be a better friend to those you care about, and grow to become the person you aspire to be. It is difficult to overstate how important Muse is, not just in terms of what it represents in terms of AI ( https://stratechery.com/2026/openai-does-math-reward-hacking-meta-launches-personal-agent/?access_token=eyJhbGciOiJFUzI1NiIsImtpZCI6IkxzanFRcEhoMUJUdmU0djJQUUVuejIiLCJ0eXAiOiJKV1QifQ.eyJhdWQiOiJzdHJhdGVjaGVyeS5wYXNzcG9ydC5vbmxpbmUiLCJhenAiOiJIS0xjUzREd1Nod1AyWURLYmZQV00xIiwiZW50Ijp7InVyaSI6WyJodHRwczovL3N0cmF0ZWNoZXJ5LmNvbS8yMDI2L29wZW5haS1kb2VzLW1hdGgtcmV3YXJkLWhhY2tpbmctbWV0YS1sYXVuY2hlcy1wZXJzb25hbC1hZ2VudC8iXX0sImV4cCI6MTc5NTc3NDE2NSwiaWF0IjoxNzkzMTgyMTY1LCJpc3MiOiJodHRwczovL2FwcC5wYXNzcG9ydC5vbmxpbmUvb2F1dGgiLCJzY29wZSI6ImZlZWQ6cmVhZCBhcnRpY2xlOnJlYWQgYXNzZXQ6cmVhZCBjYXRlZ29yeTpyZWFkIGVudGl0bGVtZW50cyIsInN1YiI6IjAxOTUxNmYxLWEzNDYtNzYxNC05ZTk2LWI2M2JkMTFmZTg1NiIsInVzZSI6ImFjY2VzcyJ9.FFhkBD5LjalVg8mDWxE5bVsbXpw5opNVsX7P8ZjwYR4pormsJKK_PswEmwxSC-xioZy0nG3prIRMEBn64NGfjQ ) , or the business opportunities ( https://stratechery.com/2026/apps-agents-and-aggregation/?access_token=eyJhbGciOiJFUzI1NiIsImtpZCI6IkxzanFRcEhoMUJUdmU0djJQUUVuejIiLCJ0eXAiOiJKV1QifQ.eyJhdWQiOiJzdHJhdGVjaGVyeS5wYXNzcG9ydC5vbmxpbmUiLCJhenAiOiJIS0xjUzREd1Nod1AyWURLYmZQV00xIiwiZW50Ijp7InVyaSI6WyJodHRwczovL3N0cmF0ZWNoZXJ5LmNvbS8yMDI2L2FwcHMtYWdlbnRzLWFuZC1hZ2dyZWdhdGlvbi8iXX0sImV4cCI6MTc5NTc3NDE2NSwiaWF0IjoxNzkzMTgyMTY1LCJpc3MiOiJodHRwczovL2FwcC5wYXNzcG9ydC5vbmxpbmUvb2F1dGgiLCJzY29wZSI6ImZlZWQ6cmVhZCBhcnRpY2xlOnJlYWQgYXNzZXQ6cmVhZCBjYXRlZ29yeTpyZWFkIGVudGl0bGVtZW50cyIsInN1YiI6IjAxOTUxNmYxLWEzNDYtNzYxNC05ZTk2LWI2M2JkMTFmZTg1NiIsInVzZSI6ImFjY2VzcyJ9.C7ISTFCKUr5MjKNWX8e34o4AfmyLXM49s1SR8T55FUXUh30uxwRUHuElP6q4mZMAz-Rvk6rYFRVBRP9eN8IeRg ) , but specifically that it represents Zuckerberg delivering on his promise in a way that Reality Labs has never come close to doing. Muse and the Frontier
---------------------

From Artificial Analysis on X ( https://x.com/ArtificialAnlys/status/2115265133467635877 ) :

Announcing Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index v5, featuring 2 new benchmarks and 3 upgrades. For the first time, the majority of the Index is built on private datasets.

The Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index is our composite measure of model capabilities across tasks reflecting real-world usage today. v5 is our biggest upgrade since v4 launched in January. With v5, 60% of the Index is now based on evaluations with private test sets or held-out answers.

Alongside Intelligence Index v5, we’re launching the Artificial Analysis Progress Index v1, which tracks model capability over time on one continuous scale.

New benchmarks are, in my estimation, the only benchmarks worth paying attention to, because by virtue of being new, models have not been bench-maxxed, i.e. trained specifically to do well on the benchmarks in question. To that end, here is the Index ( https://artificialanalysis.ai/#intelligence-category-tabs ) :

( https://artificialanalysis.ai/ )

The rankings here roughly match my sense of the landscape and, for what it is worth, my experience:

* Opus 5.5 is the best model, although the GPT-6 family is very strong.
* Chinese models are really not that close to the frontier (although Kimi K3.1 Pro’s showing is impressive; it’s also the largest Chinese model by far). This is obvious when you use them (which is to say that using them makes you very aware of what bench-maxxing means in practice).
* Google and Meta are very much on the frontier, albeit not state-of-the-art (Grok, meanwhile, is falling far behind).

Meta’s comeback here is notable, and necessary: for all of the product sensibility that goes into Muse, it really does feel like the model is just barely good enough, which makes sense when you see Muse Spark Open’s score on this chart; you also realize why the release of Muse Wonder was an important milestone, and what a difference it is going to make when Meta figures out how to serve it efficiently to more users. Zuckerberg said on the earnings call ( https://s21.q4cdn.com/399680738/files/content_files/META-Q3-2026-Earnings-Call-Transcript.pdf ) :

And then there’s just continuing to increase the value in the product which I think we will largely do by continuing to develop new models. That’s why I mentioned that we launched Muse first on Muse Spark, and that’s what is currently the default across the free tier. And we rolled out Muse Wonder, and people can access that in the paid tier today. And we know that it performs a lot better, so we’re working on getting a more efficient version of Muse Wonder that we’ll be able to roll out more broadly. But you can imagine just as we iterate forward in two, three models into the future, when you have significantly higher intelligence and capability to solve many more problems for people and the ability to deliver that at a much more affordable price point, I think that that’s going to just unlock a lot more value to people. So we’re very excited about that.

Muse presents to investors a very binary question: if you think that it is a tremendous opportunity, then you must support Meta’s compute spending, because spending is the only way to get better models. That was, as I noted yesterday ( https://stratechery.com/2026/anthropic-s-1-the-cost-of-compute-who-is-paying/?access_token=eyJhbGciOiJFUzI1NiIsImtpZCI6IkxzanFRcEhoMUJUdmU0djJQUUVuejIiLCJ0eXAiOiJKV1QifQ.eyJhdWQiOiJzdHJhdGVjaGVyeS5wYXNzcG9ydC5vbmxpbmUiLCJhenAiOiJIS0xjUzREd1Nod1AyWURLYmZQV00xIiwiZW50Ijp7InVyaSI6WyJodHRwczovL3N0cmF0ZWNoZXJ5LmNvbS8yMDI2L2FudGhyb3BpYy1zLTEtdGhlLWNvc3Qtb2YtY29tcHV0ZS13aG8taXMtcGF5aW5nLyJdfSwiZXhwIjoxNzk1Nzc0MTY1LCJpYXQiOjE3OTMxODIxNjUsImlzcyI6Imh0dHBzOi8vYXBwLnBhc3Nwb3J0Lm9ubGluZS9vYXV0aCIsInNjb3BlIjoiZmVlZDpyZWFkIGFydGljbGU6cmVhZCBhc3NldDpyZWFkIGNhdGVnb3J5OnJlYWQgZW50aXRsZW1lbnRzIiwic3ViIjoiMDE5NTE2ZjEtYTM0Ni03NjE0LTllOTYtYjYzYmQxMWZlODU2IiwidXNlIjoiYWNjZXNzIn0.kryJKEMIEMVM2xY-5MqUGqVD94ePccB2byosssj5DD8OZronVREmdTnW2T_3b1jwYcsPlbdyV4IAPsD-3DODew ) , the most important takeaway from Anthropic’s S-1: R&D compute is massively larger than inference compute, and its share of the total compute bill is growing. Yes, serving Muse will cost a lot; making Muse great will, at least for a while, cost more.

This also, I must admit, gave me more sympathy for Meta’s enterprise aspirations. The project is, to be clear, entirely solipsistic ( https://stratechery.com/2026/one-more-note-on-agents-meta-connect-meta-enterprise-platform/?access_token=eyJhbGciOiJFUzI1NiIsImtpZCI6IkxzanFRcEhoMUJUdmU0djJQUUVuejIiLCJ0eXAiOiJKV1QifQ.eyJhdWQiOiJzdHJhdGVjaGVyeS5wYXNzcG9ydC5vbmxpbmUiLCJhenAiOiJIS0xjUzREd1Nod1AyWURLYmZQV00xIiwiZW50Ijp7InVyaSI6WyJodHRwczovL3N0cmF0ZWNoZXJ5LmNvbS8yMDI2L29uZS1tb3JlLW5vdGUtb24tYWdlbnRzLW1ldGEtY29ubmVjdC1tZXRhLWVudGVycHJpc2UtcGxhdGZvcm0vIl19LCJleHAiOjE3OTU3NzQxNjUsImlhdCI6MTc5MzE4MjE2NSwiaXNzIjoiaHR0cHM6Ly9hcHAucGFzc3BvcnQub25saW5lL29hdXRoIiwic2NvcGUiOiJmZWVkOnJlYWQgYXJ0aWNsZTpyZWFkIGFzc2V0OnJlYWQgY2F0ZWdvcnk6cmVhZCBlbnRpdGxlbWVudHMiLCJzdWIiOiIwMTk1MTZmMS1hMzQ2LTc2MTQtOWU5Ni1iNjNiZDExZmU4NTYiLCJ1c2UiOiJhY2Nlc3MifQ.33vWp45zvHLmpO033tf8LksykoOnX65kPwvgVbSB5QKeQobWcgL2ojR7Uo7Z2zRZuUsKSxU7afao4z2sldx6xg ) ; Zuckerberg said as much in his opening remarks:

One other note on compute economics is that since training is a large fixed cost, now that we have leading models, we want to serve as much high-margin inference demand as possible. That is why we are building the Meta Enterprise Platform, led by CJ Desai. Muse Wonder and even more advanced models that we are training can serve additional high-margin services beyond the Muse network, including our API, Muse Code, and other enterprise services.

What I mean by solipsistic is that the enterprise initiative is coming into being not because enterprise customers are asking for it, but because Zuckerberg sees the need to maximize the number of call options on the massive training costs he’s incurring. What makes me more sympathetic is that it is more likely than I thought that there will be enough inference compute to go around if this effort hits (just because the share of compute going to inference is likely lower than I originally thought). If I’m honest, I still think it’s an unnecessary distraction, but sometimes solipsism is self-preservation!

That noted, the most compelling argument for investors opposed to this spending is not that Muse doesn’t need it, but that Meta actually doesn’t need Muse. CFO Susan Li started out her summary of the company’s business results thusly:

Q3 Total Family of Apps revenue was $64.2 billion, up 26%. Q3 Family of Apps ad revenue was $63.1 billion, up 26% on both a reported and constant currency basis. In Q3, the total number of ad impressions served across our services increased 10%. Impression growth was healthy across all regions, driven by growth in engagement and users, as well as ad load optimizations. The global average price per ad increased 14% year-over-year, driven by ad performance gains and improvements in macro conditions relative to Q3 of last year. This was partially offset by strong impression growth, particularly from lower-monetizing surfaces and regions.

The beauty of Meta’s business is that it does seem to be as AI-proof as a digital business can be. People want to be entertained more than they want to be productive, and Meta is, ultimately, in the personal entertainment business. Simply nurturing that business, however, is not and will never be what a founder is going to do, particularly if that founder is Zuckerberg. One more answer from the earnings call:

I mean these are long-term bets. I feel like this is what we’re here to do, is make long-term technology bets and design the best experiences end to end that we can for billions of people around the world. And I think as long as we continue to do that, then over time the business takes care of itself as long as we continue executing well which I’m really proud of the team for how we’re doing on that.

When investors say they want the business to take care of itself, they just want the business to print money; when Zuckerberg says it he means that he’s not worried about the business at all, you’ll just have to trust it shows up eventually.

Musebook
--------

One new idea Zuckerberg put forward was very Facebook-esque: call it Musebook:

We expect to grow Muse to serve billions of people. This is both our mission and our strategy. It’s our mission in that we believe in putting the power of personal superintelligence in everyone’s hands. We believe that that is the path to ensuring a positive future for everyone with individual empowerment as the source of prosperity and balance of power as the foundation for safety. On a values level, we believe that delivering this capability widely is the best thing that we can be doing. This is also our strategy, because we think that billions of Muses in a network at scale will be able to get more done for people and businesses by working together. So we’d like to create a large scale Muse network as quickly as possible.

In the Q&A section Zuckerberg tied this concept into the company’s enterprise and SMB initiatives:

Over time we would like to make it so that we have a whole kind of business network of Muse agents as well. There’s the business agent which we’ve talked about on previous earnings calls for businesses to help out with sales and customer support. But what we see is a lot of people are actually using Muse today to help run especially small businesses. And we think that there’s both an opportunity to help create an agent that people use to really enable them to automate and do a lot of the parts of spinning up a small business that are just kind of difficult and time consuming but aren’t the core thing that a person is trying to do. So we think that’s a big deal. And then making a version of Muse that people can bring into different corporate environments and work there, we think is going to be a big deal.

And then beyond that, building what I refer to as the Muse network which is making it so that Muse is not just a kind of single player game where you have your Muse but making it so that your Muses can work with your friends and your families. And if you want to buy something, it’s not just navigating to that business’ website. It’s talking to their agent. I think that getting that all to work well together is going to unlock tremendous value at scale. So I’m very excited about all of that. I think the enterprise use is going to be a big part of it, but it’s also going to kind of have this very valuable boost in the utility for consumers, too. So I’m very excited about both of those.

Right now agents, to the extent they successfully interact with other entities, do so by using human-shaped interaction layers: chat, email, even phone calls. You can certainly see how that interaction could be much more effective if it were actually designed for agents from the get-go. The challenge, however, is figuring out what that standard is; there are proposals ( https://stratechery.com/2026/apple-and-lg-the-house-for-everyone-else-agent-standards-and-amazon/?access_token=eyJhbGciOiJFUzI1NiIsImtpZCI6IkxzanFRcEhoMUJUdmU0djJQUUVuejIiLCJ0eXAiOiJKV1QifQ.eyJhdWQiOiJzdHJhdGVjaGVyeS5wYXNzcG9ydC5vbmxpbmUiLCJhenAiOiJIS0xjUzREd1Nod1AyWURLYmZQV00xIiwiZW50Ijp7InVyaSI6WyJodHRwczovL3N0cmF0ZWNoZXJ5LmNvbS8yMDI2L2FwcGxlLWFuZC1sZy10aGUtaG91c2UtZm9yLWV2ZXJ5b25lLWVsc2UtYWdlbnQtc3RhbmRhcmRzLWFuZC1hbWF6b24vIl19LCJleHAiOjE3OTU3NzQxNjUsImlhdCI6MTc5MzE4MjE2NSwiaXNzIjoiaHR0cHM6Ly9hcHAucGFzc3BvcnQub25saW5lL29hdXRoIiwic2NvcGUiOiJmZWVkOnJlYWQgYXJ0aWNsZTpyZWFkIGFzc2V0OnJlYWQgY2F0ZWdvcnk6cmVhZCBlbnRpdGxlbWVudHMiLCJzdWIiOiIwMTk1MTZmMS1hMzQ2LTc2MTQtOWU5Ni1iNjNiZDExZmU4NTYiLCJ1c2UiOiJhY2Nlc3MifQ.fJy92xZaxv3uj0cL09WKNxN7YDNAJBR3dm3O04zYdoNSH_cG5F4GQDW09mcL9v6VgpxAZnT9XQGXmwcGvPfTHA ) , but if there are a bunch of competitive consumer agents it’s possible that we end up stuck with the ones designed for humans, amended somewhat to accommodate agents.

The alternative is that one company owns the entire interaction layer because they have horizontal integration, which is to say they run everyone’s personal agent. That seems to be what Zuckerberg is driving at, and it’s attractive not only because it makes sense but also because it feels aligned with Meta’s roots — and the exact opposite of trying to build a vertically integrated hardware business.
