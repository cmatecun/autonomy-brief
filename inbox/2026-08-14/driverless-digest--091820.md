Top Story of the Week
Uber Exits Serve Robotics Stake as Delivery Alliance Unravels (link [ https://substack.com/redirect/c616ab61-ea2f-4e96-b244-af36fe6b2f6e?j=eyJ1IjoiMXlnZjcifQ.KOUIeBfeCRzhIwFnKXyQigR4j0VpF-SZzascYhSZunw ], no paywall [ https://substack.com/redirect/75e6b545-082f-41e6-89f1-bf7f7f9a41eb?j=eyJ1IjoiMXlnZjcifQ.KOUIeBfeCRzhIwFnKXyQigR4j0VpF-SZzascYhSZunw ]). I was surprised by this one, since you don’t see many Uber partners walk away, especially smaller ones, and Serve was also born inside the Uber/Postmates ecosystem. Now Uber has sold its stake and Serve doesn’t plan to renew their agreement when it expires next year. The disagreement seems to be less about the robots themselves and more about how to actually operate and scale them. Interestingly, Serve says its experience with DoorDash has been much better, which shows that Uber’s aggregator model isn’t as simple as plugging autonomous supply into the platform. Uber does still have several other delivery robot partners, but with both Waymo and Serve moving away from Uber, it will be interesting to see if this becomes a broader trend.
“Our extensive discussions with Uber since the emergence of this trend in Q2 have clarified that we really have differing views about the operating model to scale our shared autonomous fleet,” Kashani said on the call, adding that those differences span areas like fleet coordination and merchant integration.
