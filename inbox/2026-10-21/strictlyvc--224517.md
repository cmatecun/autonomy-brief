## Top News

A **New Mexico judge** ordered **Meta** to pay $25 billion over millions of violations of state **consumer-protection laws** tied to misleading users about privacy, a judgment the state’s attorney general says appears to be the largest of its kind and that is almost certain to be appealed. The Washington Post has [more here](https://www.washingtonpost.com/technology/2026/10/21/meta-ordered-pay-25b-new-mexico-misleading-users-privacy/).

AI has uncovered two serious, roughly 20-year-old security flaws in **PHP**, the programming language behind about 70% of websites. Security firm Calif found one using **OpenAI**’s GPT-5.6 Sol. Separately, **Google**’s Wiz team found vulnerabilities using its Wiz Atlas AI agent, Gemini 4 Argon, and GPT-5.6 Sol. The **NSA** is now telling site owners who can’t patch right away to take their sites offline until they can. TechCrunch has [more here](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/21/php-armageddon-openai-googles-wiz-find-ancient-holes-panicking-the-internet/).

**Security teams **are scrambling after eight intermediate **TLS certificates** - digital credentials browsers and apps use to verify websites and secure encrypted connections - were found to have **catastrophically weak private keys**, potentially allowing attackers to mint trusted credentials for virtually any site. Ars Technica has [more here](https://arstechnica.com/security/2026/10/the-internet-goes-on-high-alert-after-discovery-of-8-compromised-tls-certs/).

## Meta Wants to Pay for Its Broad AI Buildout with a Newly Created Class of Stock

View image: (https://media.beehiiv.com/cdn-cgi/image/fit=scale-down,format=auto,onerror=redirect,quality=80/uploads/asset/file/9e5dd730-706a-4be0-aa83-5a1edd33377a/mark-zuckerberg-muse-charm-getty.jpeg?t=1792645341)
Caption: Image Credits: Minh Connors / Bloomberg / Getty Images

**By Lucas Ropek**

Meta is making a big push into AI, and it’s creating a new kind of stock that could help pay for it.

On Wednesday, as the company reported its third-quarter earnings, Semafor [reported](https://www.semafor.com/article/10/21/2026/meta-plans-nonvoting-shares-eyeing-more-stock-sales-to-fund-its-ai-dreams) the company might issue non-voting stock, meaning shares that don’t give their owners a vote on company decisions, to help fund its push into artificial intelligence.

During Meta’s earnings call, the company confirmed that reporting. Meta’s chief financial officer, Susan Li, said the company plans to “create a new class of non-voting capital stock, which will be distributed to all existing shareholders through a stock dividend.”

Li later added that the company “created this new class of shares with the purpose of creating a publicly traded non-voting stock usable for capital raising, employee compensation, acquisitions, and other general corporate purposes while preserving the voting power of existing Class A and B shareholders.”

The shares will allow the company to maintain its current “founder-led structure,” she added. They will also expand its “funding tools and give us flexibility to invest behind the unprecedented momentum we’re seeing across every part of our business, driven by AI.”

Meta’s earnings were strong this past quarter, beating Wall Street expectations. The company made $64 billion in revenue, a jump of 26% from a year earlier. Its costs and expenses also rose too, however, by 68% year over year.
