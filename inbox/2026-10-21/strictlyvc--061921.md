## SpaceX and Tesla Boards Form Special Committees to Review Merger

View image: (https://media.beehiiv.com/cdn-cgi/image/fit=scale-down,format=auto,onerror=redirect,quality=80/uploads/asset/file/5b989670-fe48-4d50-a9c0-d6dc7b8edb5e/space-tesla-cybertruck.jpeg?t=1792559782)
Caption: Image Credits: Ethan Swope / Bloomberg / Getty Images

**By Kirsten Korosec**

The boards of SpaceX and Tesla have each formed special committees to review a possible merger. The special committees, which consist of independent directors, were created at the request of shareholders, according to Elon Musk, CEO and the largest individual shareholder of both companies.

“Because I’m a conflicted party, I can’t actually be in the discussions of the special committees, so it’s this sort of a … It’s an odd kabuki dance where two groups of people are talking, and I can’t be in the conversation,” Musk said in response to a question during SpaceX’s third-quarter earnings call.

Musk didn’t name the members of these special committees, only noting that they were independent directors. Tesla has seven independent board members under Nasdaq’s listing standards, including board chair Robyn Denholm; Ira Ehrenpreis, who is also a committee chair; former Tesla CTO JB Straubel; Joe Gebbia; Jack Hartung; James Murdoch; and Kathleen Wilson-Thompson, according to the company’s [October 19 proxy filing](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1318605/000110465926116700/tm267596-1_def14a.htm).

Neither Musk nor his brother Kimbal Musk is independent. Denholm, Gebbia, Ehrenpreis, Hartung, Murdoch, and Wilson-Thompson sit on a variety of established committees overseeing audit, compensation, and corporate governance.

SpaceX lists [six independent directors](https://ir.spacex.com/leadership/), including Ehrenpreis, who is also the chair of the company’s compensation and nominating committee. Randy Glein, Donald Harrison, Steve Jurvetson, Luke Nosek, and Roelof Botha are also considered independent directors.

Antonio Gracias, Musk, and SpaceX president and chief operating officer Gwynne Shotwell are also on the board, but not considered independent. Gracias, who once sat on Tesla’s board, founded and leads Valor Equity Partners, which has a [3.4% stake](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-16/musk-backer-valor-hands-investors-8-5-billion-of-spacex-stock) in SpaceX. Gracias’ firm also financed [SpaceX’s AI hardware](https://www.reuters.com/legal/transactional/spacex-reveals-musk-company-links-cybertrucks-jets-stock-investments-2026-05-21/) purchases through leaseback arrangements.

Read more (https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/20/spacex-and-tesla-boards-form-special-committees-to-review-merger/)

## Massive Fundings

**A****rda**, a 10-month-old San Francisco startup that uses AI agents to turn product designs into assembly plans and update factory instructions as designs change, raised a $160 million Series A round at a $1.5 billion valuation. The deal was co-led by **Altimeter**, **Accel**, and **Khosla Ventures**, with **XYZ Venture Capital**, **8VC**, **SVAngel**, and **Felicis** also chiming in. The company has raised a total of $232 million. _[More here](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20261020574393/en/Arda-Emerges-from-Stealth-to-Speed-Up-American-Manufacturing)_.

**ASI**, an eight-year-old Boston startup that builds simulation and monitoring software to forecast disruptions across air traffic, power grids, and military operations, raised a $325 million Series C round at a $3 billion post-money valuation. The round was led by **Spark Capital**, with additional support from **Citadel Securities**, **Meritech Capital**, and **Craft Ventures**. Reuters has [more here](https://www.reuters.com/business/ai-startup-asi-valued-3-billion-latest-funding-round-2026-10-20/).
