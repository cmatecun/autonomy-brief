Apple HomeView
--------------

From Bloomberg ( https://www.bloomberg.com/news/features/2026-10-13/apple-unveils-299-homeview-smart-home-hub-new-apple-tv-4k-homepod-mini-2 ) :

Apple Inc. introduced a new device called the HomeView, a $299 display that serves as the centerpiece of its push to challenge Amazon.com Inc. and Alphabet Inc.’s Google in the smart home market. The product, which will be available Nov. 13, relies mainly on hands-free voice commands and uses facial recognition to provide a personalized software experience for each member of the household. There are no physical buttons on the HomeView, though its 7-inch touch screen has a grid of apps that will instantly feel familiar to iPhone and iPad users.

Apple is framing the HomeView as the ideal showcase for its Siri AI assistant: Using a built-in camera, the device can instantly recognize up to six different users and give personalized responses for each individual, whether they’re using it as a cooking companion, videoconferencing with a relative or just listening to music through its speakers.

* I’m not necessarily opposed to showing stressful situations — which is exactly what this morning get-out-the-door sequence is — but this framing only works if the product is a salve; in this case fighting over the HomeView, while intended to showcase how it serves multiple people, actually accentuates the stress.
* It’s actually kind of charming that the entire family is together in the morning before school; given that, another screen feels like the last thing one might want to add?
* Most of the use cases highlighted in this video, particularly private messages, are obviously better suited by a private iPhone.

This last point is more profound than it might seem: John Gruber reported on Daring Fireball ( https://daringfireball.net/linked/2026/10/13/homeview ) that for the HomeView facial recognition to work you need to have a recently unlocked “registered personal device” on the same network; in other words, not only are some of these use cases obviously better on an iPhone, you would have needed to use an iPhone relatively recently to even unlock them.

The New Apple TV
----------------

From The Verge ( https://www.theverge.com/tech/1009663/apple-tv-4k-siri-ai-microphones-4th-gen-announced ) :

Apple is introducing a refreshed Apple TV 4K today with a pair of interesting new hardware features: far-field microphones to talk to Siri hands-free and Find My built into the remote. The fourth-gen Apple TV 4K, announced at Apple’s Welcome Home event in New York City, is the first model to support the Siri AI voice assistant and has a new home theatre feature that lets you pair up to four second-gen HomePods or HomePod Mini 2s for surround sound.

The new Apple TV 4K costs $299, more than twice the price the prior-generation Apple TV 4K debuted at and $50 more than the current top-of-the-line model (which is being discontinued). The fourth-gen model matches that version’s specs with 128GB of storage, an Ethernet port, and support for Thread and Matter, but it’s getting a bump up to Wi-Fi 7 and Bluetooth 6. It will be available on November 13th, with preorders opening today, October 13th.

This tweet about the Apple TV’s price point made me laugh:

X Post ( https://x.com/i/web/status/2110034483520954712 )

I think it’s a longstanding mistake by Apple to have never released an Apple TV stick; not only has the Apple TV long not been price competitive, the form factor is actually sub-optimal for many use cases, including travel. To that end, however, if Apple is going to commit to this form factor, then I think going the other way and making the Apple TV more capable for more money is defensible; the product wasn’t price competitive even before last summer’s price increases.

The reason to highlight the Apple TV, however, is that it gives important context to the HomeView. Specifically, what today’s launches represent is a commitment to Siri AI in the home. Far-field microphones is a genuinely interesting feature, one that presupposes that voice is a primary interaction method; I also know that they aren’t cheap.

There are, however, some problems with this story. First, the reason I know that far-field microphones are not cheap is that I have specced them out; my use case, however, is to create a voice interface to AI agents that are far more capable than Siri AI. I plan on using these voice interfaces to control the Apple TV’s in my rack (and I’m relieved I have plenty of the previous generation Apple TVs; I like the interface and the chip’s capabilities and my voice won’t reach the server room).

Again, I am not the target customer for these products, as I explained last week; the question is how many people are both ready to spend a significant amount of money on these products and also have no interest in being on the leading edge of AI capabilities?

The App Story
-------------

From The Verge ( https://www.theverge.com/tech/1009704/apple-homeview-smart-display-first-look ) :

While the HomeView functions as an Apple Home Hub — it enables local control of supported devices, creation of scenes and automations, and support for HomeKit Secure Video — the device feels less like a focused smart home controller and more like a way to use your iPhone less when you’re at home.

The HomeView supports a number of Apple’s own apps, including Calendar, Reminders, Messages, and Music. Apple says it also works with third-party audio and video apps that support the CarPlay architecture. A few audio apps, such as Spotify and Audible, are available, but no video apps yet…

The HomeView’s key software feature is intelligent switching that lets the screen move from a household device to a personal device. It uses facial recognition and the presence of your recently unlocked iPhone on the home Wi-Fi network to authenticate you. Once it knows who you are, it can show your customized HomeView layout and, if you have an AI-capable iPhone, use personal context to pull up that recipe Mom texted you or play that song your friend sent. Without recognizing the user, Siri AI on HomeView can show the live doorbell feed, answer general questions, control some smart devices, display recipes, and play audio and video.

This is a very interesting app story that reinforces a few recent Stratechery observations, particularly from this Daily Update after the recent iPhone launch ( https://stratechery.com/2026/the-iphone-duo-the-intelligent-personal-hub-apple-watch-audio-intelligence/?access_token=eyJhbGciOiJFUzI1NiIsImtpZCI6IkxzanFRcEhoMUJUdmU0djJQUUVuejIiLCJ0eXAiOiJKV1QifQ.eyJhdWQiOiJzdHJhdGVjaGVyeS5wYXNzcG9ydC5vbmxpbmUiLCJhenAiOiJIS0xjUzREd1Nod1AyWURLYmZQV00xIiwiZW50Ijp7InVyaSI6WyJodHRwczovL3N0cmF0ZWNoZXJ5LmNvbS8yMDI2L3RoZS1pcGhvbmUtZHVvLXRoZS1pbnRlbGxpZ2VudC1wZXJzb25hbC1odWItYXBwbGUtd2F0Y2gtYXVkaW8taW50ZWxsaWdlbmNlLyJdfSwiZXhwIjoxNzk0NTY0MTMyLCJpYXQiOjE3OTE5NzIxMzIsImlzcyI6Imh0dHBzOi8vYXBwLnBhc3Nwb3J0Lm9ubGluZS9vYXV0aCIsInNjb3BlIjoiZmVlZDpyZWFkIGFydGljbGU6cmVhZCBhc3NldDpyZWFkIGNhdGVnb3J5OnJlYWQgZW50aXRsZW1lbnRzIiwic3ViIjoiMDE5NTE2ZjEtYTM0Ni03NjE0LTllOTYtYjYzYmQxMWZlODU2IiwidXNlIjoiYWNjZXNzIn0.4Zv_pKoIDbFoW6KYavcQpodRtYf3uaw0z-O5Iyaif2T_fAn_LZ-WJh-VC8mwsZTmndi7MDKAQ51jqJQzKeEIfg ) :

* First, Apple is, for better or for worse, all in on the iPhone as the “Intelligent Personal Hub”. This isn’t a surprise, given the importance of the iPhone to Apple, but if there is a world where Thin Is In ( https://stratechery.com/2026/thin-is-in/?access_token=eyJhbGciOiJFUzI1NiIsImtpZCI6IkxzanFRcEhoMUJUdmU0djJQUUVuejIiLCJ0eXAiOiJKV1QifQ.eyJhdWQiOiJzdHJhdGVjaGVyeS5wYXNzcG9ydC5vbmxpbmUiLCJhenAiOiJIS0xjUzREd1Nod1AyWURLYmZQV00xIiwiZW50Ijp7InVyaSI6WyJodHRwczovL3N0cmF0ZWNoZXJ5LmNvbS8yMDI2L3RoaW4taXMtaW4vIl19LCJleHAiOjE3OTQ1NjQxMzIsImlhdCI6MTc5MTk3MjEzMiwiaXNzIjoiaHR0cHM6Ly9hcHAucGFzc3BvcnQub25saW5lL29hdXRoIiwic2NvcGUiOiJmZWVkOnJlYWQgYXJ0aWNsZTpyZWFkIGFzc2V0OnJlYWQgY2F0ZWdvcnk6cmVhZCBlbnRpdGxlbWVudHMiLCJzdWIiOiIwMTk1MTZmMS1hMzQ2LTc2MTQtOWU5Ni1iNjNiZDExZmU4NTYiLCJ1c2UiOiJhY2Nlc3MifQ.xE2JzVyVGkk2rbrk7iafM5XzzLHA00e1Re71Bdg4boZR6xbPHlEMdSUEZrl2-_DS0DiN0thTWzRz6b_KiGJWNQ ) , then Apple’s commitment to the last paradigm could be a winner’s curse ( https://stratechery.com/2025/paradigm-shifts-and-the-winners-curse/?access_token=eyJhbGciOiJFUzI1NiIsImtpZCI6IkxzanFRcEhoMUJUdmU0djJQUUVuejIiLCJ0eXAiOiJKV1QifQ.eyJhdWQiOiJzdHJhdGVjaGVyeS5wYXNzcG9ydC5vbmxpbmUiLCJhenAiOiJIS0xjUzREd1Nod1AyWURLYmZQV00xIiwiZW50Ijp7InVyaSI6WyJodHRwczovL3N0cmF0ZWNoZXJ5LmNvbS8yMDI1L3BhcmFkaWdtLXNoaWZ0cy1hbmQtdGhlLXdpbm5lcnMtY3Vyc2UvIl19LCJleHAiOjE3OTQ1NjQxMzIsImlhdCI6MTc5MTk3MjEzMiwiaXNzIjoiaHR0cHM6Ly9hcHAucGFzc3BvcnQub25saW5lL29hdXRoIiwic2NvcGUiOiJmZWVkOnJlYWQgYXJ0aWNsZTpyZWFkIGFzc2V0OnJlYWQgY2F0ZWdvcnk6cmVhZCBlbnRpdGxlbWVudHMiLCJzdWIiOiIwMTk1MTZmMS1hMzQ2LTc2MTQtOWU5Ni1iNjNiZDExZmU4NTYiLCJ1c2UiOiJhY2Nlc3MifQ.2aJpdxDV2OdAXTTz_HB-Oh6re_qv7Hi0opBWAA67pU-_GxLih5d8mRT0Iz1sBePyl8-FXxa1b6NIATNnmOmIKA ).
* Second, Apple is, once again, leaning on its developers to do their part to deliver on its new use cases; if apps are the key to the future then this is fine, since Apple has the best ecosystem. However, if apps are superfluous in the AI era then this presents a lowest common denominator drag on the user experience (i.e. compared to an agent with browser use).

One encouraging note is that the LG and Schneider Electric device story I wrote about last week ( https://stratechery.com/2026/apple-and-lg-the-house-for-everyone-else-agent-standards-and-amazon/?access_token=eyJhbGciOiJFUzI1NiIsImtpZCI6IkxzanFRcEhoMUJUdmU0djJQUUVuejIiLCJ0eXAiOiJKV1QifQ.eyJhdWQiOiJzdHJhdGVjaGVyeS5wYXNzcG9ydC5vbmxpbmUiLCJhenAiOiJIS0xjUzREd1Nod1AyWURLYmZQV00xIiwiZW50Ijp7InVyaSI6WyJodHRwczovL3N0cmF0ZWNoZXJ5LmNvbS8yMDI2L2FwcGxlLWFuZC1sZy10aGUtaG91c2UtZm9yLWV2ZXJ5b25lLWVsc2UtYWdlbnQtc3RhbmRhcmRzLWFuZC1hbWF6b24vIl19LCJleHAiOjE3OTQ1NjQxMzIsImlhdCI6MTc5MTk3MjEzMiwiaXNzIjoiaHR0cHM6Ly9hcHAucGFzc3BvcnQub25saW5lL29hdXRoIiwic2NvcGUiOiJmZWVkOnJlYWQgYXJ0aWNsZTpyZWFkIGFzc2V0OnJlYWQgY2F0ZWdvcnk6cmVhZCBlbnRpdGxlbWVudHMiLCJzdWIiOiIwMTk1MTZmMS1hMzQ2LTc2MTQtOWU5Ni1iNjNiZDExZmU4NTYiLCJ1c2UiOiJhY2Nlc3MifQ.ghT3TmN7TfJvHbGffAi94YGFHg3eP1W1q_g2mFPPBmquz8Cn222OFVh6g6HTYLO8vFXirDVwNuatJB8v_BB8HQ ) is an app-less one; from the Apple Newsroom ( https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2026/10/introducing-apple-home-collection-a-new-lineup-of-smart-home-accessories/ ) :

Apple today announced Apple Home Collection, a new lineup of smart home accessories — including indoor and outdoor cameras, thermostats, temperature sensors, and a video doorbell and door lock — designed in collaboration with LG Electronics and Schneider Electric. These new accessories are simple to set up starting with one tap with iPhone, and everything is managed through the Apple Home app — no separate app or account required. Each accessory is encrypted end-to-end in Apple Home, so only users and the people they choose can access their home data.

No separate app or account required is exactly right; in an AI world, apps simply get in the way: there is the interface, and there is the hardware, and everything else can and should be abstracted away. The question for Apple is if, in this world, also having access to your iPhone is enough to make up for an AI that isn’t quite competitive. I do think integration is important; just how important is going to be put to the test.
