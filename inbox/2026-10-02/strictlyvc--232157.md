**Google** won dismissal of antitrust lawsuits from **Chegg** and **Penske Media**, dealing a setback to publishers trying to use competition law to preserve the old bargain in which search engines index their content and send traffic back in return. ## Sanders Introduces Bill To Ban The Federal Government From Using Flock

View image: (https://media.beehiiv.com/cdn-cgi/image/fit=scale-down,format=auto,onerror=redirect,quality=80/uploads/asset/file/519cb8e1-e55c-484b-b052-36feb1e2ce16/flock-protest__1_.jpeg?t=1791001046)
Caption: Image Credits: Anthony Ha / TechCrunch

**By Connie Loizos**

Flock, whose cameras scan license plates across the country, continues to draw public anger, and now Senator Bernie Sanders wants the federal government out of the business altogether.

Sanders (D-Vermont) on Friday introduced the [Ban Flock Act](https://www.sanders.senate.gov/wp-content/uploads/Ban-Flock-Act.pdf), which would bar federal agencies from using automatic license plate readers (ALPRs) or tapping into data collected by readers run by local police and private companies. Despite its title, the bill doesn’t call out nine-year-old Flock in the text but rather covers any and all ALPR systems.

The bill allows exceptions only for toll collection and for uses that Congress approves in future legislation, which would have to limit data retention to 48 hours.

State and local governments would be dinged financially for not complying if the bill became law. (Like most bills, this one faces long odds.) Starting the first fiscal year after enactment, they would lose grants from five federal departments, including the Justice Department and the Department of Homeland Security, unless they ban the tech.

Americans could also sue the federal government over violations, and state attorneys general could enforce the law.

Flock is the largest ALPR vendor in the U.S., according to Sanders, with more than 120,000 cameras. In a blog post back in February, the company — currently valued by its venture backers at north of $8 billion —  said its network processes more than 20 billion vehicle reads each month.

Under increasing pressure from communities that are [protesting](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2uE13C0iEu8) the product, [suspending their use](https://www.latimes.com/california/story/2026-07-11/lapd-suspends-use-of-flock-surveillance-cameras-over-privacy-issues), and, at an escalating rate, [canceling](https://arstechnica.com/tech-policy/2026/08/cities-terminate-flock-contracts-at-record-pace-in-august/) their contracts, Flock has tightened its rules. In August, CEO Garrett Langley, who’d previously said that how police use the cameras is up to local agencies, announced a new default data retention period of seven days, down from 30.

Read more (https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/02/sanders-introduces-bill-to-ban-the-federal-government-from-using-flock/)

## Massive Fundings

**FieldAI**, a three-year-old company based in Irvine, CA, and the Bay Area that develops software enabling robots to operate autonomously across different machines, tasks, and environments, is reportedly in talks to raise $700 million at a $10 billion valuation, five times its $2 billion valuation from last year. Business Insider has [more here](https://www.businessinsider.com/robotics-startup-fieldai-raises-at-10b-valuation-in-new-round-2026-10).

**Fortem Technologies**, a 10-year-old company based in Lindon, UT, that develops radar, command software, and autonomous interceptor drones that detect, track, and capture unauthorized drones, raised a $50 million Series B round. The deal was led by **Lockheed Martin**, with **Unusual Machines** also pitching in. _[More here](https://www.tectonicdefense.com/exclusive-counter-drone-startup-fortem-raises-50m-series-b/)_.

**Homeward**, an eight-year-old Austin startup that provides short-term financing and cash offers to homeowners buying before selling or seeking a faster home sale through real estate agents, raised a $120 million Series D round led by **Saluda Grade**, with **Continental General Insurance Company**, **Citi Ventures**, **Magnetar**, **Harmony Partners**, **Norwest**, **Adams Street Partners**, **LiveOak Ventures**, **Parker89**, **Era Ventures**, and **Javelin Venture Partners** also taking part. It also secured $330 million in asset-backed debt facilities. The company has raised a total of $360 million in equity. Crunchbase News has _[more here](https://news.crunchbase.com/real-estate-property-tech/startup-homeward-raises-120m-buy-sell-homes-ai-financing/)_.

**Reverion**, a four-year-old startup based in Eresing, Germany, that builds reversible fuel cell power plants that convert gas into electricity and surplus electricity into storable gases, raised a $174.2 million Series B round led by **Kembara**, with additional support from **Allianz**, **KfW Capital**, **Aurum Impact**, and **Carbon Equity** as well as previous investors **Extantia**, **Energy Impact Partners**, **UVC Partners**, **European Innovation Council Fund**, **alfa8**, and **Possible Ventures**. EU-Startups has _[more here](https://www.eu-startups.com/2026/09/germanys-reverion-raises-e154-2-million-to-scale-production-of-power-plants-that-generate-electricity-from-gas/)_.

**Supabase**, a six-year-old San Francisco startup that provides developers with an open-source Postgres backend for databases, authentication, storage, and server functions, raised a $150 million round led by **GIC**, with **CapitalG**, **IronArc**, and **SquarePeg** also investing. The company also acquired database infrastructure startup Turso. [More here](https://www.citybiz.co/article/913344/supabase-raises-150-million-acquires-turso-to-scale-agentic-databases/).

**TriGemX Bio**, an 11-year-old New York company that develops an oral drug that blocks a sensory-signaling protein to treat acute migraines, raised a $94 million round co-led by **The** **Column Group**, **TCGX**, and **Viking Global Investors**. _[More here](https://www.citybiz.co/article/913030/trigemx-bio-raises-94-million-to-advance-migraine-drug-through-phase-3/)_.
