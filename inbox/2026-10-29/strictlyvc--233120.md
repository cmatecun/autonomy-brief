## Ex-Palantir Execs Launch Dream With $17M From A16z To Build AI World Models For Construction

View image: (https://media.beehiiv.com/cdn-cgi/image/fit=scale-down,format=auto,onerror=redirect,quality=80/uploads/asset/file/959dfe19-9ff4-4885-9298-9aaf0a789880/technology-demo.jpeg?t=1793337519)
Caption: Image Credits: Dream

**By Marina Temkin**

Andy Elder and Arjun Mathur were Stanford roommates who both studied computer science before spending a decade at Palantir. Elder led product design for a team of 800 people building software for U.S. defense and intelligence agencies, while Mathur served as a senior vice president in the company’s commercial practice. When the friends decided to build a startup early last year, there was no question they would do it together.

Despite having no direct background in architecture, Elder and Mathur were troubled by a growing problem: Fewer buildings are constructed in the U.S. today than a [_few decades ago_](https://www.richmondfed.org/publications/research/economic_brief/2025/eb_25-31), as development costs have surged.

“We’ve gone from dial-up internet to superintelligent AI, but our ability to build the physical world around us has actually decreased,” Mathur told TechCrunch.

Believing AI can boost productivity across the architecture, engineering, and construction (AEC) industry, they set out to build [_Dream_](https://www.dreamhq.com/), a startup developing built world models that automate building design and engineering. Dream has emerged from stealth with $17 million in seed funding led by Andreessen Horowitz, alongside other backers, including SV Angel, XYZ, and Zigg Capital. The startup aims to replace cumbersome, outdated AEC tools like Autodesk’s AutoCAD and Revit.

If you sit next to an architect or a structural engineer, you’ll notice that they are still “drawing” every piece of their designs part by part on legacy software, Mathur said. “AI really plays no role in that equation.”

While a standard LLM like ChatGPT can generate an image of a building, the result won’t be structurally sound. Dream, by contrast, is grounded in real physics and construction standards. Just as LLMs learn from text, Dream’s world model is trained on more than 100 million square feet of building data gathered from permit archives across the United States.

Read more (https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/29/ex-palantir-execs-launch-dream-with-17m-from-a16z-to-build-ai-world-models-for-construction/)
