## Is Jensen Huang’s Job on the Line? Yale’s Jeffrey Sonnenfeld Estimates It’s ‘About a 50-50’

View image: (https://media.beehiiv.com/cdn-cgi/image/fit=scale-down,format=auto,onerror=redirect,quality=80/uploads/asset/file/e211dbd8-175f-49c4-bcdf-2466438d18f7/Jensen-Huang.jpeg?t=1794246331)
Caption: Image Credits: Tomohiro Ohsumi / Getty Images

**By Connie Loizos**

Nvidia’s board announced Sunday that it’s launching an [_independent investigation_](https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/statement-from-nvidia-board-of-directors) after CEO Jensen Huang told staff he was unfaithful to his wife. But this isn’t a case of just another CEO having an extramarital affair. The romantic interest is Megaspeed executive Alice Huang, whom U.S. officials believe is a Chinese spy who was feeding information back to Beijing. In short, the stakes reach well beyond Nvidia, given how many tech and financial partners depend on its place at the center of the AI economy. “I’m responsible for an [_entire industry_](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/videos/2026-11-09/jensen-huang-on-who-could-succeed-him-as-nvidia-ceo-video),” Huang recently told Bloomberg in a sit-down.

A spokesperson for the Chinese Embassy in Washington dismissed the allegations in late October as “[_groundless smears and slanders_](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/10/29/us/politics/nvidia-china-jensen-huang-trump.html),” and no one has been charged. But U.S. intelligence officials reportedly briefed President Donald Trump about their concerns a year ago, and unnamed senior officials inside the Trump administration told the Times that they doubt Huang could have [_remained unaware_](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/10/29/us/politics/nvidia-china-jensen-huang-trump.html) all that time of Alice Huang’s intelligence-gathering role for China. When Trump was first briefed, the Times reported, officials didn’t believe Huang knew, and it isn’t clear whether their assessment has changed. The board, meanwhile, says it sees no sign that national security or company secrets were compromised.

The question is whether Huang can survive this. To think through what could come next, this editor spoke Sunday with famed professor Jeffrey Sonnenfeld, senior associate dean for leadership studies at the Yale School of Management and founder of the Chief Executive Leadership Institute, which has hosted Huang at its CEO summits. Sonnenfeld likes Huang, he said, and he called Huang’s career salvageable but put the odds at “about a 50-50.” He also had a lot more food for thought.

First, the damage. Until now, Huang has been “revered and trusted by everybody in technology,” Sonnenfeld noted. But he called this episode “remarkably poor judgment.” Even if Huang was merely naive, he said, “any employee in Nvidia other than him would be fired over this,” and the recklessness alone would mean “an irretrievable loss of confidence.”

Sonnenfeld suggested that Huang’s saving grace could be what social psychologists call [_idiosyncrasy credits_](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34990180/), if he can prove he was being duped. The term comes from Edwin Hollander, a social psychologist who proposed in a 1958 paper that people earn latitude in a group by showing competence and following its rules. Huang has been earning latitude since he co-founded Nvidia in 1993. But all that goodwill can run out when a screw-up looks self-serving or touches the group’s core norms. Here that group includes Nvidia’s board, its roughly 42,000 employees who are bound by security rules, and, in a looser sense, the U.S. government, given it restricts its exports.

We also talked about precedents, except, as it turns out, there really are none for what’s unfolding right now. Executives who survive personal scandals sometimes pick up where they left off at another company, Sonnenfeld observed. Former HP CEO Mark Hurd is one example. Between 2005 and 2010, he drove HP to the top of the tech industry by revenue, then was pressured to resign after he filed inaccurate expense reports that hid a personal relationship with a contractor. Oracle hired him about a month later. Huang has no equivalent because Nvidia is his company.

## Massive Fundings

**Mavria**, a startup founded this year and based in Texas that mass-produces modular data centers designed to be shipped by truck and rapidly deployed, has raised $130 million in total funding and was recently valued at $1 billion. Investors include **Paradigm**, **Liquid 2**, **Critical Frontier**, and **AMP**. The Wall Street Journal has [more here](https://www.wsj.com/business/energy-oil/they-led-an-energy-department-overhaul-now-they-plan-to-build-data-centers-914f47c2).

**Nominal**, a four-year-old Austin startup that connects hardware data and engineering context so engineers and AI agents can analyze, test, and monitor complex systems, raised a $240 million Series C round at a $3 billion valuation, triple the valuation of the Series B extension it raised in March. The deal was co-led by **Lightspeed Venture Partners** and **Insight Partners**, with **Lux Capital**, **Sequoia**, **General Catalyst**, **Founders Fund**, **Avenir**, **Salesforce Ventures**, **M13**, **Lockheed Martin Ventures**, **Red Glass Ventures**, **PSP Growth**, and **Deployment Capital** also stepping up. TechCrunch has [more here](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/09/ai-hardware-startup-nominal-triples-valuation-to-3b-in-8-months/). 

**Onodrim**, an Amsterdam startup founded this year that acquires controlling stakes in established European defense, aerospace, and manufacturing companies and provides technology and operational support, raised a $300 million Series A round at a $1.3 billion post-money valuation. The deal was led by **Lux Capital**, with previous investors **General Catalyst**, **Lakestar**, **Altimeter**, **Strike Capital**, **137 Ventures**, **8090**, and **Expeditions** also engaging. Tech Funding News has [_more here_](https://techfundingnews.com/onodrim-300m-series-a-european-defence-acquisitions/).

**Project Omega**, a one-year-old startup based in Newport, RI, that recycles used nuclear fuel into long-duration power sources, reactor fuel, and commercial, medical, and scientific isotopes, raised an $80 million Series A round led by **B Capital**, with **noa VC** and **645 Ventures** as well as previous investors **Starship Ventures**, **Mantis Ventures**, **Decisive Point**, **Virta Ventures**, **Pace Ventures**, **Essential Capital**, and **Washington Harbour Partners** also investing. Tectonic Defense has [_more here_](https://www.tectonicdefense.com/project-omega-raises-80m-to-turn-nuclear-waste-into-the-pentagons-next-power-supply/).

**Spines**, a five-year-old startup based in Boynton Beach, FL, that uses AI agents and publishing professionals to edit, design, translate, distribute, and market authors' books, raised a $29 million Series A-1 round led by **Zeev Ventures**, with **Dan Senor** as well as previous investors **Aleph**, **NordStar**, **M-Fund**, and **LionTree** also pitching in. It also secured a $35 million non-dilutive growth financing facility from **Twelve**. The company has raised a total of $86 million. CTech has [_more here_](https://www.calcalistech.com/ctechnews/article/h1civuyrge).
