Jensen Secures Top Spot on Naughty List
Insane story over the weekend as Bloomberg reported that: “Nvidia Corp. Chief Executive Officer Jensen Huang admitted he had an affair with a woman identified in a series of reports as a suspected Chinese spy, plunging the chip colossus into a crisis like none before in its three-decade history.”
Alice Huang (no relation to Jensen) has been a suspected spy for a while now, Culper research called it out back in May:
And Byron Wan posted allegations over a year ago:
There are a bunch of downstream implications from this, but none of Jensen’s moves over the past year feel wildly misaligned with his long term position as a global capitalist. Diversifying revenue internationally has always made sense if you don’t believe in AI existential risk or technology as a significant geopolitical football. That being said, it certainly seems like China thinks it’s a significant geopolitical football. The investigations are still on-going, so making sense of Alice Huang’s career is a bit of a mess, just look at this summary [ https://substack.com/redirect/c6a87688-ab0f-48cf-9486-6813ba580217?j=eyJ1IjoiMXlnZjcifQ.KOUIeBfeCRzhIwFnKXyQigR4j0VpF-SZzascYhSZunw ]:
According to the November 9 Wall Street Journal investigation, Alice Huang began her career as a Chinese state-media journalist before moving into finance, including positions at Chinese financial firms and HSBC. In early 2023, she took control of Megaspeed, a Singapore company spun out of Chinese gaming business 7Road, and became its managing director, building it into an Nvidia AI-compute provider serving customers including Alibaba. Although she formally relinquished ownership and resigned as director in November 2023, when Singaporean businessman James Tan took over, she apparently continued exercising significant operational leadership, representing Megaspeed at Nvidia’s California conference in early 2024, meeting Jensen Huang at Computex in June 2024, and publicly interacting with him again in Taiwan in May 2025. A business partner told the WSJ she was still introducing herself as Megaspeed’s CEO in August 2025. Meanwhile, Megaspeed was acquiring advanced Nvidia GPUs, prompting a U.S. Commerce Department investigation beginning in late 2024 into whether restricted chips were being diverted to China. U.S. officials also suspect Alice of passing information to Chinese intelligence, and Nvidia is investigating whether Jensen Huang was targeted through their personal relationship. The key unresolved question is whether the November 2023 sale represented a genuine transfer of control or primarily a change in legal ownership while Alice continued running the business. The WSJ does not establish that the sale was a sham, that chips were illegally diverted, or that the espionage allegations are proven; Megaspeed and China deny wrongdoing.
A resume this confusing couldn’t normally raise a seed round from a serious venture capital firm, and yet she was allegedly moving billions of dollars worth of GPUs around the globe.
The big question that I’m hoping the investigations will answer (beyond the obvious “is she a spy or not?”), is: was this an espionage or sabotage operation? Meaning: was the goal to get access to Jensen’s phone, computer, files, etc, effectively stealing information about Nvidia’s business and technology, or was the goal to pressure Jensen into changing his strategy, sabotage his operation, or something else? It could be both of course, but understanding the scope of the damage to American semiconductor dominance is more important than ever now.
