Jensen Huang’s Affair
---------------------

From Bloomberg ( https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-08/nvidia-board-to-probe-spying-case-after-ceo-jensen-huang-admits-infidelity ) :

Nvidia Corp. Chief Executive Officer Jensen Huang admitted he had an affair with a woman identified in a series of reports as a suspected Chinese spy, plunging the chip colossus into a crisis like none before in its three-decade history. Huang disclosed the romantic relationship in a memo to employees on Sunday, saying the Nvidia board was launching an investigation. He had previously said there was nothing inappropriate about his relationship with the woman, who worked for a Singapore-based customer.

It was a humbling moment for an executive who became one of Silicon Valley’s biggest celebrities in recent years. Huang has crisscrossed the globe promoting artificial intelligence computing — a field his company dominates — drawing throngs of fans swept up in “Jensanity.” His relentless appearances, featuring science lectures, dad jokes and his signature leather jackets, have turned him into AI’s main cheerleader.

The incident also threatens to further roil an AI market shaken by concerns about a slowdown in data center spending. Nvidia makes chips that are crucial to AI computing equipment and is one of the world’s most valuable businesses. Turmoil within the company threatens to reverberate through the broader industry. Nvidia has emerged as a key source of financing for AI infrastructure as well. Huang has worked with some of Wall Street’s biggest firms on a $500 billion effort to help companies rapidly build out their equipment, and he’s funneled money into bolstering the AI supply chain and fledgling customers.

That’s a good overview of the stakes: it’s not just Nvidia products, but also Nvidia financing ( https://stratechery.com/2026/nvidias-risky-business/?access_token=eyJhbGciOiJFUzI1NiIsImtpZCI6IkxzanFRcEhoMUJUdmU0djJQUUVuejIiLCJ0eXAiOiJKV1QifQ.eyJhdWQiOiJzdHJhdGVjaGVyeS5wYXNzcG9ydC5vbmxpbmUiLCJhenAiOiJIS0xjUzREd1Nod1AyWURLYmZQV00xIiwiZW50Ijp7InVyaSI6WyJodHRwczovL3N0cmF0ZWNoZXJ5LmNvbS8yMDI2L252aWRpYXMtcmlza3ktYnVzaW5lc3MvIl19LCJleHAiOjE3OTY4MTQxMjksImlhdCI6MTc5NDIyMjEyOSwiaXNzIjoiaHR0cHM6Ly9hcHAucGFzc3BvcnQub25saW5lL29hdXRoIiwic2NvcGUiOiJmZWVkOnJlYWQgYXJ0aWNsZTpyZWFkIGFzc2V0OnJlYWQgY2F0ZWdvcnk6cmVhZCBlbnRpdGxlbWVudHMiLCJzdWIiOiIwMTk1MTZmMS1hMzQ2LTc2MTQtOWU5Ni1iNjNiZDExZmU4NTYiLCJ1c2UiOiJhY2Nlc3MifQ.yDsO-Bhi2GPNhAZywuR20fyQCroaTjc8zsB8fuSk7C-gSMD2I1EjOV00rb4_UOgEN2_fnDmp589GOxnl0QfZ4g ). Meanwhile, the most prominent in those “series of reports” was this New York Times article ( https://www.nytimes.com/2026/11/08/us/politics/rubio-nvidia-ceo-chinese-spy.html ) :

Secretary of State Marco Rubio oversaw the drafting of the highly classified memo this past spring warning top Trump administration officials that Nvidia’s chief executive, Jensen Huang, was in a close relationship with a Chinese spy, multiple people briefed on the matter said. The document was based on classified intelligence from electronic surveillance of Alice Huang, a Chinese citizen who was a senior executive at a Singapore-based company that was buying Nvidia’s advanced artificial intelligence chips and being investigated for illegally smuggling them into China. U.S. officials had concluded that Ms. Huang was in a close relationship with the Nvidia chief, who is no relation, and that she had been recruited to spy by China’s top intelligence agency, the Ministry of State Security…

Despite his status as an A.I. pioneer and head of one of the world’s most valuable companies, Mr. Huang was initially left off the list of major business and tech executives invited to join Mr. Trump in China. It is unclear whether the exclusion was connected to the memo. By that point, a number of people inside the administration were concerned about Mr. Huang’s influence on the president’s policy of only lightly regulating A.I., people briefed on the matter said…

This strikes me as the most important paragraph. First, it was odd that Huang wasn’t invited on the trip; second, it was notable that Trump made a big deal out of inviting him anyways; third, I think we have our best clue as far as who is the most interested in this news becoming public, and it’s not necessarily China. Continuing with the story:

The president chose at that point not to take any action. He has viewed Nvidia’s chips as integral to America’s competition with China in A.I. He sees Nvidia’s success as crucial to maintaining a robust stock market. Asked about Mr. Huang’s admission to his company about his affair with Ms. Huang, Mr. Trump told reporters on Sunday evening that he did not regret his own lack of action. “No, I don’t think so,” the president said. He added that he believed Mr. Huang should stay on as Nvidia’s chief executive. “I think he’s an honorable guy. I think he probably has an excuse for it. You have to hear it,” Mr. Trump said.

Huang didn’t offer much of an excuse in an email to all Nvidia employees:

To my colleagues at NVIDIA,

I owe my family and all of you an apology.

I was unfaithful to my wife. My actions hurt her and our children. There is no excuse for what I did. I am deeply sorry.

Throughout my life, my family has given me love and support. They deserve my honesty and loyalty. Instead, I let them down and damaged the life we built together. I take full responsibility for what I did and the hurt I caused, and I accept the criticism that comes with it.

To my wife and family: I love you. I know an apology cannot undo what I have done. I hope our family will heal and that, over time, I can earn back your trust.

To my colleagues at NVIDIA, including those I have worked alongside for decades, and to our board, partners, and shareholders around the world: you deserve better from me. Leading NVIDIA is the privilege of my life. My responsibility is to serve you and be worthy of your trust. I have fallen short of that responsibility. I am fully committed to NVIDIA and will work to earn back your confidence.

Regarding the national security concerns raised in news reports, NVIDIA’s Board of Directors has initiated an independent investigation. I support the investigation and will cooperate fully. I love our country and our company. I am committed to protecting NVIDIA and supporting America’s national security. I am proud and grateful to be an American.

My family bears no responsibility for my choices. I do. Please give them privacy.

Jensen

The Board of Directors issued a statement:

The NVIDIA Board is initiating an independent investigation in light of recent reporting that founder and CEO Jensen Huang has been the target of a foreign intelligence effort.

A committee of independent directors, assisted by outside independent counsel, will oversee the investigation.

The Board prioritizes U.S. national and economic security, which is paramount to the interests of NVIDIA and its shareholders.

While the Board has no reason to believe that either national security or NVIDIA secrets were compromised, the Board’s inquiry will examine all available information.

Jensen fully supports the review and has committed to cooperating with it.

The Board has confidence in Jensen and his leadership team and will provide further updates as appropriate.

Quite a Sunday — and if this news were contained to just this Sunday, then this would all probably blow over. But it’s not.

From the report:

Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang has not only vouched for Megaspeed, but seems to have quite literally walked alongside Megaspeed at every step, while maintaining an uncomfortably close relationship with Megaspeed’s Alice Huang, a Chinese national. A current Megaspeed employee told us that Megaspeed is “one of the most important customers” for YTL, but that the parties conceal the relationship, saying, “Megaspeed is able to get the supply of the GPUs and supply to the Chinese companies. They don’t want the media attention so that it doesn’t disrupt their business plans in the data centers.”

There is more to the report than just Alice Huang, but the primary focus is Megaspeed in support of Culper’s contention that Nvidia actually still has a sizable China business and is very much aware of that fact. It’s pretty damning.

Huang’s Righteousness
---------------------

As news of this scandal seeped out over the weekend, I was pretty put off by tweets from China hawks strongly insinuating that Huang’s (then) rumored affair explained his push to sell chips to China, as if Huang would normally have agreed with them but was betraying the country because of who he was sleeping with. I think this is completely wrong.

The fact of the matter is that it is entirely rational — and defensible — for Nvidia to want to sell chips to China (an argument I have made repeatedly ( https://stratechery.com/?access_token=eyJhbGciOiJFUzI1NiIsImtpZCI6IkxzanFRcEhoMUJUdmU0djJQUUVuejIiLCJ0eXAiOiJKV1QifQ.eyJhdWQiOiJzdHJhdGVjaGVyeS5wYXNzcG9ydC5vbmxpbmUiLCJhenAiOiJIS0xjUzREd1Nod1AyWURLYmZQV00xIiwiZW50Ijp7InVyaSI6WyJodHRwczovL3N0cmF0ZWNoZXJ5LmNvbS8_cz1udmlkaWErY2hpbmEiXX0sImV4cCI6MTc5NjgxNDEyOSwiaWF0IjoxNzk0MjIyMTI5LCJpc3MiOiJodHRwczovL2FwcC5wYXNzcG9ydC5vbmxpbmUvb2F1dGgiLCJzY29wZSI6ImZlZWQ6cmVhZCBhcnRpY2xlOnJlYWQgYXNzZXQ6cmVhZCBjYXRlZ29yeTpyZWFkIGVudGl0bGVtZW50cyIsInN1YiI6IjAxOTUxNmYxLWEzNDYtNzYxNC05ZTk2LWI2M2JkMTFmZTg1NiIsInVzZSI6ImFjY2VzcyJ9.uGWHrNFJtGlZP5ix_DKvY-GlXXksuL4TFDSfv-QH66MRpDIM-FYfQv77RQaOgSjGezvX4wuHQyTEtvj4qIrVJA&s=nvidia+china ) ). From an Nvidia-specific perspective, selling chips to China is critical for maintaining the CUDA software moat; if China can’t use Nvidia chips, then it will be motivated by necessity to build an alternative, and that alternative is not going to stay in China: it will, in the long run, be an alternative to CUDA around the world. This is no small thing: it’s Huang’s life’s work, and one of Nvidia’s most important differentiators.

From a U.S. perspective, meanwhile, a similar motivation exists: as long as Chinese companies have access to Nvidia chips they have much less motivation to develop the software infrastructure and feedback mechanisms necessary for Chinese alternatives to come to market. Yes, the central government and Huawei are pushing to do just that, but chips are such a hard problem that you need “pull” as well as “push”, and Chinese AI companies are focused on building competitive models, not on national science projects (this is why China is itself not allowing the sale of Nvidia chips into China).

I think both of these points are valid, and I also have more overarching concerns about banning chips for China given our reliance on Taiwan ( https://stratechery.com/2025/ai-promise-and-chip-precariousness/?access_token=eyJhbGciOiJFUzI1NiIsImtpZCI6IkxzanFRcEhoMUJUdmU0djJQUUVuejIiLCJ0eXAiOiJKV1QifQ.eyJhdWQiOiJzdHJhdGVjaGVyeS5wYXNzcG9ydC5vbmxpbmUiLCJhenAiOiJIS0xjUzREd1Nod1AyWURLYmZQV00xIiwiZW50Ijp7InVyaSI6WyJodHRwczovL3N0cmF0ZWNoZXJ5LmNvbS8yMDI1L2FpLXByb21pc2UtYW5kLWNoaXAtcHJlY2FyaW91c25lc3MvIl19LCJleHAiOjE3OTY4MTQxMjksImlhdCI6MTc5NDIyMjEyOSwiaXNzIjoiaHR0cHM6Ly9hcHAucGFzc3BvcnQub25saW5lL29hdXRoIiwic2NvcGUiOiJmZWVkOnJlYWQgYXJ0aWNsZTpyZWFkIGFzc2V0OnJlYWQgY2F0ZWdvcnk6cmVhZCBlbnRpdGxlbWVudHMiLCJzdWIiOiIwMTk1MTZmMS1hMzQ2LTc2MTQtOWU5Ni1iNjNiZDExZmU4NTYiLCJ1c2UiOiJhY2Nlc3MifQ.mqbLmnYCCW2zOilrUzYRlJipswinFvq5BUXcvYLvfghzZZfBelyc-y3O7S_IPbFPte2AGcgY0WgDoi9insPkNQ ). This is a close question, to be clear! I completely understand and could, if pressed, articulate the arguments in favor of a chip ban; the trade-offs are brutal. What I continually find frustrating, however, is the extent to which people in favor of the bans — particularly the China hawk types I just referenced — seem incapable of comprehending arguments opposed to their position, which is how they fall into suggesting that Huang’s affair explains his position.

There is, however, a different trap that this sort of obtuseness presents, and that is the temptation towards righteousness in one’s position. Everyone opposed to you is dumb, and so by extension their position is dumb, and so by extension it’s fine to disregard it, and do what you, the only person who has fully thought this through, knows is right.

I don’t know Huang well — all of my interactions with him have been recorded as Stratechery Interviews ( https://stratechery.com/?access_token=eyJhbGciOiJFUzI1NiIsImtpZCI6IkxzanFRcEhoMUJUdmU0djJQUUVuejIiLCJ0eXAiOiJKV1QifQ.eyJhdWQiOiJzdHJhdGVjaGVyeS5wYXNzcG9ydC5vbmxpbmUiLCJhenAiOiJIS0xjUzREd1Nod1AyWURLYmZQV00xIiwiZW50Ijp7InVyaSI6WyJodHRwczovL3N0cmF0ZWNoZXJ5LmNvbS8_cz1qZW5zZW4raHVhbmcraW50ZXJ2aWV3Il19LCJleHAiOjE3OTY4MTQxMjksImlhdCI6MTc5NDIyMjEyOSwiaXNzIjoiaHR0cHM6Ly9hcHAucGFzc3BvcnQub25saW5lL29hdXRoIiwic2NvcGUiOiJmZWVkOnJlYWQgYXJ0aWNsZTpyZWFkIGFzc2V0OnJlYWQgY2F0ZWdvcnk6cmVhZCBlbnRpdGxlbWVudHMiLCJzdWIiOiIwMTk1MTZmMS1hMzQ2LTc2MTQtOWU5Ni1iNjNiZDExZmU4NTYiLCJ1c2UiOiJhY2Nlc3MifQ.KzjE-SwhYDl0iyr6KDDrzI06EEnQRJRFGEho3aP0ox2Jk7oWTUFncVMgrOIUZE410voCgUGv5b4pTXz_7nXuVA&s=jensen+huang+interview ) — but he is a man who has achieved what he has — a truly monumental amount — by betting on himself and doing what he thinks is right, even when investors or even his employees disagree. And, thanks to that self-belief, Huang and Nvidia have achieved unimaginable success in a very short amount of time as far as the broader public is concerned, but after a multi-decade slog as far as Huang is concerned.

I’m certainly not in a position to judge Huang’s personal choices; what I am driving at, however, is the possibility that, to the extent he has known about the hijinks being pulled by Megaspeed and the other entities smuggling chips to China — which Huang and Nvidia have expressly denied ( https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-29/nvidia-ceo-says-ties-to-company-under-spying-probe-were-normal ) — Huang may have been just a bit willing to turn a blind eye because, at least in his mind, it was the right thing. What do those people in Washington D.C. know anyways?

The problem, of course, is that it was the law, and to the extent the law is mistaken, it’s hardly the first regulation that leads to an outcome those it covers know is wrong. The duty, alas, is still to follow the law. It is, if I may draw the comparison, not dissimilar to a man drawn to a worldly executive who understands him and sees him as a titan: no matter how good an affair may feel, it doesn’t change the fact it’s a violation of one’s marriage covenant. There’s an aspect of self-belief that morphs into self-righteousness that justifies whatever you think is right, or deserved.

Again, I don’t know Huang, and I don’t know that he knew about Megaspeed; what we do know is that he had an affair with Alice Huang, and that that was an incredibly stupid thing to do. It’s the kind of stupid mistake that flows from hubris, and hubris in one area of your life is likely present in others — particularly when you know you’re right, and your detractors can’t even be bothered to understand your position.

Here, though, Huang’s detractors need to do some reflecting of their own. The fact of the matter is that AI is the most important industry to the U.S., Nvidia is one of the most important and highly differentiated companies supporting that industry, and that Huang is responsible for and essential to Nvidia’s success, which is to say he is responsible for and essential to U.S. success broadly. I do not subscribe to the idea that anyone could do his job; there are great men, and Huang is one of them. That he is flawed does not change this reality; indeed, great men always are.

My hope is that he is humbled — how could he not be? — but redeemable, which is where the detractors come in. Yes, conduct the investigations; yes, demand better controls. At the end of this, however, absent evidence of any sort of clear betrayal of U.S. interests, it is in the interests of the U.S. that Huang continue to be CEO of Nvidia. I very much hope that is where this ends up.

That noted, things for Nvidia will be different now. I noted in the first section that the entities who are likely to benefit the most from this news are not China’s national security apparatus, but the U.S.’s, specifically the contingent who sees Huang and his access to Trump as an obstacle in fully cutting China off from chips — not just in terms of smuggling current chips, but also in terms of banning older chips and stopping cloud access to servers mostly located in Southeast Asia. Huang’s ability to resist this contingent is likely over now; Nvidia and its board — particularly if they keep Huang, as I hope they do — will be very cognizant of the importance of staying in line. Huang may think he’s right about China, but that’s not going to matter anymore, and he has only himself to blame.
